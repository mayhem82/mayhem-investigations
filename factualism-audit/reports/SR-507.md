## 3. Findings
M01 Enforcement follow-through: distinguish suspected breach, regulatory intervention, corrective action and verified cessation. ANAO A01 supplies direct oversight evidence; accepted recommendations are not completed remediation.
M02 Shared-risk ownership: cross-sector service markets can share staffing and geographic constraints. Coordination without a shared risk mechanism can leave problems at institutional boundaries. A02 supplies both adverse findings and constructive examples.
M03 Evidence state integrity: indexed ANAO pages returned earlier in-progress descriptions for both A01 and A02, whereas direct retrieval returned published reports. Record the retrieval discrepancy, not a claim of deliberate suppression. An older index cannot override a recovered original.
M04 Protective access: A03, A05, A07 and A08 show distinct mechanisms for targeted exemptions, access to healthcare and public participation. Their actual effects must be verified separately.
M05 Surveillance expansion: A04 supports distinguishing improved detection from independently measured worsening. No precise local disease claim.
M06 Law versus effect: A06 demonstrates the need to distinguish parliamentary passage, assent, commencement, operational enforcement and individual redress.

## 4. Conclusion
Strongest findings: independent ANAO assessment of enforcement follow-through and shared care-sector coordination. No evidence of coordinated unlawful conduct between unrelated institutions. The accepted items include institutional announcements and scheduled milestones, not exclusively adverse findings or new incidents. No prevalence estimate or predictive modelling.

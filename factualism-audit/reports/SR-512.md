## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — ASIC finds audit concerns can disappear inside workplace complaint handling

ACCEPTED — original ASIC Report 848, released 25 November 2026. Printed pages 3–4, 8–9 and 12 examined, including page images.

Of 558 complaints supplied in response to compulsory notices, 35 concerned possible statutory audit issues or misuse of audit-client information. The other 523 were outside that narrower issue category. Some complaints involved alleged unsupported audit work, suppression of concerns or retaliation. ASIC did not establish every allegation.

ASIC observed HR investigations that addressed workplace conduct without examining underlying audit quality. Specialist functions reviewed approximately 26% of statutory audit complaints raising audit quality concerns. The report identifies inconsistent registers, missing original documents and incompatible recording practices between firms.

Counter-evidence: Some firms repeated audit work, disciplined staff or otherwise remediated problems; one investigation was reopened following ASIC engagement. ASIC regulates registered auditors' statutory work, not audit firms generally.

UNKNOWN: Individual allegation outcomes, complete complaint capture, subsequent enforcement and remediation. Neither 35 nor 26% is a sector-wide misconduct rate.

### A02 — ASIC changes investment-platform information delivery

ACCEPTED — original regulatory announcement and tracker, 26 November 2026. Updated RG 148 reflects Instrument 2026/395 and consolidates platform guidance. ASIC identifies a move from quarterly reporting to substantially continuous electronic access to account information.

Positive evidence: ASIC says protections are clarified and consultation occurred. UNKNOWN: Actual continuous availability, accessibility for digitally excluded investors, alternative delivery arrangements and preservation of historical statements. The operative instrument was not independently examined.

Inference: Continuous availability and a durable, accessible record are distinct protective properties; neither should be presumed from the other.

### A04 — APRA publishes insurance access and cost measures

ACCEPTED — original statistical summary dated 27 November 2026; new numerical detail, not re-admission of SR-510's general publication announcement.

## 3. Findings

M01 — Classification displacement: ASIC documents complaints routed into workplace processes while underlying audit quality was not fully examined. This directly advances the semantic preservation and responsibility-allocation tracks. It is stronger than a hypothetical concern because the regulator examined actual complaint records. Preserve each distinct issue and its referral, investigation, correction and closure separately.

M02 — Complaint capture and denominator integrity: inconsistent records and missing original material undermine comparison and discovery. The number recorded cannot establish the underlying incidence. Cross-reference SR-510 and SR-511; apply the same standard to MAYHEM's incomplete historical register.

M03 — Access versus durable evidence: investment-platform electronic access, AI delegation and facial-recognition governance involve different legal regimes. Compare the design requirements for verifiable access, bounded authority and preserved evidence without equating the laws.

M04 — Human outcome measurement: insurance coverage and utilisation can increase while reported patient costs rise. Infrastructure and grants must be traced through completion and actual access; judicial appointments must not substitute for performance evidence.

M05 — Individual assessment: ASIC's retiree guidance provides constructive evidence against substituting an easily measured proxy (traditional salary) for the actual question of repayment capacity.

M06 — Retrieval integrity: the indexed family court news page included 27 November material while the directly opened version began at 17 November. Individual appointment article confirmed the newer item. ACCC and NSW sport article direct retrieval failed; their limited indexed admissions are explicitly marked. No deliberate suppression inferred.

## 4. Conclusion

The strongest finding is ASIC REP 848's documented displacement of audit-quality concerns into workplace complaint processes, together with inconsistent records. The report provides a concrete basis to examine preservation of the original issue, routing, specialist scrutiny and correction without treating every complaint as proven. Positive and corrective evidence remains visible. No coordinated misconduct, improper common motive or new Bellbrook-specific failure established.

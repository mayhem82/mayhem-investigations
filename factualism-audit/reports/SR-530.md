### A04 — NSW overseas systems access circular
ACCEPTED — original mandatory administrative circular DCS-2026-05, issued 23 November 2026. Overseas staff access to government systems requires exceptional approval following security assessment and a compelling organisational need without a reasonable domestic alternative. Approval sits at senior executive level; residual cyber risk is formally accepted by that executive. The circular requires pre-travel preparation, monitoring during travel and risk-based checks afterwards. Third-party vendors are excluded from this circular and are subject to existing NSW cyber policy obligations. Positive evidence: defined authority, documented risk assessment and controls across the travel lifecycle. Unknown: implementation, exception records, vendor assurance and actual incidents. No link to the Commonwealth VIQ incident or evidence of a new NSW breach established. Inference: assigning and accepting risk does not verify that controls work in practice.

### A06 — Commonwealth FOI disclosure timing independently examined
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — original OAIC Disclosure Log Desktop Review 2026 and announcement, 7 August 2026. The review examined 30 high-volume agencies representing 75% of granted non-personal FOI requests in 2024–25; data collected January–March 2026. Twenty agencies had not published every relevant entry within the required period; four more could not be assessed for timeliness. The general statutory timeframe is 10 working days after giving access, subject to applicable exceptions. Positive counter-evidence: overall compliance was described favourably; many agencies employed useful search and access features. Recommendations address discoverability, current entries and governance. The selected agencies are not a random sample; agency-level counts are not a proportion of all entries. Unknown: current compliance, reasons for specific delays, and individual public consequences. Inference: release, timely publication and practical accessibility are separate. This strengthens SR-524's FOI investigation with independent review evidence; no specific ASIC or NDIS Commission breach determined here.

### A07 — Successful litigant's recoverable costs reduced for late abandonment of defences
ACCEPTED — original Federal Court judgment and order, Rosenlund Constructions Pty Ltd v Certain Underwriters at Lloyd’s of London (Costs) [2026] FCA 1831, 27 November 2026, order 1 and paragraphs 1–7. The insurers succeeded on the substantive proceeding but abandoned multiple defences two weeks before the hearing. The Court recognised unnecessary preparation costs incurred by the opposing party and ordered the applicant to pay 70% of the respondents' costs incurred after 28 April 2026 on the ordinary party-party basis. The Court rejected the contention that the applicant's case was hopeless. Positive counter-evidence: narrowing the case was considered commendable; the adjustment recognised costs incurred rather than punishing the narrowing itself. Unknown: assessed dollar amount, payment and subsequent proceedings. No finding of deliberate abuse inferred. Mechanism: substantive success does not erase avoidable procedural burdens; accountability can distinguish the outcome from the process.

## 3. Findings
M01 — Public decision versus evidence supporting it. The alternate Kempsey draft-minutes path resolves the Rise resolution retrieval gap, but the assessment remains unavailable. Willawarrin's refusal documents a protective evidentiary threshold without establishing physical harm. The High Performance Centre resolution preserves conditional approval boundaries. These are different decisions, not evidence of a common improper motive.
M02 — Responsibility across layers. The NSW circular assigns residual risk to a named level of authority and excludes vendors from its scope, while the grants memorandum specifies publication and audit requirements. Operational evidence is still required. This extends SR-525 and SR-526 without asserting noncompliance.
M03 — Time-dependent transparency. The OAIC review establishes that generally favourable compliance and significant timeliness shortcomings can coexist. It strengthens SR-524 without proving the specific FOI entries previously studied were unlawful.
M04 — Procedural burden survives substantive outcomes. The Court's costs decision distinguishes winning a case from responsibility for unnecessary preparation imposed on an opposing party. This is case-specific judicial evidence, not proof that all institutions impose similar burdens.
C01 — Append-only continuity update to SR-529 H04 and SR-527 A04: alternate draft-minutes path retrieved with text and visual evidence from printed pages 9–10; Rise resolution now recovered. Full independent assessment, CEO delegation attachment and confirmed minutes remain outstanding. Earlier reports unchanged. No substantive earlier factual error identified.

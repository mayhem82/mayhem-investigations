ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY, incremental detail only, NSW Auditor-General performance audit dated 23 November 2026. The original report text, printed pp. 2–4, was recovered despite the HTML report timing out. Screenshot attempts returned HTTP 405; no visual verification of those pages is claimed. The report identifies incomplete executive visibility when hazard information is fragmented; some selected agencies lacked escalation thresholds or consistent records of escalation decisions. Consultation outcomes were not always documented or communicated back to workers. Risk assessments did not account adequately for interacting hazards. The Auditor-General recommends sector-wide self-assessment and preventative improvements by 30 June 2027. All five examined agencies had improvement initiatives underway.

Evidence class: independent performance audit, not a court finding. Counter-evidence: existing improvement initiatives and differing maturity among the five agencies. The five-agency sample is not a finding about every NSW agency or Kempsey Council.

### A03 — Court permits bounded referral of compelled evidence despite overlapping proceedings
ACCEPTED, original Full Federal Court judgment, 20 November 2026, Marlton Group Pty Ltd v Villawood Management Pty Ltd [2026] FCAFC 147. Orders 1–2; reasons [2]–[6], [49]–[55], [74]–[79], [133]–[134]. Leave to appeal granted; appeal dismissed. The Court upheld a confined release from the obligation restricting collateral use of compulsorily disclosed material, allowing referral to the AFP. It distinguished the public purposes of possible statutory offence investigation from pending contempt proceedings, despite overlapping facts. The mere possibility of a later court referral did not displace the present public-interest assessment.
Evidence class: judicial determination of a procedural issue. Counter-evidence: confidentiality of compelled documents serves the administration of civil justice; release requires judicial balancing and appropriate scope, not a general licence to publish. Unknown: actual referral, police action, charges, final liability and subsequent litigation. No underlying allegation is admitted as proven, and unnecessary identifying information is omitted. Inference: overlapping institutional proceedings do not automatically exhaust every distinct accountability purpose.
Source: https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/full/2026/2026fcafc0147

## 3. Findings — longitudinal analysis

M01 — Preventative oversight. SR-523 and SR-527 distinguished complaint collection from meaningful detection. The NSW audit adds specific evidence concerning fragmented reporting, escalation thresholds, consultation feedback and interacting risks. A record must travel to an authority capable of acting, without losing its local context. This is an inference across separate cases, not proof of common intent.

M02 — Infrastructure accountability. SR-524 and SR-526 distinguish administrative milestones from human outcomes. The reported Hunter corridor changes are constructive evidence, but contract delivery, actual landholder impacts, compensation and consumer outcomes must remain independently testable.

M03 — Distinct accountability purposes. The Full Court's decision illustrates why a separate lawful inquiry may serve a public purpose even when facts overlap existing proceedings. Confidentiality is a genuine competing interest and the judicial release was confined. Do not generalise case-specific findings into a licence to disclose.

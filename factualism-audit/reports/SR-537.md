### A01 — Conditional interim permission for payment-system resilience cooperation

ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — original ACCC regulatory announcement, 16 November 2026; application AA1000749.

FACT/EVIDENCE: ACCC reports granting AusPayNet and specified participants conditional interim permission to continue the industry resilience initiative and committee, including plans for priority account credits and offline card transaction processing. The permission operates while the substantive application is assessed. Participants must report meetings and plans to the regulator.

Positive/counter-evidence: explicit regulatory conditions and a reporting obligation accompany the cooperation. The underlying register returned HTTP 403; exact conditions beyond the announcement, coverage, rollout, outage performance, consumer access and final authorisation remain UNKNOWN.

INFERENCE: payment resilience depends on coordination beyond the individual retailer or bank, and protective planning can be enabled while formal assessment continues. Interim permission is not proof that offline payments work everywhere, or that cash alternatives are unnecessary.

### A02 — Wholesale broadband regulation includes price and non-price access

ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — original ACCC inquiry and discussion-paper listing, 12 November 2026.

FACT/EVIDENCE: Following the July 2026 continuation of the service declaration for another four years, the ACCC commenced a public inquiry into terms for superfast broadband access service (SBAS). The inquiry covers price and non-price conditions; submissions close at 5 pm on 11 December 2026. The regulator intends a further draft consultation before its final decision. The listed discussion paper PDF was not examined.

Positive/counter-evidence: the process includes end-user interests and more than one consultation stage. UNKNOWN: eventual prices, service conditions, competition outcomes, rural applicability and household service reliability. This is wholesale fixed-line access regulation, not a finding about mobile reception or Bellbrook's network.

INFERENCE: affordability is one dimension of effective access; non-price access conditions and actual service delivery are distinct dimensions. An inquiry is not a completed regulatory determination.

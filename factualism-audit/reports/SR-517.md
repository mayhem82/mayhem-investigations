## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — Council payment-channel withdrawal and reported displaced demand
ACCEPTED — original NSW ministerial statement, 24 November 2026, and indexed original Central Coast Council meeting summary, 24 March 2026. Council's March summary describes a draft budget saving of $350,000 annually from removing Australia Post as a rates-payment option. The minister's November release attributes a 236% increase in counter payments (September–November year-on-year), 244 formal complaints, more than 3,000 petition signatures, and additional staffing at three service centres to the change. These are attributed representations, not independently audited raw records. Counter-evidence: the original proposal had a fiscal rationale; a Council payment page last updated 10 June 2026 still lists Australia Post and alternatives including direct debit, BPAY and post. Its earlier date does not establish present availability. Direct Council pages returned 403; indexed text was available. Unknown: final resolution, precise time boundaries, actual savings net of staffing costs, unique affected residents, travel costs, current alternatives and accessibility. No claim of unlawful conduct.
Inference: removing a payment channel may transfer costs to other institutional channels and people; savings must be evaluated across the affected system, not merely the originating budget.

### A03 — End-of-life pathway extension and access deadline
ACCEPTED — original Commonwealth Health department notice, 23 November 2026. From 8 March 2027, eligible people remaining at home after an initial 12-week period of approximately $25,000 support are to receive a further 12 weeks and approximately $25,000. The department also describes potential immediate assistive technology funding. Current take-up requires services to commence within 56 days of approval, or 84 days with an approved extension; otherwise approval is withdrawn and reassessment required. Counter-evidence: the proposed extension and extended take-up period offer some flexibility. Unknown: provider capacity, actual commencement, approvals withdrawn, individual outcomes and whether any further exceptions apply. No causal inference about deaths or specific cases.
Inference: entitlement duration, provider availability and timely delivery are independent conditions, particularly for time-sensitive care.

### A04 — ACCC explicitly connects AI error remediation to human escalation
ACCEPTED — original ACCC release, 27 November 2026, directly retrieved in this run; SR-512 A03 was limited to indexed text. ## 3. Findings
M01 — Displaced burden: A01 provides an attributed example of service-channel change followed by increased demand elsewhere. A forecast saving does not measure customer travel, staff costs or hardship. Extends SR-511 A02; different institutions, no shared motive established.
M02 — Counter-evidence integrity: A02 corrects an omission in our own audit. Observation date, publication date, reported corrective action and independent verification must remain separate. Preserve both records.
M03 — Temporal service accessibility: A03 shows eligibility and service take-up are different. Provider availability is critical; no causal harm established here.
M04 — Constructive implementation: A04–A07 identify human review, commissioning, recruitment and referral mechanisms; actual human outcomes still require evidence.
M05 — Case isolation and authority: A08 is an oversight submission, not a Senate finding; A09 is a distinct judicial outcome. Neither may be inflated into a broader finding.

## 4. Conclusion
Strongest new accessibility evidence is the Central Coast payment-channel case, subject to attributed-data and net-cost limitations. Most important audit correction is the omitted Services Australia response. The evidence does not establish coordinated misconduct, a current Digital ID breach or a new Bellbrook-specific service failure. No prediction or simulation performed.

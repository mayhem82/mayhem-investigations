## 2. Analysis — Accepted candidates
### A01 — Disability oversight: reduced reporting detail and rising workload
ACCEPTED — original NSW parliamentary committee report, incremental evidence. Tabled 24 November 2026; government response due 24 May 2027. 43-page PDF; printed pages v–vi visually examined, with text of opening chapter retrieved.
The committee found that the shorter annual information statement reduces administrative work but omits key abuse, neglect and exploitation reporting statistics. It identified growing reports, longer closure times, fewer closures through early intervention proportionally, and indicators suggesting increasing case complexity. The alternate-year systemic-issues reporting process for Official Community Visitors resulted in omission of those issues from the 2024–25 annual report. Additional baseline funding was welcomed but the committee judged it insufficient against demand. It recommended attaching a dashboard summary to annual statements and reviewing funding.
Counter-evidence: the committee considered the short-form statement compliant with annual reporting obligations; the Commission has limited resources, dashboard data exists, and a staff wellbeing plan is being implemented. The report identifies deteriorating staff wellbeing indicators as well as corrective work. Reported allegations are not proven incidents; the committee's interpretation of complexity is not a verified causal finding. Future demand modelling is the committee's, not MAYHEM's.
UNKNOWN: actual outstanding cases, causal harm, completeness of later reporting, funding response, subsequent closure times and human outcomes. No underlying case dataset examined.
Mechanism (inference): reducing the workload of oversight reporting may also reduce the visibility of the workload and risks the oversight institution must address.

### A02 — NSW electoral expenditure caps: threshold and reference-date effects
ACCEPTED — original parliamentary committee report, tabled 27 November 2026; response due 27 May 2027. 42-page PDF, printed page v visually verified. Findings 3–5 and recommendations 1–2.
The committee identified disproportionate changes in local election spending limits arising from the eight-tier enrolment-based structure. It also found that enrolment measured a year before polling may not reflect the population at election time, and that CPI adjustment does not reflect actual campaign cost changes. It recommended reviewing the tiers and moving the enrolment reference date closer to polling, including provision for councils emerging from administration.
Counter-evidence: expenditure limits can reduce spending imbalances; the committee recognises the need to protect political communication and continuing disagreement over third-party limits. The findings are not enacted amendments. UNKNOWN: government response, alternative tier design, actual effect on individual campaigns, Kempsey-specific applicable cap and any future amendment.
Mechanism (inference): a uniform rule can yield discontinuous treatment at thresholds; a historical reference date can detach a regulatory calculation from current circumstances.

## 3. Findings
M01 — Oversight visibility under capacity pressure. A01 documents a trade-off between reporting burden and information availability, alongside growing demand. Compliance with a reporting requirement does not independently establish sufficient disclosure for oversight. The proposed dashboard appendix could preserve longitudinal data while reducing duplicated work; implementation is unknown.
M02 — Threshold effects and time integrity. A02 provides direct parliamentary recognition of discontinuities in nominally consistent rules. Classification criteria and their reference dates should be retained with decisions. A legitimate spending limit can still warrant examination of proportionality.
M03 — Accountability infrastructure. A03 and A04 concern different institutions; both distinguish formal governance provisions from practical information availability. No common cause or coordinated misconduct established.
M04 — Sequential evidence. A05 extends, not replaces, previous observations. Count progression is not final turnout.

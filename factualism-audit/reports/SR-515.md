## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — Federal Court identifies failure to consider favourable evidence
ACCEPTED — original judgment and orders, [2026] FCA 1808, 27 November 2026. Orders 2–4; paragraphs 28–47, 53–60. The Federal Court allowed an appeal, quashed the former Administrative Appeals Tribunal's 10 December 2020 decision and required the ART to determine the application according to law. The Court found that the applicant's clearly articulated case relied on favourable communications which had not been adequately considered; the decision also interpreted one communication in the opposite sense to its actual meaning. A general assurance that material had been considered did not overcome contrary probative evidence. Counter-evidence: the Court acknowledged that a decision-maker need not mention every piece of evidence, and conflicting material existed. The Court did not grant the visa or decide the underlying merits. UNKNOWN: redetermination, final costs and wider prevalence. Inference: document receipt, accurate interpretation and substantive consideration are separate evidentiary conditions. Personal details omitted.
Source: https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/single/2026/2026fca1808

### A02 — Court separates evidenced expenses from unspecified future liabilities
ACCEPTED — original judgment [2026] FCA 1820, 27 November 2026, orders 1–3 and paragraphs 65–68. The Court varied a freezing order to permit $486,049.64 in receiver remuneration and legal expenses including GST, but declined a requested future buffer of up to $300,000 because further work was not sufficiently specified. Counter-evidence: another application could generate additional costs; a sufficiently precise and quantified future request might be treated differently. UNKNOWN: payment and future applications. Inference: documented expenditure and potential future liabilities require separate justification; administrative convenience does not alone establish a particular allocation.

Positive evidence: response scope changed with new evidence, with additional tracing, surveillance and community engagement. ## 3. Findings

M01 — Substantive consideration and semantic preservation. A01 provides judicially established evidence that a record can contain a general assurance of consideration while relevant favourable evidence remains unconsidered and a communication is interpreted oppositely. This strengthens SR-511's semantic transformation and SR-512's complaint classification displacement investigations, without conflating the legal duties. The distinction is between receipt, representation, consideration and reasoned resolution. Omission alone does not establish error in every case.

M02 — Evidenced versus contingent cost. A02 provides a judicial example of separate treatment of incurred and unspecified future expenses. Compare SR-508's distinction between realised and projected effects. Different legal and accounting contexts; no universal rule inferred.

M03 — Adaptive correction. A03 shows a public response adjusted after additional surveillance. An earlier intervention may be successful within its limited scope without resolving the larger problem. Preserve all three publication stages.

M04 — Actual accessibility and continuity. A05, A06 and A07 demonstrate why physical coverage, shared dependencies and communication channels should be assessed separately. A08 provides positive coordination design evidence, subject to measured implementation.

M05 — Measurement and harm. A04 and A09 demonstrate the need to preserve measurement boundaries, distinguish reported counts from population prevalence, and keep observed conditions separate from forecasts.

## 4. Conclusion

The strongest current evidence is the Federal Court's finding in A01 that a materially favourable case was not adequately considered despite assurances that evidence had been considered, and its order for lawful redetermination. Positive corrective mechanisms are also documented in judicial cost allocation and the adaptive biosecurity response. No coordinated misconduct, common improper intent, or new Bellbrook-specific failure established.

Corrections: no amendment to previous reports warranted on verified evidence in this run. A03 retains the initial treatment announcement and subsequent escalation without retrospectively rewriting either. Prior reports unchanged.

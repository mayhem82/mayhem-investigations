## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence
### A01 — Medicare AI governance and evidentiary boundaries
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — original independent performance audit, Auditor-General Report 5 of 2026–27, published 21 September 2026, audit snapshot and summary. Departmental enterprise AI governance was assessed as largely effective, while governance for provider compliance was partly effective. A detection model operated July 2024–December 2025; eight potential cases with an estimated value of $5.2 million were identified using the model in part. The department had not systematically assessed provider AI risks and assurance could improve. Counter-evidence: three recommendations accepted; broader governance was largely effective. Unknown: final case outcomes, model validation, false positives, actual recoveries, subsequent remediation. Potential non-compliance is not proven fraud, estimated exposure is not recovered money, and the model was only one input. Inference: detection, investigation, adjudication and remediation must remain separate.
Source: https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/artificial-intelligence-and-medicare-benefits-integrity

### A02 — Debt balance reporting and effective debt management diverge
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — original independent performance audit, Report 28 of 2022–23, published 31 May 2023, snapshot and conclusion paragraphs 7–8. ANAO assessed Services Australia's social security and welfare debt management as partly effective, identifying inadequate coordination across detection, legal determination, recovery and waiver. At 30 June 2022 there were $4.6 billion in determined outstanding debts and $1.9 billion undetermined; $3.7 billion of the former was temporarily written off. Counter-evidence: accurate balance reporting and acceptance of all four recommendations; disaster and pandemic-related recovery pauses were relevant. Unknown: current outcomes, recommendation implementation and actual harm. Undetermined amounts are not established liabilities; temporary write-off is not necessarily permanent waiver. Inference: accurate aggregate accounting cannot independently establish lawful and effective individual debt handling. Historical comparison with SR-518–520, not a current finding.
Source: https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/debt-management-and-recovery-services-australia

### A03 — Northern Rivers temporary housing transition
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — original NSW ministerial statement, 9 November 2026. Eleven villages were established following the 2022 floods; five had closed and six remained. Ballina and Mullumbimby are to continue for another 12 months; Brunswick Heads and Coraki are proposed for transfer to councils; Lismore and Wardell are scheduled to close early 2027. Government reports individual housing assistance, including up to $10,000, and commits that closures will not leave residents homeless. Counter-evidence: differentiated local arrangements and reuse of publicly funded assets. Unknown: actual secure rehousing, affordability, completed transfers, occupancy and post-transition outcomes. Commitment is not independently verified delivery. Inference: closing an emergency accommodation program and resolving displaced residents' housing needs are separate events.
Source: https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/repurposing-temporary-housing-as-focus-shifts-to-permanent-homes

### A04 — Federal Court announces simultaneous online service interruption

### A06 — Federal Court distinguishes absence of corroboration from adverse credibility
ACCEPTED — original orders and reasons, DJF18 v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural Affairs [2026] FCA 1803, 27 November 2026, paragraphs 15–24. Court dismissed appeal and fixed costs at $5,000. Court distinguished documents that failed to support an asserted involvement from documents that positively undermined credibility; it also assessed the tribunal's treatment of medical evidence in the context of the reasons as a whole. The asserted jurisdictional errors were not established. Counter-evidence to SR-515's separate case of semantic reversal: this Court found the challenged reasoning was logically available. Unknown: subsequent proceedings, costs payment and underlying factual merits beyond the judgment. Private details omitted. Inference: lack of corroboration is not automatically contradictory evidence; judicial review is not a fresh determination of merits.
Source: https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/single/2026/2026fca1803

## 2. Analysis — accepted evidence

### A01 — APRA finalises revised governance requirements
ACCEPTED at original regulator announcement level. Published 26 November 2026.

APRA announced a revised CPS 510 governance standard commencing 1 January 2028. It consolidates five standards, strengthens requirements for conflicts and director/executive suitability, allows greater delegation and removes overlapping suitability reporting for approximately 6,000 individuals. APRA retained a 12-year non-executive director tenure limit, with non-significant financial institutions allowed until the end of 2029 to comply.

Counter-evidence: APRA reports extensive consultation and retains core requirements while adjusting prescription for smaller entities. The complete operative standard and precise limits on delegation are not examined here.

Inference: Reducing duplicate reporting or transferring board functions may improve efficiency; whether accountability remains intact depends on retained oversight and actual implementation. No present breach or loss of protection is established.

### A05 — NDIA compliance performance reporting altered measurement boundaries
ACCEPTED retrospectively at original Auditor-General report level. Report 48 of 2024–25, published 25 June 2025. Paragraphs 3.46–3.54.

ANAO identified that a February 2025 dashboard marked identity-related measures achieved because participants or nominees had access to validated myGov accounts and strengthened credentials. The measure had changed from actual use to capability without explanatory disclosure. NDIA advised that robust actual-use data were unavailable.

The dashboard also combined realised and projected financial effects without an explanatory note. A payment error rate of 4.19% was used in the dashboard rather than the reported 5.0% for 2023–24; ANAO concluded the measure should have been classified off track against the original projected trajectory.

Counter-evidence: NDIA agreed to all four audit recommendations, including separate reporting of actual estimates and projections and disclosure of assumptions. Current implementation is unknown. This is not a new November 2026 report.

Inference: a performance target can appear achieved after its evidentiary meaning changes; the original denominator, definition, baseline and time horizon must be preserved.

Source:
https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/ndia-management-of-claimant-compliance-with-ndis-claim-requirements

### A06 — ASIC continues financial reporting enforcement after payment
ACCEPTED at original regulator announcement level; 2026 release 26-268MR. Exact publication day not displayed in the retrieved page.

ASIC reports 22 large proprietary companies paid $4,356,000 in infringement notices for alleged late lodgement of audited 2025 financial reports. ASIC states investigations continue where reporting obligations remain outstanding. Among the 234 companies contacted in 2026, 193 were alleged to have failed to lodge the relevant reports; 147 companies subsequently lodged at least one outstanding report.

Payment is not admission of liability. The contacted group is a selected surveillance population, not a representative estimate of all Australian companies. The 147 figure does not establish all outstanding reporting obligations were resolved. Duplicate status across earlier unrecovered audits is unknown.

Inference: enforcement payment and completion of the underlying transparency obligation are separate outcomes.

## 3. Findings — longitudinal analysis

M01 — Measurement substitution. The 2025 ANAO finding establishes a specific example of an undisclosed change from observed use to available capability. A measure may no longer answer the question it was created to answer. Preserve the original measure, changed measure, baseline, denominator, calculation and explanatory history.

M02 — Temporal integrity. Mixing prospective benefits with realised effects can misrepresent performance. Similarly, parliamentary passage, assent, commencement and functioning implementation are distinct stages.

M03 — Responsibility preservation. APRA's governance reform introduces greater flexibility in delegation. SR-505 independently documented an earlier formal accountability allocation failure. The comparison supports examination of how responsibility is retained and verified; it does not establish the new standard will cause that failure.

M04 — Technology continuity. The Diversa allegations supply a candidate example of a migration affecting time-sensitive rights. The AEC acknowledgement of accessibility barriers supplies a positive example of transparent identification before full implementation. The cases remain separate.

M05 — Enforcement completion. ASIC's continuing investigation where reports remain outstanding provides positive comparative evidence against treating payment of a penalty as closure of all underlying obligations. This extends SR-507's enforcement follow-through investigation.

M06 — Local public participation. Kempsey's proposed classification is a traceable decision stage. The notice does not prove final approval or delivery of the sewage upgrade.

## 4. Conclusion

The strongest verified retrospective finding is the ANAO's documented change in performance measurement meaning and inadequate separation of realised and projected effects. The strongest current developments are APRA's governance standard announcement and AEC's electoral implementation updates.

Seven candidates admitted at their specified evidentiary levels. Ten rejected. No coordinated misconduct established; no inference of intent from measurement discrepancies. The original reports and announcements do not establish current implementation outcomes beyond their own temporal boundaries.

## 2. Analysis — Accepted

A01 — ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY, limited to indexed original NSW Audit Office HTML report and official snapshot. Health capital expenditure and infrastructure delivery, 12 November 2026; examined 2021–2026. The audit covers four sample projects, including Lower Mid North Coast Health Service (Manning Hospital, not Kempsey). The Auditor-General found that NSW Health cannot demonstrate prioritisation of investment toward sustainable future service demand, and all four sampled projects received budgets before their business cases. Recurrent funding gaps are generally addressed close to opening; $90.5 million was received in 2025–26 to address operating affordability gaps at newly commissioned facilities. The December 2025 Lower Mid North Coast business case planned a roughly 1,600 m² fourth floor capable of accommodating 28 inpatient beds as an unfinished space requiring further investment. A 2026 additional capital proposal included the fitout. This does not establish 28 currently operating beds or 28 existing beds removed. Positive counter-evidence: the unfinished floor also preserves future expansion options; building it during redevelopment can avoid higher later construction costs; NSW Health has undertaken planning reforms; the audit makes six recommendations. Unknown: approval and delivery of fitout, staffing, opening dates, actual patient outcomes, whether other projects have equivalent gaps. Tender probity was outside scope. Direct HTML report timed out, direct snapshot returned HTTP 405; detailed indexed HTML sections including Table 4 and snapshot were retrieved. No underlying PDF analysed or visually verified. Historical duplication possible despite no matching keyword hits in recovered full reports.
Sources: https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/health-capital-expenditure-and-infrastructure-delivery-0 ; https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/media-release/report-snapshot-health-capital-expenditure-and-infrastructure-delivery
Inference: a constructed space, funded fitout, recurrent operating funds, staff and actual clinical service are separate evidence stages. Budget compliance may coexist with reduced initial usable capacity. No claim of actual harm or deliberate concealment.

A02 — ACCEPTED, retrospective inquiry and current implementation follow-through. Victorian Parliament Electoral Matters Committee report announced 8 September 2026; chapter 4, section 4.4, finding 16 and recommendation 13. The committee identified that restricting dedicated low-sensory voting to the day before ordinary early voting can disadvantage voters who need that environment, including by requiring earlier decisions before all campaign policies are available. It recommended at least three sessions across the election period. The VEC's 9 September response announced expansion to all 88 districts on 17 November and stated it would respond to the recommendations following the election. Its current accessible voting and election pages report that a dedicated quieter voting opportunity was held in each district on 17 November (10am–4pm in indexed election information). Positive evidence: geographical expansion, alternate voting channels, accessible equipment and broader accessibility efforts. Unknown: attendance, actual sensory conditions, whether any additional dedicated sessions occurred, unmet need, post-election evaluation and final response. The identified statewide dedicated service was on one advertised day; no evidence of three sessions recovered. Do not infer no alternative voting options, election exclusion, or unlawful discrimination. Election day 28 November; no final result admitted. No full original prior report recovered; related electoral access evidence in SR-518 and SR-534, but this specific inquiry/implementation comparison is incremental.
Sources: https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/get-involved/inquiries/inquiry-into-voting-centre-accessibility/reports/report/selecting-venues-for-voting-centres/ ; https://prod12.parliament.vic.gov.au/news/general-news/vcareport ; https://vec.vic.gov.au/about-us/media/media-releases/vec-welcomes-accessible-voting-focus ; https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/voting/2026-state-election/voting-options/accessible-voting-options?linkId=323718 ; https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/voting/2026-state-election?linkId=322252
Inference: geographical coverage does not establish temporal accessibility or equivalent decision-making opportunity.

## 3. Findings

M01 — Operational capacity is not equivalent to delivered physical infrastructure. A project can remain within its allocated budget while some space cannot deliver care until further investment and staffing. This extends the distinction between delivery milestones and human outcomes in SR-531 and service access measurement in SR-528. The Manning example is not evidence about Kempsey Hospital. Preserve both the potential future-expansion benefit and the initial operational limitation.

M02 — Accessibility has temporal as well as physical dimensions. The VEC reports statewide geographical expansion; the parliamentary committee identifies a potential restriction on the timing of informed participation. Both are preserved. This extends SR-534 external infrastructure dependency and SR-533 decision-time integrity. No claim that the mechanism was intentionally designed to disadvantage anyone.

C01 — Append-only clarification of SR-535 H05: archive retrieval confirms that [2026] FCA 1840 had already been admitted in SR-518. The current classification is duplicate, not a new judicial finding. Prior reports unchanged. D15 date discrepancy remains unresolved, not a correction of original publication chronology.

## 4. Conclusion

Two accepted evidence candidates, both limited to the demonstrated source scope. Financial and construction milestones do not establish usable clinical capacity. The number of accessible venues does not establish an equivalent opportunity to use them throughout a voting period. No deliberate misconduct, quantified exclusion, new Bellbrook-specific governance failure or causal injury established.

### A01 — NSW State agencies report: financial assurance and operational control failures coexist

ACCEPTED — incremental original documentary recovery, not a new incident count. Published/tabled 26 November 2026; financial year ended 30 June 2026. Source: NSW Auditor-General, State agencies 2026, original tabling PDF, executive summary and financial audit results (printed pp 2–6), IT projects section (including p 30), and official snapshot PDF. Previous partial coverage SR-493 reported in SR-526; exact original SR-493 unavailable. SR-529, 531, 533, 536 and 537 recorded retrieval limitations.

FACT (auditor findings): 205 clean audit opinions; one disclaimer among completed general-purpose financial statement audits. Uncorrected misstatements above $5 million rose from 16 ($355 million gross) in 2024–25 to 28 ($1,122 million gross) in 2025–26. Of 14 IT projects assessed, 11 exceeded planned timeframes; six exceeded or were expected to exceed budgets by $179 million combined. Approximately $246 million was spent on subsequently cancelled or abandoned IT work over three years, comprising $99.8 million in write-offs and $146 million on an abandoned cloud application. Three of four agencies examined for cancelled projects had not conducted post-implementation or post-cancellation reviews. Public reporting of key project information was deficient for all 14 selected projects.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: clean opinions provide assurance over financial statements within their scope. Some cancelled project outputs may be repurposed; cancellation can be a rational response to changed feasibility. The auditor also recorded reduced contingent workforce spending in three portfolios. Gross misstatements are accounting errors, not a measure of missing cash; the $179 million includes expected overruns and must not be added to the $246 million as a combined loss.

UNKNOWN: net economic loss, recoverable value, individual human consequences, completed corrective actions, results outside selected agencies. No inference of fraud. PDF text recovered; screenshot requests for both PDFs returned HTTP 405, so visual verification is not claimed.

INFERENCE / mechanism: a clean financial statement opinion does not independently establish effective project delivery, public transparency or institutional learning after cancellation. Missing post-cancellation review limits evidence that lessons were captured, not proof of repeated failure.

### A02 — Federal Court distinguishes unclear administrative communication from material legal error

ACCEPTED — original judicial reasons recovered. [2026] FCA 1837, Rao v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship. Judgment/orders 16 November 2026; reasons published 27 November 2026. Orders 1–2, paragraphs 13–20, 24–27, 32–40. No personal information beyond the public case citation is reproduced.

FACT: The Court dismissed an appeal involving an earlier migration review. The Court identified confusing wording in a notice and imprecise descriptions of the effect of the legislation. An invited hearing did not proceed; the available record did not establish whether cancellation was formally communicated. The Court nevertheless found sufficient notice of the adverse information and its relevance, and found that the applicable statutory provisions permitted a decision without a hearing in the particular circumstances. The underlying employer nomination had been refused, and the employer subsequently deregistered. The Court held that remittal would be futile as an independent basis for dismissal.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: The appellants did not contend that the communications had confused or misled them or that the lack of hearing denied procedural fairness. The Minister raised potential notice issues in accordance with model litigant responsibilities. Judicial examination expressly addressed the administrative wording deficiencies.

UNKNOWN: actual receipt/understanding beyond judicial findings, formal cancellation notice, subsequent proceedings, individual effects. No general licence to issue unclear notices or remove hearings inferred; historical statutory provisions and specific facts govern this result.

INFERENCE / mechanism: legal sufficiency, communication quality, practical accessibility and materiality of a defect are distinct. A defect can be identified without establishing an entitlement to judicial relief; refusal of relief does not establish exemplary administrative communication.

## 3. Findings — longitudinal structural analysis

M01 — Assurance boundary: SR-535 distinguished audited fiscal data from interpretation; SR-536 distinguished construction from clinical capacity. A01 adds evidence that reliable financial statement presentation and inadequate delivery controls can coexist. Do not use the audit opinion to erase operational findings or operational shortcomings to negate the audit opinion.

M02 — Institutional learning: the missing post-cancellation reviews are a documented governance gap. Recording cancellation, reconciling expenditure, determining causes, preserving lessons and verifying future improvement are distinct stages. No causal attribution to named officials.

M03 — Administrative language: A02 shows that judicially sufficient notification can coexist with poor wording. Legal validity is not a comprehensive measure of human usability. Conversely, poor wording alone is not proof of actionable legal error. No new Bellbrook-specific failure established.

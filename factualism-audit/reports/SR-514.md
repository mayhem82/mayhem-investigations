## 2. Analysis — Accepted candidates
### A01 — Digital ID Exchange privacy assessment
ACCEPTED — original OAIC assessment, published 26 November 2026. Fieldwork interviews 19 August 2025. Paragraphs 1.4–1.8, 3.13–3.19, 5.11–5.13.
The OAIC identified one high privacy risk: Exchange-specific staff training was not mandatory for relevant Services Australia personnel. It recommended training before relevant duties, annual refreshers and tracking completion, with implementation within two months of publication. Two low risks concern inconsistent descriptions of destruction timeframes and comprehensive retention policy. Counter-evidence: no non-compliance identified for s44 or specific privacy risk for s45 in the assessed evidence; consent management controls were described. Assessment is point-in-time and partly redacted, not an endorsement or a current system-wide compliance certificate. No actual data breach established by these findings. Unknown: current training completion and remediation, actual effects on individuals.
Inference: technical protection and staff competence are distinct control layers; publication date must not substitute for fieldwork date.
Source: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-assessments-and-decisions/privacy-assessments/digital-id-assessment-6-handling-of-personal-information-by-the-digital-id-exchange

### A02 — Tribunal AI verification obligations recovered
ACCEPTED — original ART practice direction, signed 20 August 2026; announced 24 August 2026. Clauses 1.3–1.4, 1.12, 2.2–2.6, 3.3–3.6; PDF text and selected page images examined.
The direction recognises potential accessibility benefits, but requires independent checking of AI-assisted material, continued responsibility by the person using it, and disclosure or certification in specified circumstances or when requested. A tool cannot be the sole verifier of its own output. Clause 3.6 requires checks of relevant legal authorities, evidence, chronology and traceable external sources. Counter-evidence: the direction distinguishes incidental research outputs not materially relied upon from material prepared for the Tribunal; it is not a blanket AI ban. It does not govern internal Tribunal administration. Unknown: case-specific orders, implementation and subsequent changes.
Inference: assistive drafting does not transfer evidentiary responsibility. This resolves the document-recovery component of SR-510 H05, not proof of practical compliance.

### A03 — Domestic gas reservation parliamentary passage
ACCEPTED — original joint ministerial statement, 27 November 2026.
Ministers report parliamentary passage of a scheme requiring LNG exporters to supply up to 20% of gas exports domestically. Export licensing is scheduled from 1 January 2027 and domestic supply obligations from 1 January 2028. Counter-evidence/boundary: statement describes parliamentary amendments, consultation and alignment with contracting cycles; actual affordability is an intended outcome, not a verified result. Unknown: authorised Act, assent, precise exemptions and calculation rules, volumes, prices and consumer outcomes.
Inference: passage, licensing, supply obligations and household affordability are separate stages.

### A06 — FOI complaint procedure consultation
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — original OAIC consultation, published 9 November 2026; closes 3 December 2026.
Proposed Part 11 changes clarify preliminary inquiries, investigation stages, recommendations and discretion not to investigate; also emphasise internal agency complaint mechanisms and readability. The OAIC says the scale of revisions prevents provision of a comprehensive individual amendment list. Counter-evidence: drafts and a public consultation pathway are available. Unknown: final guidance, amendment-by-amendment effect and complainant outcomes. The draft attachments were not analysed.
Inference: procedural clarity and change traceability are independently important; consultation is not final operative guidance.

## 3. Findings
M01 — Control-layer separation: A01 documents a staff-training weakness despite evidence of consent and attribute-handling safeguards. A control assessment is not an incident determination.
M02 — Independent verification: A02 supplies operative tribunal guidance supporting source traceability and human responsibility without treating AI assistance as intrinsically improper.
M03 — Continuity after institutional change: A05 identifies a service gap during ownership transition and a proposed response. A07 records a separate consequential remedy following reversal. Neither establishes a common cause or intent.
M04 — Temporal integrity: A01 fieldwork precedes publication by over a year; A03 and A04 establish future implementation stages, not achieved outcomes.
M05 — Procedural traceability: A06 identifies complaint-process changes without a comprehensive change list. A consultation is evidence of a proposed change, not proof of harm or improvement.

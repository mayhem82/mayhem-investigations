## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — Victorian child-safety clearance lifecycle
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — official operational guidance; overview updated 16 October 2026; WWC Connect introduced 19 October 2026. Observed 28 November.
Victoria requires organisations and individuals engaging new workers or volunteers in child-related work to verify eligibility before commencement, register the engagement, maintain relevant records and notify the regulator of changes. The platform provides status-change notifications for connected workers. Existing workers are strongly encouraged to be connected; the guidance also requires updating details or cessation for workers engaged before 19 October. New applicants and renewal applicants must undertake child-safety training. The platform does not directly notify organisations about status changes to interstate clearances; the guidance says interstate exclusion of a Victorian clearance holder triggers Victorian exclusion and notification. The regulator recommends a computer for optimal use while mobile responsiveness is being improved.
Positive evidence: ongoing status monitoring supplements initial screening; guidance identifies employer obligations and the limits of notification. Training content is reported to include lived-experience input and user testing with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities.
UNKNOWN: coverage of existing workers, notification delivery and response times, compliance, accessibility in practice, privacy safeguards and measured child safety outcomes. Statutory text and actual operational logs not independently examined. No finding of harm or breach by a particular organisation.

### A02 — Victorian ADHD care pathway expansion
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — official Department of Health service information, observed 28 November; announcement February 2026, initial GP training September–October 2026; page publication day not established. The department states that appropriately trained GPs can diagnose and treat ADHD for adults and children aged six or older. It lists participating practices including regional locations. The virtual emergency service provides short-term prescription access for eligible people with an existing diagnosis when their usual clinician is unavailable; this is not ongoing care. The department warns of possible appointment waits, multiple assessment visits and variable out-of-pocket costs.
Positive evidence: the service directory and reported completed training are more specific than an announcement of intended reform. The published caveats distinguish availability from effective access.
UNKNOWN: verified appointment availability, fees, wait times by region, numbers of patients receiving care, long-term outcomes and independent implementation evaluation. No claim that Victoria has universal free ADHD assessments or that these arrangements apply in NSW.
Source: https://www.health.vic.gov.au/primary-care/adhd-care-in-general-practice

## 3. Findings — longitudinal analysis

M01 — Point-in-time screening versus continuing protection. A01 extends the distinction between formal authorisation and operational safeguards (SR-530, SR-537). Verification, connection, change detection, notification receipt and protective action are separate stages. Existing-worker coverage and interstate status limitations are important boundaries. The notification design is positive evidence; actual compliance is unknown.

M02 — Access pathways versus realised access. A02 complements SR-536's operational health capacity and SR-537's multidimensional accessibility. Training and published service locations establish reported capacity more strongly than a future commitment; appointment availability, cost, continuity and clinical outcomes remain separate. The emergency pathway cannot substitute for regular care.

M03 — Regulatory correction versus broader assurance. A03 extends SR-529 and SR-535's enforcement/restoration distinction, this time with explicit regulator-reported correction and revocation. A disclosure intervention may address the regulator's identified concerns without establishing investor comprehension, restitution or overall investment safety.

No substantive correction to an earlier report established. No historical original recovered. No evidence freeze, no predictive modelling. The identified structural mechanisms are analytical distinctions, not proof of common causes or coordinated misconduct.

## 4. Conclusion

Three constructive institutional measures were admitted within their evidentiary limits: continuing clearance monitoring, expansion of a primary-care pathway and regulator-reported correction of disclosures. Each requires independent outcome verification. No new Bellbrook-specific failure or causal human harm established.

## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — Final structural steel safeguards report released

ACCEPTED — original Productivity Commission final-report HTML findings summary. Delivered to government 23 November 2026; publicly released 27 November 2026.

FACT: The Commission found conditions for definitive safeguards were not met. Fabricated structural steel imports increased during the investigation period, but the increase did not meet the applicable high threshold. Estimating domestic production entails uncertainty. The Commission reports that after refining its analysis of producers and undertaking additional quality checks, the aggregate results remained consistent with interim findings.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: Some firms are experiencing pressure, according to participant evidence and administrative data. Industry-wide indicators improved on most injury measures during the examined period. The finding does not establish that every firm is financially secure or that imports cause no harm.

INFERENCE: Evidence of individual hardship and evidence meeting an industry-wide legal threshold are distinct. An aggregate determination should preserve the underlying firm-level evidence, without misrepresenting the statutory test.

UNKNOWN: Full quantitative estimates and sensitivity analyses, individual firm outcomes, government's subsequent decision, and other available policy responses. Full PDF, referee report and spreadsheet not examined; admission limited to published HTML findings.

CONTINUITY: SR-507 H03 held final findings pending publication. This resolves the publication and headline findings gap, not the full methodological review. The original report remains unchanged. The search-index version of the inquiry still displayed report preparation; direct inquiry and report pages confirm release. PC homepage search representation displayed 27 November 2025 for the report, whereas the direct report and inquiry pages explicitly identify 2026. Cause unknown; no deliberate manipulation inferred.

### A03 — Fair Work regulator commences construction right-of-entry proceedings

ACCEPTED — original Fair Work Ombudsman announcement dated 23 November 2026. Alleged incident 22 November 2024; directions hearing listed 15 March 2027.

FACT: FWO reports commencing proceedings against the CFMEU and two officials in the Federal Circuit and Family Court, alleging failure to comply with safety requirements and improper conduct while exercising entry permits at a Gold Coast construction site. The regulator identifies sections 499 and 500 of the Fair Work Act. It also reports that the matters transferred from the former ABCC in December 2022 have been finalised, with approximately $3.85 million in penalties secured across those cases.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: Statutory workplace entry rights have legitimate purposes; the regulator's allegations in the new matter remain unadjudicated. Prior case outcomes cannot establish liability here. Individual officials' names omitted as unnecessary to structural analysis.

INFERENCE: Protective authority and the conditions governing its exercise are separate. Enforcement commencement is not a determination. The time between alleged conduct and proceedings warrants examination but does not independently establish unjustified delay.

UNKNOWN: Respondents' position, pleadings, final determination, actual penalties in this matter and whether delay caused any harm. No judicial reasons or operative statutory text examined.

## 3. Findings — Longitudinal analysis

M01 Threshold integrity: A01 provides direct counter-evidence against treating all reported adverse experiences as sufficient to satisfy a particular legal test. Conversely, the absence of an aggregate threshold finding does not erase individual hardship. This complements SR-519's systemic issue methodology and SR-521's evidentiary discrimination. The distinction is an inference about method, not evidence of wrongdoing by the Commission.

M02 Funding and outcomes: A02 adds a live inquiry into drought funding allocation. The eventual assessment should distinguish allocation processes from actual resilience outcomes. No adverse finding admitted.

M03 Authority and accountability: A03 illustrates the separation of statutory authority, obligations governing its exercise, allegations, adjudication and remediation. No conclusion about individual liability or common institutional motives.

M04 Documentary chronology: conflicting indexed and direct PC publication representations preserved in A01. Direct dated primary report is preferred. No deliberate suppression established.

## 4. Conclusion

The strongest new evidence is the release of the final steel safeguards findings, resolving the earlier publication gap. An industry-wide legal threshold must not erase evidence of individual pressure; individual pressure must not be substituted for evidence meeting that threshold. The drought inquiry and Fair Work proceedings are new procedural milestones, not completed protective outcomes.

No coordinated misconduct, new Bellbrook-specific failure or new correction to an earlier factual finding established. SR-507 H03 receives a separately identified continuity update in A01, not a rewrite.

## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — NSW flood recovery response: completion labels and funding dependencies
ACCEPTED — incremental primary government response, with parliamentary register and selected visual verification. Inquiry report tabled 28 May 2026, response tabled 27 November 2026, cover dated 28 November 2026. The 35-page response says its information reflects September 2026 knowledge. Both document dates are preserved.

The response summary (printed p4) says 22 of 23 recommendations are supported or supported in principle, but the individual entries for recommendations 11 and 18 (pp14,21) both classify the response as “Noted”. This is an internal classification discrepancy, not proof of intent.

Selected findings: recommendation 1 (p4) describes disaster impact data standards as still being implemented, requiring funding and time. Recommendation 9 (p12) supports permanent local or regional recovery officers in principle, but reports they are ineligible under current disaster funding arrangements and new funding goes through the budget process. Recommendation 11 (p14) describes Tell Your Story Once as a 2026–27 pilot with wider implementation conditional on funding and government consideration. Recommendation 18 (p21) leaves increased upfront farmer grants dependent on intergovernmental funding negotiations. Recommendations 21–22 (pp24–25) are labelled completed: the government reports quarterly claims and advances to councils, and says it addressed residual cash-flow problems for many councils while continuing to work with individual councils. The latter is not a council-by-council reconciliation of all outstanding money.

Positive counter-evidence: the response identifies responsible agencies, implementation stages, and concrete administrative payment reforms; a pilot exists according to the government. Unknown: actual payments outstanding, individual recovery outcomes, pilot coverage, funding, and explanation of the summary discrepancy. No deliberate misrepresentation established. Evidence class: government self-report, not independent implementation assurance. Mechanism (inference): administrative closure and conditional support must not substitute for demonstrated discharge of a recommendation.

### A02 — Kempsey Shire Council's own disaster-recovery evidence
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — primary institutional testimony, supplementary answers listed 30 March 2026; six-page PDF retrieved, pages 1–2 visually examined. Council sought earlier ongoing engagement, shared mapping/data, defined contact responsibilities and prompt funding eligibility advice. It described residents on Toose Road as isolated and the recovery project as subject to numerous approvals; it acknowledged the importance of those approvals but sought assistance accelerating urgent works. Council reported participation in multiple after-action reviews and said it did not appear to possess the final Authority-led review at that time. Council positively assessed the recovery coordination and broad waste-management response, while identifying timing concerns about the flood waste facility.

Counter-evidence: the NSW Government response now reports publication of the after-action review on 1 June 2026, and the Authority's research library independently confirms a combined review of March and May 2025 events is listed. This does not establish when Council received it. Unknown: current road access, final approvals, specific reimbursement balances, review implementation, actual receipt and distribution of findings. These are Council's reported experiences and proposals, not adjudicated findings or evidence of deliberate obstruction. Mechanism (inference): coordination and approval dependencies can shift delay and administrative burden onto local government and affected residents; independent verification of outcomes is required.

## 3. Findings
M01 — Completion status must be tied to the original requested outcome. Payment process reform and actual clearance of approved arrears are distinct. The NSW response provides constructive implementation evidence alongside unverified completion claims and a summary classification discrepancy. The distinction extends SR-538 and SR-542.
M02 — The burden of recounting lived experience is recognised in disaster recovery, but the project intended to reduce repetition remains a pilot with conditional wider funding. Council testimony separately identifies practical coordination and data-sharing needs. These establish a policy and implementation question, not proof that the same harm occurred for each resident.
M03 — Digital fallback and cash distribution are complementary; both rely on operational networks. Extends SR-537. Interim legal protection is not evidence of universal practical access.
M04 — Current electoral figures require a source timestamp and processing-stage label. Direct retrieval revealed newer figures than the index. No election prediction.

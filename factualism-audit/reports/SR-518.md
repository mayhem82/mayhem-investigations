## 2. Analysis — Accepted candidates

### A01 — Court pauses pharmaceutical inquiry pending procedural challenge
ACCEPTED: original Federal Court judgment, 27 November 2026, [2026] FCA 1840, order 1 and paragraphs 33–43. The Committee had obtained documents, and the applicants sought to question their producers. The Committee subsequently said it would disregard the documents. The Court granted an interim injunction, finding an arguable apprehended-bias case and that the balance of convenience supported temporary restraint. It expressly distinguished this from actual bias, which was not alleged, and from a final determination. The applicants undertook to preserve relevant records and expedite proceedings; the Commonwealth opposed the application. The question of statutory entitlement to cross-examine document producers was not decided. Unknown: final outcome, underlying compliance allegations, ultimate costs.
Inference: A statement excluding evidence does not necessarily resolve an objectively arguable concern about its influence. Interim review can preserve procedural protection without deciding the merits. Compare SR-515 and SR-510; distinct case and legal test.
Source: https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/single/2026/2026fca1840

### A02 — Historical NDIS debt correction: additional evidence from previously admitted report
ACCEPTED: retrospective, ANAO Report 48 of 2024–25, published 25 June 2025, paragraphs 2.64–2.65 and Box 1; new passage relative to SR-508's measurement analysis, not a new report. ANAO records NDIA's internal identification of problems in post-payment reviews, including debts inconsistent with legislation and deficient reasons. Active pursuit paused January 2024; historical review began March 2024. A $9.1 million provision in 2023–24 financial statements represented potential liability from revocations, not actual repayments. ANAO documents three examples of debts raised in 2019, 2022 and 2023 and revoked in 2025 for procedural fairness, insufficient evidence or inability to determine compliance with plan wording. Counter-evidence: NDIA developed a revised procedure and told ANAO it began using it in April 2025. Unknown: current implementation, total revocations, reimbursements and consequential remedies. No claim all debts were unlawful.
Inference: Failure to document a valid legal basis and failure to establish non-compliance are different problems. Reversal of debt does not itself quantify restoration.
Source: https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/ndia-management-of-claimant-compliance-with-ndis-claim-requirements

### A03 — Cross-agency childcare compliance oversight deficiency
ACCEPTED: retrospective original ANAO Report 42 of 2024–25, 16 June 2025, conclusion paragraphs 8–14. ANAO assessed management and oversight of Child Care Subsidy compliance as partly effective; Education lacked sufficient visibility over Services Australia's compliance work despite formal bilateral arrangements. Counter-evidence: payment accuracy improved during 2022–24, prevention arrangements were comprehensive, and Education accepted all seven recommendations while Services Australia accepted its three joint recommendations. Unknown: present remediation, outcomes for families, individual payment decisions and duplication in unrecovered audits. This is distinct from the NSW childcare fund audit in SR-516.
Inference: Delegating delivery does not remove the need for whole-program oversight; reporting arrangements can exist without providing adequate assurance.
Source: https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/management-and-oversight-of-compliance-activities-within-the-child-care-subsidy-program

### A04 — Disability services compliance notice enforcement
ACCEPTED: retrospective regulator announcement, 19 November 2026. FWO reports a court imposed a $9,801 penalty against a former director of a disability support company for involvement in failure to comply with a notice requiring $7,789 in back-pay to two workers. Notice issued October 2024; employment period November 2023–May 2024; company entered liquidation in 2026. Counter-evidence: workers sought assistance, the regulator investigated and pursued court enforcement; a sector inquiry is ongoing. Unknown: actual wages recovered, liquidation distributions, full court reasons, subsequent sector findings. Penalty does not equal restitution; company insolvency does not establish whether employees received payment.
Source: https://www.fairwork.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/2026-media-releases/november-2026/20261119-llorente-penalty-media-release

### A05 — First litigation over allegedly unlawful advertised pay
ACCEPTED: retrospective FWO statement, 17 November 2026, litigation commencement only. FWO alleges Lush Australasia Retail published two advertisements with unlawfully low hourly rates in July and August 2026; it seeks penalties for two alleged contraventions. Regulator cites a 2020 enforceable undertaking involving back-pay exceeding $2.44 million plus interest as relevant to its enforcement choice. Directions hearing listed 4 December 2026. Counter-evidence: past undertaking included compliance measures; current allegations are unadjudicated. Unknown: defence, final orders, whether anyone was paid the advertised rates. Inference: preventive obligations and continuing compliance after past remediation require separate verification.
Source: https://www.fairwork.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/2026-media-releases/november-2026/20261117-lush-litigation-media-release

### A06 — Electoral accessibility and tighter time windows
ACCEPTED: VEC official operational information observed 27 November 2026; page publication date not confirmed. Victorian election scheduled 28 November. Roll closed 3 November, early voting 18–27 November, with a shorter early voting period than prior elections. VEC provides provisional enrolment at voting centres, reports low-sensory sessions in every district on 17 November, and identifies phone voting for specified disability-related needs and mobile voting at selected facilities. Counter-evidence: shorter windows coexist with additional accessibility arrangements, not proof of overall reduced access. Unknown: uptake, unmet demand, actual venue accessibility, electoral participation and outcome. Inference: accessibility is channel- and population-specific; evaluate actual participation rather than counting channels alone.
Sources: https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/voting/2026-state-election ; https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/voting/2026-state-election/voting-options

### A07 — First Bundian Way segment opens
ACCEPTED: original NSW ministerial announcement, 27 November 2026. Government reports opening a 25.4 km section near Eden, supported by $7.18 million in funding to help Eden Local Aboriginal Land Council develop it. The historical route extends 365 km; the entire route has not been established as open. Positive evidence: Land Council participation and guided cultural experiences reported. Unknown: final expenditure, accessibility for different mobility needs, ongoing maintenance, economic and community outcomes. Inference: staged physical delivery, community involvement and realised benefits are distinct evidence layers.
Source: https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/first-part-of-bundian-way-opens-on-nsw-south-coast

### A08 — Free public recreation service reopens with protective arrangements
ACCEPTED: NSW ministerial announcement, 27 November 2026. Penrith Beach reopened for the 2026–27 season; government reports nearly half a million visits since 2023, more shade, extended seasonal hours, lifeguard supervision and water-quality monitoring. Positive evidence: free entry and reported operational safeguards. Unknown: actual water-quality results, incident rates, geographic access, unique visitors and use by disadvantaged groups. Inference: counting visits is not counting unique people; service availability, protective controls and actual human benefit should be evaluated separately.
Source: https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/pondi-returns-for-fourth-summer

### A09 — ACCC corrects scope of proposed competition-law remedy
ACCEPTED: retrospective original ACCC announcement and explicit clarification, 26 October 2026. Perfection Fresh admitted cartel conduct; parties agreed to propose $12 million in penalties for nine contraventions and one attempted contravention. Court determination remains necessary. The ACCC clarified that the proposed compliance obligation was to continue implementing a programme, not establish a new one. Positive evidence: transparent same-day wording correction and judicial oversight. Unknown: subsequent court orders, implementation, consumer losses and duplication in older unrecovered audits. Inference: continuing an existing protective system is materially different from creating one. The proposed penalty is not an imposed penalty.
Source: https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/perfection-fresh-admits-to-fixing-the-price-of-vegetables-sold-to-aldi

## 3. Findings
M01 — Evidence exclusion and procedural safeguards. A01 complements SR-515's evidence consideration finding and SR-510's caution about cumulative inference, but establishes an interim judicial assessment rather than a final failure finding.
M02 — Historical remediation. A02 provides concrete debt revocation examples; A04 supplies separate evidence of enforcement after a compliance notice, while actual recovery remains unknown. Compare SR-513 and SR-516.
M03 — Delegation and authority. A03 provides independent audit evidence that written interagency arrangements may not provide adequate visibility across a whole programme.
M04 — Protective access. A05 addresses intervention before potential underpayment; A06 shows differentiated participation channels; A07 and A08 show reported community delivery milestones. Their actual effectiveness requires separate evidence.
M05 — Corrective precision. A09 illustrates transparent correction of an obligation's meaning without erasing historical context. No new correction to an earlier MAYHEM report established; A02 is an additional passage, not a correction.

## 4. Conclusion
Strongest current evidence: interim Federal Court injunction preserving review of procedural fairness concerns. Strongest retrospective evidence: NDIA historical debt revocations and the ANAO childcare oversight finding. An institutional statement of exclusion or compliance is not independently sufficient to establish the functioning of every protection. No coordinated misconduct, actual bias in A01, current equivalent NDIA failure or new Bellbrook-specific service failure established. No predictive modelling.

### A01 — Automated family assistance debt recovery and gaps in review
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — original ART President Notice of Systemic Issue No 1 of 2025, dated 16 September 2025; pages 1–2 examined visually and parsed, paras 1, 6–15; ARC inquiry page retrieved 27 November 2026; terms of reference dated 18 March 2026. The President identifies repeated cases in which income tax refunds were offset against alleged family assistance overpayments without individual consideration, including existing repayment arrangements or pauses. Applicants reported inability to meet essentials. The notice identifies uncertainty about lawful exercise of discretion and contended gaps in merits review: the agencies argued the offset was not a reviewable decision by them. This is a statutory systemic-issue notification, not a final judgment that all offsets were unlawful. Counter-evidence: ATO published information about requesting a fresh decision and judicial review on 25 July 2025; the agencies began discussing changes; some offsets were reversed. ARC's inquiry examines recovery processes and adequacy and communication of review rights. Its page still proposes reporting in the first quarter of FY2026–27 (July–September 2026); no final report recovered. July 29 meeting outcomes note development of recommendations and a bill addressing aspects of the issue. UNKNOWN: current legislative status, final report, number of affected people, remediation, present practices; elapsed planned reporting period does not establish non-delivery.

### A03 — Compensation pathway and its limits
ACCEPTED — undated original ART CDDA guidance, observed 27 November 2026. The discretionary last-resort scheme can address quantifiable loss directly resulting from defective administration where payment is not legally required. ART distinguishes its administrative functions from its review decision-making powers, which are ordinarily outside the scheme. Personal time, distress/inconvenience and claim preparation expenses are generally excluded. ART aims to acknowledge claims in five business days and decide within 90 business days once necessary information is available; complex matters may take longer. Counter-evidence: alternate application arrangements, reconsideration on new relevant information and Ombudsman complaint pathway are identified. No automatic administrative review right for CDDA decisions. UNKNOWN: uptake, actual processing, outcomes, other legal remedies. A scheme's existence is not equivalent to complete restoration.
Source: https://www.art.gov.au/pa/node/390

## 3. Findings — analysis, not further independent facts

M01 — Hardship controls must survive cross-agency data exchange. A01 identifies a historical arrangement in which individual repayment settings were not considered by the downstream offset process. Compare SR-511 downstream protection and SR-518 delegated oversight and historical NDIS debt review. These are distinct legal regimes; no equivalence or common intent established.

M02 — Systemic escalation requires a defined recurring mechanism, human impact, and a pathway for correction. A02 provides a constructive institutional comparison to Factualism case isolation: patterns need evidence of repeated practice, not mere accumulation of adverse outcomes.

M03 — Remedy scope and residual burden: A03 identifies an existing but bounded discretionary pathway. Compare SR-513 and SR-516: correcting an original decision does not quantify or redress every consequence. No conclusion that other remedies are unavailable.

M04 — Semantic integrity is reciprocal. A04 acknowledges assistive AI and identifies the possibility that generated text or repeated follow-ups could distort or obscure a person's account. The originating human account, verified evidence and analysis must remain separate; no assumption about any individual complaint.

M05 — Actual housing access versus construction. A05 reports occupancy; A06 reports construction and intended access infrastructure. These are distinct delivery milestones. A07 highlights the separate need for oversight access; the reported positions are not judicial findings.

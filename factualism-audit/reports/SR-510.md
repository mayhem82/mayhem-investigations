ACCEPTED — original regulator publication.

### A03 — Federal Court rejects an unsupported financial comparison

ACCEPTED — original Federal Court judgment.

*First Class Securities Limited v Global Future Holdings Pty Ltd (No 5)* [2026] FCA 1798.

Judgment: 26 November 2026.

Relevant paragraphs: 10–16.

The Court examined whether refusing a proposed security-for-costs arrangement constituted unreasonable conduct.

The amount initially sought was $450,000.

A subsequent offer was $350,000.

The eventual security amount was $362,724.

The Court found that the argument concerning the significance of the reduction depended upon treating the original $450,000 amount as reliable, which had not been established.

The application for indemnity costs and immediate taxation of costs was dismissed.

Structural mechanism: **An unverified starting value can distort the apparent significance of subsequent changes.**

This applies more broadly to institutional reporting involving claimed savings, financial concessions, expenditure reductions and performance improvements.

Counter-evidence: The Court acknowledged that the applicant's position on the original security application was weak. Nevertheless, weakness did not independently establish unreasonable conduct warranting the requested costs orders.

[Original Federal Court judgment](https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/single/2026/2026fca1798)

### A04 — Federal Court distinguishes adverse decisions from evidence of bias

ACCEPTED — original Federal Court judgment.

*Storey, in the matter of Britton v Britton (No 6)* [2026] FCA 1817.

Judgment: 20 November 2026; subsequently listed among recently published judgments.

The Court considered allegations of disparate procedural treatment and apprehended judicial bias, examining the matters individually and cumulatively.

It applied an objective test requiring identification of the relevant circumstances, an articulated logical connection to possible departure from impartial decision-making, and assessment from the perspective of a reasonable, informed observer.

The recusal application was dismissed.

This is important counter-evidence for the Factualism methodology itself.

Accumulating numerous concerns does not automatically establish a structural mechanism. A defensible connection between the circumstances and the proposed conclusion must still be demonstrated.

UNKNOWN: Subsequent substantive proceedings.

## 3. Findings — longitudinal structural analysis

M01 — Reporting granularity. Aggregate figures and institution-level disclosure answer different questions. APRA's announced restoration is constructive evidence; actual delivery remains outstanding.

M02 — Baseline integrity. The security-for-costs judgment and APRA's explicit statistical comparability warning establish distinct examples of why original measurement definitions and reference values must remain visible. These cases are not causally linked.

M03 — Cumulative inference discipline. The recusal judgment provides counter-evidence against treating repeated adverse experiences as sufficient proof of a shared improper mechanism. A logical connection and appropriate evidence are necessary.

M04 — Human experience and reporting. The AHRC identifies gaps in capture and classification; its recommendations support community agency and reporting accessibility. Missing records do not independently establish an absence of harm.

M05 — Operational continuity. The ART notice identifies a backup service channel. Its effectiveness requires evidence of accessibility for those affected, not merely existence of a second channel.

M06 — Corrective verification and capacity. ASIC's announced remediation monitoring provides a positive design example. OAIC's corporate plan demonstrates why completed work, incoming demand and actual human resolution must be separately tracked.

M07 — Publication chronology. The AHRC media listing displays 27 November for an article dated 9 November; its race-data listing displays 28 October for an article dated 27 October. Council's indexed date is 6 November while the directly retrieved page shows 20 November. Preserve both representations without alleging motive or assuming why they differ. Do not manufacture a new event from a listing date.

## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — NSW electoral donation disclosure timeliness
ACCEPTED: primary NSW Electoral Commission release, updated 26 November 2026, 3:19 pm; underlying period 2023 state election. The Commission reports analysis of 5,327 reportable political donations valued at about $6.727 million. It reports 85% declared within the then-required 21 days, and late lodgements associated with 17% and more than $1.145 million. The latter percentage appears alongside a dollar amount; do not add it to the 85% count measure or assume the same denominator. Following 1,051 compliance audits, the release identifies recurring late lodgement, inaccurate donor details, duplicate disclosures and failures to aggregate donations. It reports a seven-day disclosure requirement for the 2027 pre-election period (1 October 2026–13 March 2027). Positive evidence: audit and public reporting, earlier disclosure deadline. Unknown: full report methodology, precise percentage denominators, actual compliance under new rules, sanctions and corrections. Acceptance is limited to the release; report PDF not examined. Inference: timely, accurate disclosure is a distinct protection from merely publishing records; eventual publication may not restore access at the time voters needed it.
Source: https://elections.nsw.gov.au/about-us/media-centre/news-and-media-releases/political-donations-transparency-special-report-to-nsw-parliament

### A02 — Kempsey by-election participation timetable
ACCEPTED: original NSW Electoral Commission election page and enrolment notice; observed 27 November 2026; notice indexed 23 November (exact page publication time not established). One councillor position; election 16 January 2027. Residential roll closes 6 pm 7 December; postal applications open 30 November and close 5 pm 11 January; early voting 9–15 January; telephone-assisted voting opens 11 January and is limited to electors who are blind or have low vision. Election day 8 am–6 pm. Council independently confirms election date. Positive evidence: alternative channels and advance timetable. Unknown: final voting venue accessibility, uptake, actual travel burdens and turnout. No exclusion or electoral misconduct established. Inference: the existence of alternative voting channels does not establish that each is available to every voter. Local public-interest record, not a candidate endorsement.

### A03 — Services Australia privacy assurance and complaint intelligence
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY: independent ANAO Report 12 of 2025–26, 9 December 2025, snapshot and conclusion. The auditor assessed management of client privacy as partly effective. It identified shortcomings in risk management, recordkeeping and privacy impact assessment transparency, and found that incident and complaint data were not analysed to assess privacy risk. Services Australia reported 89 notifiable breaches in 2024–25 versus 50 in 2023–24; 6,042 privacy incidents were recorded across 2022–23 to 2024–25. These are different categories and periods, not unique affected individuals. Positive evidence: governance arrangements existed; four of five recommendations to Services Australia agreed, one agreed in principle; recommendations to regulators also agreed wholly or in principle. Unknown: present compliance, unique affected people, reporting changes and completed remediation. Inference: collecting complaints and incidents without analysing them for risk can limit preventive assurance. This is historical and not evidence of a current Digital ID breach.
Source: https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/managing-the-privacy-of-client-information-services-australia

### A04 — NDIA board oversight and follow-up
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY: ANAO Report 41 of 2024–25, 10 June 2025, snapshot and recommendations 1–3 (paras 2.56, 3.37, 3.113). The auditor rated board governance largely effective, but operational oversight arrangements only partly fit for purpose. It recommended better documentation of conflict management, defined and implemented risk-framework review timing, and more consistent requests for further information or assurance when performance missed targets, assumptions were unclear or risks emerged. All three recommendations were accepted. Positive counter-evidence: governance was largely consistent with legislative requirements; the audit notes the chair and eight board members had lived experience of disability. This neither establishes that lived experience was ignored nor that representation guarantees adequate oversight. Unknown: implementation, subsequent board follow-up and participant outcomes. Inference: reporting to a board is not equivalent to the board testing and acting on that reporting.
Source: https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/effectiveness-of-the-board-of-the-national-disability-insurance-agency

### A05 — Aged care assessment and post-assessment waiting
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY: Productivity Commission Report on Government Services 2026, 29 January 2026 release, Part F media summary. For 2024–25 median assessment wait was 27 days (previously 22); median time to sign a service agreement after successful assessment was 245 days (previously 118 in 2023–24). A signed agreement is not necessarily the first delivered service; do not add medians across different cohorts. Positive counter-evidence: residential care services meeting total care minute targets increased from 34.0% to 45.9%, and 24/7 registered nurse coverage compliance from 89.3% to 93.5%. Different service settings and indicators. Unknown: current post-November-2025 reforms, distribution/tails of waiting, regional breakdown and underlying dataset definitions. Inference: assessment approval and access to support are distinct stages; improvement in residential staffing does not demonstrate improvement in home-care access.
Source: https://www.pc.gov.au/ongoing/report-on-government-services/

### A06 — RPA hospital construction milestone
ACCEPTED: original NSW Health release, 26 November 2026. The department reports the 15-storey East Tower has reached its final structural height as part of the $940 million RPA redevelopment. Fit-out, building services and façade remain; construction completion expected 2028–29. Planned services include expanded intensive care and maternity facilities and relocated transplant, respiratory and haematology units. Positive evidence: reported physical milestone and identified clinical needs including single rooms in transplant ward. Unknown: net operational capacity, staffing, final expenditure, commissioning and patient outcomes. Inference: physical construction progress, relocation, net expansion and operating capacity must be tracked separately. No assertion that all new services are available now.
Source: https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/news/Pages/20261126_00.aspx

## Rejected interpretations

R01 — Late political donation disclosure proves the donations were illegal or changed an election result. Unsupported; A01 addresses compliance reporting, not those conclusions.
R02 — The 245-day median represents all older Australians' current waiting time or proves no services were delivered before signing. Unsupported; A05 is a historical measure for a defined stage.

## 3. Findings — longitudinal comparison

M01 — Time-dependent transparency: A01 extends SR-513/SR-517 on delayed correction and SR-508 on electoral reporting. Disclosure after a relevant decision window may not deliver the same practical transparency as timely disclosure. Actual voter impact unknown.
M02 — Local accessibility: A02 extends SR-518 electoral accessibility; alternative channels have distinct eligibility and timing. No actual Kempsey exclusion established.
M03 — Complaint intelligence and oversight: A03 and A04 reinforce SR-520's concern about complaints failing to trigger systemic detection and SR-518's oversight distinction. Independent historical reports identify concrete governance gaps, but the entities, timeframes and legal obligations differ. No common motive or coordinated conduct established.
M04 — Access and delivery stages: A05 and A06 extend SR-517 and SR-521: assessment, agreement, commissioning, operational capacity and actual care are distinct milestones. Positive evidence remains preserved alongside adverse metrics.

## 4. Conclusion

The strongest fresh finding is the NSW Electoral Commission's report of recurring pre-election donation disclosure problems, with a shorter disclosure deadline now reported. The Kempsey by-election timetable provides immediately actionable local evidence. Historical privacy, board oversight and aged-care waiting evidence adds comparative depth. No present equivalent historical deficiency, electoral outcome manipulation or coordinated misconduct is established.

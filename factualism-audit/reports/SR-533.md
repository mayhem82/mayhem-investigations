## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — Federal Court distinguishes incurred expenses from unspecified future expenditure
ACCEPTED — original judicial reasons and orders, 27 November 2026, Shinetec (Australia) Pty Ltd v The Gosford Pty Ltd (Receivers and Managers Appointed) (No 2) [2026] FCA 1820, orders 1–3, paragraphs 57–68. The Court varied a freezing order to permit payment of $486,049.64 including GST in incurred receiver remuneration and legal expenses. It declined a further requested $300,000 allowance for potential future expenses because the nature and extent of future work had not been sufficiently specified. The reasons preserve the ability to challenge the amounts separately under Corporations Act sections 423 or 425. The Court acknowledged the costs of making a further application; it nevertheless preferred that process to an unqualified entitlement to unspecified future expenditure. The incurred costs were assessed as not unreasonable on a preliminary basis, not comprehensively audited or finally assessed under those separate provisions. Actual payment, later applications and final allocation of the frozen funds remain UNKNOWN.
Structural inference: a demonstrated entitlement to past expenses does not independently establish an entitlement to unspecified future expenses. This case does not establish a general rule for all public expenditure.
Source: https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/single/2026/2026fca1820

### A02 — Historical Black Friday sweep identifies potentially misleading urgency signals
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — ACCC announcement, 14 April 2026, concerning the 2025 sales period. Approximately half of 50 retailers examined made advertising claims of concern to the regulator. Some online countdowns implied a sale was about to finish despite the promotion continuing afterward. The ACCC reported commencing several investigations and indicated enforcement would depend on evidence of legal breaches. This is a regulator's screening finding, not a finding of liability against half the retailers or a representative prevalence estimate for the entire market. Positive evidence: active monitoring and a stated evidentiary threshold for enforcement. UNKNOWN: individual determinations, restitution, sampling representativeness, and results of the distinct 2026 sweep. The earlier sweep is distinguishable from the November 2026 warning previously recorded in SR-507 and SR-532.
Structural inference: a commercial interface can compress the decision time perceived by a consumer without the underlying time constraint being genuine.
Source: https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-investigating-retailers-making-concerning-black-friday-claims

## 3. Findings

M01 — Bounded authorisation. A01 provides a case-specific example of differentiating documented incurred expenses from unspecified prospective expenditure. The Court's decision preserves a separate challenge pathway rather than treating permission to pay as conclusive assurance. This extends the analytical distinction in SR-530 and SR-531 between particular decisions and separate accountability obligations.

M02 — Decision-time integrity. A02 extends SR-532's consumer-protection analysis and SR-523's time-dependent information analysis. A misleading urgency signal may reduce the time a person believes is available to consider a purchase. Genuine time limits and accurate information are not inherently improper. No quantified consumer harm is established by the sweep.

M03 — Historical continuity. Earlier references to SpinTel and ACMA financial blocking support probable duplicate classifications, not recovery of the original SR-395. No new historical original has been reconstructed. Search indexing differences in H04 remain unresolved.

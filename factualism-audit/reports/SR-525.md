## 2. Analysis — Accepted candidates

### A01 — Court transcription access outside contractual controls
ACCEPTED, retrospective incremental evidence — OAIC preliminary inquiry report, 4 November 2026. Previously held under SR-524 H08; an SR-501 extract describes the incident and preliminary outcome.
Source: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-assessments-and-decisions/privacy-decisions/Investigation-inquiry-reports/viq-solutions-pty-ltd-and-commonwealth-agency-contracting/_nocache
Companion announcement: https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/oaic-closes-preliminary-inquiries-into-viq-solutions

The OAIC identified a permissions error involving an overseas subcontractor. Five workers obtained access permissions; three actually accessed recordings from 93 court matters, including two ceremonial sittings. The Australian-hosted information remained within a transcription platform. Access was revoked on 11 February 2026. The courts encountered delays obtaining information. The regulator distinguished unauthorised access from cross-border disclosure, considered an APP 8 contravention unlikely on available information, and identified a possible APP 11 security issue. Formal contractual protections were assessed as adequate, but implementation of company security policies was not independently verified. VIQ ceased Australian operations in July 2026 and entered liquidation; the OAIC decided not to commence a formal commissioner-initiated investigation, taking account of the circumstances and resources. No concluded breach finding or general compliance assurance was issued. Counter-evidence: scope was narrower than media accounts suggested; contractual provisions supported inquiries and the courts assessed individual harm likelihood as low. Unknown: individual consequences, full implementation of controls and subsequent complaints. The number of court matters is not a count of individuals. Mechanism (inference): contractual adequacy and practical permission enforcement are distinct; investigation closure is not exoneration or completed restitution.

### A04 — Follow-up inspection of women in immigration detention
ACCEPTED, retrospective — AHRC announcement, 23 October 2026, following the December 2024 inspection report.
Source: https://humanrights.gov.au/about-us/media-centre/media-releases/women-in-immigration-detention-remain-an-afterthought-follow-up-inspection-finds

### A05 — Laboratory testing contradicts therapeutic product labels
ACCEPTED, retrospective — TGA safety advisory, 5 November 2026.
Source: https://www.tga.gov.au/safety/safety-monitoring-and-information/safety-alerts/tga-testing-finds-unapproved-peptides-do-not-contain-what-their-labels-claim
TGA reports laboratory testing of Synedica-branded unapproved peptide samples: some had no detectable labelled ingredient, and another group had less of its stated active ingredient with variation across samples. TGA advises people not to use the affected brand and to return remaining products to a pharmacy for disposal. Evidence class: regulator-reported laboratory findings and safety advice. Counter-evidence/limit: applies to tested samples; no full laboratory dataset or market-wide sampling frame examined. Unknown: total distribution, actual adverse events and final enforcement outcomes. Mechanism: a product's description is not proof of its composition; physical verification is distinct from documentary representation.

## 3. Findings — Longitudinal structural analysis

M01 — Contractual protections versus technical enforcement. A01 extends SR-518's delegated oversight and SR-520's intermediary supervision investigations. Contract clauses can establish protective obligations and support investigations while operational permissions still fail. Adequate contract terms are not evidence of complete operational compliance.

M02 — Evidence status and corrective closure. A01, A02 and A03 distinguish preliminary inquiries, admitted conduct, proposed orders and unadjudicated allegations. Closing an inquiry due to resource considerations and cessation of a business is not equivalent to a finding that no breach occurred. A remedy proposal is not an executed remedy.

M03 — Follow-up verification. A04 demonstrates why both verified improvements and remaining concerns must be retained. Acceptance of recommendations is a separate milestone from actual implementation.

M04 — Representation versus physical verification. A05 supplies regulator-reported direct testing as evidence of a mismatch between a label and sampled contents. This is comparable methodologically, not causally, to SR-521's record-integrity findings.

M05 — Community delivery. A06 identifies allocations to organisations delivering preventative and remedial services. Track disbursement and practical access separately from announcements, including regional outcomes.

## 4. Conclusion

The most important incremental evidence is the OAIC's detailed distinction between adequate contractual protections, unauthorised access, possible privacy non-compliance and closure without final findings. The TGA evidence reinforces separation of verified laboratory findings, regulatory allegations, admissions and proposed penalties. No coordinated misconduct, common improper motive, or new Bellbrook-specific failure established. No correction to a prior factual finding is warranted; SR-524 H08 is resolved at preliminary-report level by A01, not by a concluded legal determination.

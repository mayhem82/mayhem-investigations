## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — APRA retains macroprudential settings while monitoring lending risks

ACCEPTED at original regulator information-paper level. Published 25 November 2026.

FACT: APRA retained the mortgage serviceability buffer at 3 percentage points, the countercyclical capital buffer at 1% of risk-weighted assets and the high debt-to-income flow limit at 20% for loans with DTI of at least six times. The regulator reports exceptions to serviceability policy accounted for 5.8% of new lending in the June quarter.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: APRA considers access to credit and allows prudent exceptions; it says most banks are operating below the DTI cap. It reports that its policy assessment uses multiple indicators rather than automatic triggers.

UNKNOWN: Individual access outcomes, regional distribution and whether aggregate resilience measures adequately capture hardship.

INFERENCE: Prudential safeguards and individual access should be evaluated separately. A system-wide risk control does not itself establish that every individual application is fairly assessed.

Source: https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/update-apras-macroprudential-settings-november-2026

### A02 — Court allows a defective employment pleading to be corrected

ACCEPTED at original Federal Court judgment level. 26 November 2026, [2026] FCA 1797, orders 1–4 and paragraphs 49–53.

FACT: The court struck out an amended statement of claim because it did not sufficiently particularise the alleged exercise of workplace rights. It permitted a further amended claim by 18 December 2026 and otherwise dismissed the respondent's interlocutory application.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: The judge was not persuaded that the pleading defects could not be remedied. The respondent retains the right to understand the case it must answer.

UNKNOWN: The merits of the employment allegations and the subsequent outcome.

INFERENCE: Procedural sufficiency and substantive truth are separate determinations. Corrective access can preserve a claimant's opportunity while protecting procedural fairness for the respondent.

Source: https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/single/2026/2026fca1797

### A03 — Court separates costs according to independently contested matters

ACCEPTED at original Federal Court judgment level. 26 November 2026, Tama Australia Pty Ltd v Parterra Australia Pty Ltd (Costs) [2026] FCA 1819, paragraphs 10–19 and orders.

FACT: The court ordered the applicant to pay the respondents' costs of the originating application and the cross-claimant to pay the applicant's costs of the cross-claim. No costs order was made for the amendment application.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: The court considered the practical difficulty of disentangling costs but concluded the material did not support a fair global assessment. It declined to allocate costs separately to every individual issue within the infringement proceeding.

UNKNOWN: Final monetary amounts.

INFERENCE: A single aggregate outcome can conceal different responsibility allocations. Disaggregation is warranted where the underlying events are genuinely distinct, not simply whenever one party wins an individual argument.

Source: https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/single/2026/2026fca1819

### A04 — Tribunal president distinguishes assistive AI from judicial decision-making

ACCEPTED retrospectively at original institutional speech level. Delivered 25 June 2026.

FACT: The ART President describes the Council of Chief Justices' March 2026 judicial conduct guidance as prohibiting generative AI for judicial decision-making while allowing supportive uses subject to jurisdictional guidance. He recognises potential benefits for self-represented litigants, including clearer submissions.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: The speech discusses unreliable outputs, the risk of reduced human capability and the difficulty of explaining opaque systems. It notes the absence of a uniform national approach.

UNKNOWN: The actual impact of AI assistance on tribunal access or outcomes, and the complete operative directions of each jurisdiction.

INFERENCE: Improving a person's ability to articulate a case must be distinguished from delegating the authoritative determination of that case. This extends the accessibility-masking investigation in SR-506 without establishing that masking occurred in any tribunal case.

Source: https://www.art.gov.au/presidents-speech-australian-perspective-ai-and-judging

### A05 — Data breach frequency and affected-population severity move differently

ACCEPTED retrospectively at original OAIC statistical announcement level. Published 26 October 2026; reporting period January–June 2026.

FACT: OAIC received 667 notifications, compared with 662 in the preceding period. Notifications concerning breaches affecting 100,000–250,000 people increased from three to six; those affecting 500,000–1,000,000 people increased from zero to three. OAIC separately identifies global breaches affecting more than one million people.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: OAIC reports improvements in detection speed, with more than half of notified breaches identified within ten days.

UNKNOWN: Unique individuals affected, unreported breaches, current risk and sector-specific exposure rates. Notification counts are not a census of all incidents, and global affected populations cannot be counted as Australians.

INFERENCE: A relatively stable event count can coexist with increasing severity. Detection speed, prevention and human exposure are different measures.

Source: https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/large-scale-data-breaches-on-the-rise%2C-latest-oaic-statistics-reveal

### A06 — NSW Ambulance pilot reports alternative care for lower-acuity patients

ACCEPTED at verified NSW Health statement level. Published 25 November 2026; reported activity 1 June–30 September 2026.

FACT: NSW Health reports more than 1,200 lower-acuity patients in Illawarra and Shoalhaven received home care rather than travelling to an emergency department through an integrated clinical referral pilot. The hub links patient records, care teams and paramedic referrals.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: The model includes individual clinical assessment, not automatic diversion. The announcement does not report a controlled comparison of clinical outcomes.

UNKNOWN: Re-presentations, adverse events, patient consent and experience measures, comparative cost, and feasibility in the Upper Macleay.

INFERENCE: Reduced emergency department attendance is a potential benefit only when alternative care meets the patient's needs. This is positive comparative coordination evidence for SR-507, not proof of equivalent outcomes or an operating Bellbrook service.

Source: https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/news/Pages/20261125_00.aspx

### A07 — Regional hospital announces operational imaging upgrades

ACCEPTED at verified NSW Health statement level. Published 24 November 2026.

FACT: NSW Health reports that its $7.5 million Milton Ulladulla Hospital upgrade is open. X-ray equipment is operational; new CT services are available to inpatients and emergency department patients, with staged implementation. The cancer service centre has also been upgraded.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: A 21 May 2026 statement announced construction completion of the cancer centre stage but said operational commissioning was still required. The November notice supplies a later reported operational milestone; it does not establish unrestricted CT availability.

UNKNOWN: Actual service hours, transfer reductions, waiting times, staffing and measured patient outcomes.

INFERENCE: Construction completion, commissioning, restricted operation and full service availability must remain separate evidence states.

## 3. Findings

M01 — Measurement dimensionality. The OAIC announcement distinguishes notification frequency, exposure scale and detection performance. None can substitute for the others.

M02 — Procedural correction. A02 provides constructive evidence that a legal process can require greater specificity without foreclosing a potentially remediable claim. The corresponding right of the other party to understand the case must be preserved.

M03 — Attribution boundaries. A03 demonstrates why independent components of a dispute may require separate attribution of costs, while not every individual issue warrants its own allocation. Preserve both distinctions.

M04 — Assistive versus authoritative automation. A04 and A08 describe different legal and institutional contexts; their common analytical question is how authority, verification and human responsibility remain attached to technology. The comparison does not imply identical legal obligations.

M05 — Operational access. A06 and A07 document reported regional care pathways and operational milestones. Cross-reference SR-507's care-sector coordination finding and SR-506's infrastructure assurance findings. Patient outcomes and regional transferability remain unknown.

M06 — Prudential discretion. A01 provides positive evidence of a regulator acknowledging the need to balance aggregate resilience and credit access. Individual hardship and access require separate evidence.

## 4. Conclusion

The strongest current judicial evidence concerns procedural correction and distinct allocation of costs. The strongest constructive service evidence concerns regional clinical coordination and staged infrastructure operation.

The OAIC retrospective record demonstrates why stable aggregate event counts cannot be treated as proof of stable human exposure. The institutional AI speech distinguishes legitimate support from authoritative decision-making.

No accepted evidence establishes coordinated misconduct, improper motive or a new Bellbrook-specific service failure. No source establishes implementation of the outstanding ANAO audit recommendations because those two final reports remain unrecovered.

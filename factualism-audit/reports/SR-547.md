## 3. Findings — Longitudinal analysis

M01 — Notification latency and escalation: SR-539 continuing protection and SR-541 cross-agency coordination extend into AI governance. An official review identifies non-continuous monitoring of the initial notification channel. The documented chronology permits scrutiny of handoffs without assuming the cause of every interval. The same standard applies to AI providers and public institutions.

M02 — Parliamentary follow-through: the FOI release exposes internal tracking and response preparation rather than merely a final public response. A deadline, a workflow status, a signed response and a tabled response are distinct records. This complements SR-543's separation of completion labels and discharged obligations.

M03 — Principles-based obligations: ACMA guidance illustrates regulation of infrastructure intermediaries and contextual compliance. Neither uniform technical methods nor a guidance checklist alone demonstrates adequate outcomes. Actual protective performance and collateral access impacts require evidence.

M04 — Electoral accessibility: SR-541 and SR-542 retained unverified reports of storm closures. Official indexed text now identifies one named centre and cause. This strengthens the evidence of local operational interruption, without establishing the scale of electoral exclusion.

C01 — SR-517 H05 and SR-530 H07: original Industry FOI documents now recovered, with contemporaneous official briefing corroborated by subsequent review. Preserve original rejections as correct for the earlier evidence state.
C02 — SR-541 H05 and SR-542 H05: one official voting-centre closure identified; alternative voting arrangements and actual individual exclusion remain unknown. Prior reports unchanged.

Four accepted evidence developments. Factualism separates detection from disclosure, administrative tracking from discharged obligations, guidance from compliance, and service interruption from individual exclusion.

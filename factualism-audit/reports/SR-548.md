## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — Transitional housing capacity as a dependency of crisis accommodation

ACCEPTED — original joint ministerial announcement, 28 November 2026; announcement-stage evidence only.

Source: https://ministers.pmc.gov.au/gorman/doorstop-interview-innaloo-wa

The WA minister for prevention of family and domestic violence and the federal assistant minister to the Prime Minister announced 48 planned homes in the Perth area: 31 for family and domestic violence transitional accommodation and 17 for young people at risk of homelessness. The federal minister described approximately $23 million of Commonwealth construction funding through the Housing Australia Future Fund and distinguished construction support from state operational responsibilities involving community housing providers.

The state minister explicitly described a bottleneck in refuge accommodation and the intended function of transitional housing in enabling people to move beyond crisis accommodation. This is evidence of the government's recognition of a capacity dependency, not independently measured occupancy or unmet demand.

Counter-evidence/positive measures: an identifiable construction commitment and distinction between transitional and crisis accommodation; operational coordination recognised. The official transcript omits some questions to the state minister, so it is not a complete record of the event. No funding agreement, procurement records, construction schedule, occupancy or operating budget independently verified. No claim that the 48 dwellings are completed or available today. The wider approximately 9,800-home pipeline mentioned in the transcript is not a completed housing count.

UNKNOWN: actual refuge waiting times, funding disbursement, construction completion, service staffing, access criteria, safe transitions and permanent housing outcomes.

INFERENCE — downstream capacity dependency: if suitable transitional accommodation is unavailable, people may need to remain in crisis accommodation longer. The actual magnitude of this mechanism is unverified here. Providing shelter and enabling a safe transition to longer-term housing are distinct protective outcomes.

### A02 — Regulator addresses financial incentives linked to gambling customer activity

ACCEPTED — original ACMA announcement and guidance publication listing, 25 November 2026, retrospective. Scheduled commencement: 1 January 2027.

Sources: https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2026-11/acma-publishes-industry-guidance-online-wagering-provider-commissions
https://www.acma.gov.au/publications/2026-11/guide/prohibition-commissions

ACMA published initial guidance for licensed interactive wagering providers about a forthcoming prohibition on commissions linked to customer activity. Its announcement identifies employees, agents and other intermediaries involved in customer acquisition, retention, referral and account reactivation. It identifies customer actions including deposits, betting and removal of a harm-minimisation measure as examples relevant to the prohibition. Guidance also addresses commission promises that may never be realised.

Counter-evidence/positive measures: advance regulatory communication; recognition that incentives can influence conduct before a payment actually occurs. The announcement is not proof that any specific provider engaged in prohibited conduct. The underlying legislation and downloadable guidance PDF were not independently examined; legal scope is therefore bounded to the regulator's published explanation. The prohibition is scheduled for the future, not asserted as operative today.

UNKNOWN: implementation, enforcement, effectiveness, customer outcomes, and whether incentives are replaced with arrangements outside the prohibition.

INFERENCE — incentive architecture: protective measures can be undermined when commercial incentives reward actions that are in tension with those measures. Regulatory attention to incentive design is distinct from proving a particular person's harm or motive. This extends SR-547 A03's infrastructure-level regulatory analysis, not a duplicate of its separate digital access obligations.

### A02 — Federal Court: unopposed native title application still requires proof
ACCEPTED — original judgment and order, 27 November 2026, Mowburn Nominees Pty Limited v State Minister for the State of Queensland [2026] FCA 1832, paragraphs 2, 11–12 and 31–41. The Court considered a non-claimant application on the papers. Although the Minister did not oppose it and statutory notifications had been made, the applicant still bore the civil burden of proving that native title did not exist over the specific land. The Court found the confined evidence sufficient in the particular circumstances and considered a negative determination appropriate. The order directs parties to submit draft orders within 14 days; the final formulated determination was not examined. Counter-evidence and safeguards: the Court considered the significance and lasting consequences of a negative determination and treated absence of responses to notification as context-dependent, not conclusive. UNKNOWN: subsequent orders, appeals, consequences for affected people. No inference about other land or Aboriginal communities is warranted.
Inference: lack of opposition does not discharge an independent evidentiary burden; procedural silence and proof are distinct.
Source: https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/single/2026/2026fca1832

### A03 — ACCC review proposes changing unsolicited selling from cancellation to affirmative confirmation
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — original ACCC announcement 28 July 2026, release 85/26, reporting its review following a Consumer Action Law Centre designated complaint in March 2025. Among surveyed people who bought after an unsolicited approach in the previous two years, 54.1% reported receiving a written agreement and 63.0% being informed of the termination period. The regulator recommends replacing the existing cooling-off arrangement with a separate positive confirmation before the sale becomes effective and payment can proceed, and explicitly including lead generation in the rules. Counter-evidence: some businesses and consumers value this sales channel; the regulator proposes preserving it and reviewing reform effectiveness within two years of implementation. These are recommendations, not verified enacted law. Survey responses are not a census or individual court findings. UNKNOWN: underlying sampling methods (full report not examined), government response, enacted rules, individual restitution, measured outcomes. No previous explicit admission located in targeted screening; historical duplication remains possible.
Inference: a system that assumes consent unless a person cancels allocates the burden of protection differently from one requiring affirmative consent after the sales interaction.
Source: https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/misleading-and-high-pressure-unsolicited-sales-practices-widespread

## 3. Findings
M01 — Procedural silence is not proof. A02 supplies a case-specific judicial example of a principle also relevant to SR-527 (no complaints does not establish compliance). Different legal settings must remain separate.
M02 — Protective burden allocation. A03 identifies a concrete proposal to move responsibility from consumer cancellation to positive confirmation. This could reduce the burden placed on people exposed to high-pressure selling, but effectiveness is not yet verified. Inference, not prediction or demonstrated outcome.
M03 — Oversight existence versus capability. A01 establishes the scope of a current independent examination, not its outcome. Compare SR-523's distinction between board arrangements and effective oversight, without inferring equivalent findings.
C01 — Append-only clarification of SR-530 H08: the recovered NSW Audit Office project page describes an ongoing ARIC audit and contribution period, not a completed report. The earlier listing date of 12 November is not independently confirmed here. No earlier report altered. Final report remains outstanding.

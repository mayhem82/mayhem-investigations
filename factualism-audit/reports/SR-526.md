## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — Whole-of-initiative oversight lost across an $843.8 million Queensland programme
ACCEPTED — original Queensland Audit Office assessment, Report 6: 2026–27, tabled 27 November 2026. Report summary, recommendations, assessment scope and chapter 3 examined in HTML.
Source: https://www.qao.qld.gov.au/reports-resources/reports-parliament/oversight-advance-queensland-initiative

FACT/EVIDENCE: Advance Queensland grew from eight programmes and an initial $180.2 million budget in 2015 to 114 programmes and an approved $843.8 million budget by June 2026. QAO could not establish total expenditure. Following the 2022 change to decentralised governance, whole-initiative expenditure reporting ceased and no further macro-level evaluation occurred. The previous website's documents and reports were removed when it was replaced in August 2026. QAO found objectives lacked measurable success targets. The report is an assessment, not an audit or assurance engagement under Australian auditing standards; it did not determine whether programme objectives were achieved.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: Two earlier macro evaluations had been commissioned; the responsible department agreed to five recommendations for future initiatives. Some earlier contractual commitments continue.
UNKNOWN: Actual total expenditure, programme-level benefits, whether removed material remains available elsewhere, future implementation.
INFERENCE: A governance transition can interrupt the evidence chain needed to evaluate spending and benefits across the entire life of a programme. Approved funding is not money proven lost or spent.

### A02 — Excessive false positives impair public-sector fraud detection
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — WA Auditor General, Report 4: 2026–27, 11 November 2026; overview, case study 4 and recommendations examined in HTML.
Source: https://audit.wa.gov.au/reports-and-publications/reports/managing-fraud-and-corruption-risks-in-main-roads-alliance-projects/

FACT/EVIDENCE: Three road alliance projects costing $2.4 billion were examined; the auditor identified inadequate fraud-risk management and weaknesses in contractor integrity checks. A duplicate-payment test generated 60,978 flags from January 2020 to February 2025. Of 59,688 reviewed, 34 were confirmed duplicate payments worth approximately $975,000; 59,654 were false positives.
COUNTER-EVIDENCE: Main Roads accepted the recommendations, reported improvements and highlighted that actual fraud was not identified. The auditor's scope was risk controls, not establishing instances of fraud. Proposed implementation timeframes include February and March 2027.
UNKNOWN: Actual staff time, completed recovery of duplicate payments, independently verified control improvements, any actual fraud outside the audit scope.
INFERENCE: Poor detection design can divert resources away from meaningful risk; a reviewed flag does not necessarily mean a root cause was assessed. Neither the total project cost nor duplicate payments establish fraud losses.

### A03 — Recurring IT-control deficiencies and remediation delays
ACCEPTED — Queensland Audit Office, Information systems 2026, Report 4: 2026–27, tabled 19 November 2026. Report on a page and chapter 1 examined.
Source: https://www.qao.qld.gov.au/reports-resources/reports-parliament/information-systems-2026

FACT/EVIDENCE: QAO identified 13 new significant IT-control deficiencies and 240 other new deficiencies, the latter 21% above the prior year. Entities had remediated 63% of findings raised in 2025. The report links remediation difficulties to external providers, project dependencies, legacy systems and organisational change. Some unresolved findings remained beyond intended deadlines.
COUNTER-EVIDENCE: Financial-system IT controls were generally effective; progress on significant deficiencies was identified. QAO explicitly notes altered testing, scope and technology as factors affecting detection.
UNKNOWN: Individual incidents or losses, current remediation, whether all interim controls are effective, comparability under unchanged testing scope.
INFERENCE: A higher detection count can reflect both changes in underlying risk and changes in detection capability; corrective activity must address the recurring mechanism, not only close individual findings.

### A04 — Reliable financial reporting coexists with losses and control deficiencies
ACCEPTED — Queensland Audit Office, Energy 2026, Report 5: 2026–27, tabled 23 November 2026. Summary and chapter 2 examined.
Source: https://www.qao.qld.gov.au/reports-resources/reports-parliament/energy-2026

FACT/EVIDENCE: State-owned energy entities reported a combined $660.9 million loss in 2025–26 versus a $131.6 million profit the previous year. QAO issued unmodified financial-statement opinions. It recorded 60 internal-control deficiencies (46 previously), including 42 new and 18 recurring or unresolved findings. The audit identifies lower wholesale revenue, project losses and other factors behind the financial result.
COUNTER-EVIDENCE: Reporting deadlines were met and the reviewed climate disclosures received unmodified limited assurance; financial reporting controls were effective overall but improvable.
UNKNOWN: Individual household affordability, reliability of future electricity supply, remedial implementation, causal contribution of specific policies.
INFERENCE: Financial reporting assurance is not assurance of profitability, service quality or complete control effectiveness.

## 3. Findings — longitudinal comparison (inference)

M01 — Governance continuity: A01 strengthens SR-518 delegated oversight, SR-523 evidentiary challenge and SR-524 public-record visibility. Changing programme ownership or replacing a website can interrupt the longitudinal chain necessary for assessment; accountability requires a reconciled record that outlives the administrative structure. No intentional concealment established.

M02 — Detection overload: A02 adds an independently documented mechanism to SR-520 complaint intelligence and SR-521 detection-versus-determination. Excessive false positives can absorb investigative capacity. Both insufficient detection and excessive low-quality detection may reduce meaningful protection. A03 separately reinforces the need to distinguish changed measurement from changed underlying conditions.

M03 — Assurance boundaries: A04 extends SR-521 and SR-525: reliable accounts, financial performance, security and service outcomes are distinct conditions. A05 supplies constructive funding evidence without substituting allocations for outcomes.

M04 — Retrieval integrity: A06 resolves the earlier HTML-page access gap but not the underlying resolution gap. Do not infer that a 403 is deliberate restriction or that no public copy exists.

## 2. ANALYSIS — Accepted evidence

### A01 — Delegation does not discharge board oversight
ACCEPTED, incremental original regulatory text. APRA finalised CPS 510 Governance on 26 November 2026; paragraph 6 gives commencement as 1 January 2028. The original 34-page standard was retrieved and printed pages 3 and 6 visually examined. Part A paragraphs 13–15 concern locally incorporated regulated entities. Paragraph 14 allows delegation where applicable legislation permits it. Paragraph 15 retains board oversight and requires documented limits, monitoring and reporting of material issues or changes, and management of delegation-related conflicts.

FACT: APRA has published a final standard with a future commencement date. EVIDENCE: paragraphs 6 and 13–15, not merely the press release. INFERENCE: delegated execution can be separated from continuing oversight responsibility; this is a constructive example of a safeguard against responsibility diffusion. Counter-evidence: delegation can legitimately improve efficiency and proportionality; the requirements explicitly accommodate delegation rather than prohibit it. UNKNOWN: actual implementation, enforcement, any entity-specific adjustment, and comparative changes from every predecessor provision. No current breach or direct application to local councils is established.

### A02 — Revenue NSW publishes different starting dates for 2027 land tax clearance applications
ACCEPTED, retrospective direct institutional documentary comparison. Revenue NSW's 16 November 2026 announcement states clearance certificates can be requested through its client service providers from Monday 14 December 2026. Its land tax landing page, also marked updated 16 November 2026, states 15 December 2026. Both pages were directly retrieved. The announcement advises applying at least 14 days before settlement and indicates some charge-clearance processes may take up to 10 business days.

FACT: the published dates differ by one day. EVIDENCE: two official pages. INFERENCE: inconsistent timing information can transfer verification work to people managing time-sensitive transactions. Counter-evidence: both pages offer advance guidance; the difference could reflect distinct channels or an editorial error. No determination as to which date applies universally is made. UNKNOWN: reason for the difference, correct channel-specific date, correction, and actual delayed settlements or harm. Do not silently replace either date.

### A03 — Council recognises that a parking intervention may affect people living in vehicles
ACCEPTED, original council announcement dated 23 November 2026. Inner West Council announced a 12-month trial restricting long-term boat, trailer and caravan parking at an initial 25 locations, with rollout expected in the first half of 2027. Council intends to consult affected residents, businesses and sporting organisations before introduction, and homelessness support organisations about assistance for people living in affected vehicles.

FACT: announced trial and consultation commitment, not measured implementation. EVIDENCE: council media release. INFERENCE: a measure intended to resolve one public-space access problem may create a distinct exposure for people using vehicles as accommodation. Explicit recognition of that exposure is constructive preventive governance evidence. Counter-evidence: freeing scarce kerbside space is a legitimate objective; the trial is geographically targeted, not a demonstrated blanket prohibition; consultation and assistance are contemplated. UNKNOWN: final restrictions, exemptions, alternative accommodation, number of affected people, enforcement practice, and actual displacement. No demonstrated eviction, unlawful discrimination or individual injury.

## 3. FINDINGS
M01 — Responsibility retention. The original APRA standard supplies an explicit design benchmark for documented delegation, oversight, escalation and conflict management. It extends SR-508 and SR-549's delegation/consolidation themes. This is comparative analysis, not a claim that the prudential standard governs councils.
M02 — Administrative date integrity. The Revenue NSW discrepancy extends SR-546's Ombudsman date-integrity track. Accurate official information can matter even where the substantive service exists. Actual effects remain unknown.
M03 — Cross-purpose policy effects. The parking trial makes a potential competing human need visible before implementation. Recognition is constructive but not itself mitigation or realised protection. This extends SR-548's protective housing track.
M04 — Processing-stage integrity. Provisional district results show why identical measurement labels do not necessarily imply identical counting completeness. Differences cannot establish causes or final outcomes.

C01 — SR-508 and SR-549 H08: APRA's final CPS 510 text, specifically paragraphs 6 and 13–15, now recovered and visually checked. Earlier announcement-level and unresolved classifications remain valid for their original evidence states. No previous report overwritten.
C02 — SR-549 H05: indexed district observations now available; statewide retrieval remains unresolved. This is a bounded continuity update, not a correction of historical counts.

## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — ACMA identifies practical interface barriers despite generally high compliance

ACCEPTED — original regulator compliance update and accompanying announcement, 25 November 2026. Framework commenced 10 January 2026.

FACT/EVIDENCE: ACMA reports generally strong compliance and no complaints within its jurisdiction about device compliance at publication. It nevertheless identifies some devices requiring extra user action to obtain offered free-to-air broadcaster apps and a small number where required apps or live-TV icons may not be visible on the home screen. Additional downloads or obscured icons are identified as likely non-compliance, not final adjudications. The regulator used manufacturer voluntary attestations and commissioned real-device testing through Swinburne University.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: Positive industry engagement, generally high compliance, and user choice after compliant setup are recognised. Absence of complaints does not independently establish absence of access barriers.

UNKNOWN: Complete sample and methodology, individual device compliance findings, actual consumer effects and remediation. The 19.21 MB testing report was identified but not analysed.

INFERENCE — Structural mechanism: Nominal availability versus practical discoverability; independent testing can identify risks not captured by complaint counts.

### A02 — Bathurst distinguishes rural development access standards from road maintenance obligations

ACCEPTED — original council meeting summary published 19 November 2026, meeting 18 November; retrospective outside preferred window by one day.

FACT/EVIDENCE: Council reports adopting a revised policy for minimum public-road standards for dwellings in rural and village areas, including Crown roads, and repealing its previous construction and transfer policy. The consultation ran 28 days and received two formal submissions. Council explains that the revised policy concerns prospective development applications requiring suitable vehicle access, not compulsory upgrading of existing properties outside that process, and not a maintenance standard. Its summary identifies differing approaches between Crown Lands and council regarding road transfers.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: Council sought consultation and explicitly defines the policy's limited purpose. Its account of Crown Lands' approach is an institutional position, not an independently established motive or finding of unlawful conduct.

UNKNOWN: Full adopted policy, underlying resolutions and road-specific reports, Crown Lands' response, actual maintenance responsibility for particular roads, costs and practical resident access.

INFERENCE — Structural mechanism: Regulatory scope and responsibility transfer. An access standard for development approval does not establish an ongoing maintenance obligation. This is a Bathurst-specific record, not a finding about Kempsey.

Source: https://www.bathurst.nsw.gov.au/Council/News-Media/November-2026-Council-Meeting-News-Report

### A03 — Face-to-face assistance bridges digital government service access

ACCEPTED — original Bayside Council notice dated 20 November 2026 and event listing.

FACT/EVIDENCE: Free individual myGov coaching by Services Australia staff was scheduled at Eastgardens Library on 20 November and Rockdale Library on 30 November, 10 am–1 pm for the latter. No registration required. The assistance covers account setup/access, linking services, self-service navigation and scam awareness.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: The initiative is a concrete human-assistance pathway for digital services, rather than merely directing people to a website. It is time- and location-limited, and coaching does not replace direct resolution of substantive benefits or service disputes.

UNKNOWN: Attendance, actual delivery of the first session, continuity beyond these dates, accessibility, successful resolution and rural equivalents.

INFERENCE — Positive structural mechanism: Supported digital participation. Human assistance can bridge a digital access barrier, but an announced service does not establish effective access for every person.

### A04 — Partial recovery of Kempsey draft minutes establishes a delegation resolution

ACCEPTED — direct observation of one page image (printed page 6) of original draft minutes of 17 November 2026, retrieved 28 November.

FACT/EVIDENCE: Resolution 2026.214, item 10.13, records a unanimous 6–0 decision to delegate or subdelegate functions to the Chief Executive Officer, in accordance with the instrument attached to the report. The document records the CEO as the General Manager for Local Government Act 1993 purposes. The same page records resolution 2026.213, a unanimous decision to exhibit a proposed $1,650 dual-position columbarium fee for at least 28 days; a subsequent report is to determine adoption.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE: The draft minutes page supplies a traceable resolution reference and voting result; the proposed fee is subject to exhibition and further consideration.

UNKNOWN: Full delegation instrument and its exceptions, final confirmed minutes, underlying Rise Stage 2A resolution, other pages. The initial PDF response identified 14 pages; subsequent text retrieval and most page screenshots returned HTTP 403. Only printed page 6 was successfully examined. This is partial resolution of SR-526 H04's document access gap, not full recovery or proof of intentional obstruction.

INFERENCE — Structural mechanism: Formal delegation and the actual scope of delegated power must remain distinct. A resolution pointing to an attachment cannot establish the contents of that attachment.

## 3. Findings — Longitudinal structural analysis

M01 — Complaint intelligence and independent testing: A01 provides constructive comparative evidence for SR-520 and SR-523. A regulator can detect practical access barriers by testing a system even when no complaint within its remit has been received. This is not evidence of equivalent misconduct across institutions. Reporting, testing, determination and correction remain separate.

M02 — Scope integrity: Bathurst's road policy and Kempsey's delegation resolution show why the precise scope of instruments matters. A development approval standard, existing maintenance obligation, transfer of road responsibility, and delegation of functions are distinct legal and administrative conditions. Do not infer the content of an unexamined attachment or apply another council's policy locally.

M03 — Human assistance: Bayside's myGov coaching offers constructive comparison with SR-511, SR-521 and SR-524 on channel accessibility. Digital availability and supported digital access are distinct. Announced coaching is not equivalent to completed service delivery or nationwide coverage.

M04 — Retrieval integrity: The Kempsey PDF produced mixed results in the same run. Preserve successful page-level evidence and failed requests; do not promote partial access to complete recovery or infer intention from HTTP 403.

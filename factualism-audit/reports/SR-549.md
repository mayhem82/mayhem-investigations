## 2. ANALYSIS — Accepted

### A01 — AI-generated complaint summaries within Commonwealth Ombudsman processes
ACCEPTED, retrospective incremental primary evidence. FOI-2026-80168, access 2 October 2026, one-page partial release, s47E(d) exemption; register row records impact assessment date 30 March 2026. Original PDF and page image examined. The UC001 ORBIT case summaries and timeline highlights entry identifies a large language model within Microsoft Dynamics 365, in operating lifecycle, intended to summarise complaint details and recent correspondence for complaint handlers. It records workplace productivity/service delivery, low inherent and residual risk, technical standard fully applied, and a scope criterion concerning personal/sensitive or security-classified information. These are institutional classifications, not independently tested safeguards. The public transparency statement (April 2026 revision) confirms summaries help allocation and urgency triage, says they are not used to decide action on complaints, and states officer confirmation is required for AI-populated complaint forms. It prohibits AI statutory decisions and externally directed AI-generated text. Public complainant guidance (date not displayed) warns AI-generated text can be inaccurate or misrepresent the person's intended account, offers human assistance and advises checking accuracy. DTA policy update 12 January 2026 describes accountable owners, internal registers and pre-deployment impact assessments, with phased requirements.
Counter-evidence: named accountable role, risk assessment, stated human decision responsibility, public transparency and advice to complainants. Unknown: original-to-summary fidelity, omission/error rates, officer access to full original, correction/audit trail, actual effects on prioritisation, whether all listed tools are in scope, complete assessment, current deployment details. No automated rejection, unlawful processing, inconsistent register scope or actual harm established.
Inference: AI may influence which complaint receives attention and when, without making the eventual statutory decision; the preservation of original accounts and traceability of summaries should therefore be separately assessed. A summary is a derived evidence object, not the original.

### A02 — Complaint allocation time is measurable, but not equivalent to resolution
ACCEPTED retrospective primary FOI data, FOI-2026-80129, access 16 September 2026. One-page PDF visually examined. Average calendar days to first allocation to a complaints officer, as reported: 2023–24: 28 (Q1 13, Q2 31, Q3 45, Q4 25); 2024–25: 41 (28, 35, 48, 49); 2025–26: 31 (47, 29, 32, 13). Grand total average 33. Positive counter-evidence: Q4 2025–26 was 13 days, compared with 49 in Q4 2024–25; annual average fell from 41 to 31. Unknown: denominator, unallocated cases, median and tail distribution, urgency cohorts, cause of changes, whether period grouping is by receipt or allocation, time to resolution and human outcomes. No causal relationship with AI implementation established. The allocation measure does not establish investigation commencement, final remedy or all complaints' waiting time.

## 3. FINDINGS
M01 — Evidence transformation precedes formal decision-making. SR-544 examined editorial transformation of disaster evidence; A01 shows a documented machine-generated intermediate account used for triage and allocation. Legitimate summarisation may aid staff, but the original must remain distinguishable and available. Human final decision authority is a positive safeguard but does not by itself verify upstream fidelity. The public warning about AI-generated complaint distortion is also a relevant standard for internal summaries; external and internal contexts are different, so no hypocrisy or equivalence of risk is inferred.
M02 — Accountability delay requires stage-specific measures. A02 provides allocation averages and counter-evidence of improvement; neither an average nor a first allocation measures the full complaint lifecycle. No causal linkage to A01.
M03 — Jurisdictional process consolidation requires traceable responsibility. A03 identifies a proposed single approval route, not a verified reduction in protection or proof of efficiency.
M04 — Financial accessibility is component-specific. A04 extends SR-540's free-admission versus journey-access distinction: tuition waiver does not eliminate materials or student fees.
C01 — SR-548 H10: the Ombudsman AI register is now recovered and visually examined; earlier rejection stands as the historical evidence state.
C02 — SR-539 H09: explicit tuition-versus-other-fees scope recovered. Expansion outcomes remain unknown.
No prior report overwritten.

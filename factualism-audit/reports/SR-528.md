## 2. Analysis — Accepted evidence

### A01 — Mid North Coast aged-care elapsed times and cohort boundaries
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — original statutory departmental report, 11 November 2026, quarter 1 July–30 September 2026. Sections 1, 2, 4, 6, 10; tables 1 and 6; page images of executive summary, methodology, triage count and regional table examined.
FACT (reported measures): 267 days median and 301 days mean nationally from first application to commencement of covered non-specialist services. Ongoing Support at Home median 309 days; ongoing residential care 146 days; end-of-life pathway 15 days; restorative care 58 days. Mid North Coast aged-care planning region (all covered service types) median 283 days, mean 335 days. 11,270 first applications awaiting triage decision nationally at quarter end.
Counter-evidence and boundaries: measures relate to service commencements during the quarter, not all applicants or those still waiting. A person can contribute multiple service types. Reassessments, support plan reviews and specified opt-out/transition cohorts are excluded; groups with fewer than ten relevant commencements are suppressed. Regional attribution uses address at commencement; not necessarily residence at application. Time includes individual choice, provider availability and other factors, not exclusively departmental delay. Faster end-of-life pathway is positive differentiation, not proof of universal timely access. Department publishes method and limits. Data extracted 12 October. The triage snapshot is not total care waiting list.
INFERENCE: completed-access measures cannot reveal the full experience of those who never reached service commencement. Regional variation is visible, but causation is not established. The historical SR-523 245-day assessment-to-agreement median is a different measure and cannot be used to calculate deterioration against the 309-day application-to-commencement median.
UNKNOWN: present queues, waits among non-commencers, unmet needs, deaths while waiting, avoidable deaths, Bellbrook-specific results, drivers of regional differences, outcomes of excluded people. No causal attribution of deaths.

### A02 — CommBiz remediation and respondent counter-evidence recovered
ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — incremental evidence, same case as SR-520 A01, not a new incident. Original ASIC release 26-279MR (exact day not visible in retrieved regulator text); CBA ASX announcement dated 12 November 2026, single page visually examined.
FACT: proceedings commenced. ASIC ALLEGATIONS: around 42,000 customers overcharged about $8.5 million from November 2020 to May 2025; complaints from at least 2015; internal audit in 2016; alleged issue extended roughly 19 years. Not adjudicated.
Counter-evidence: ASIC itself reports that the bank halted the fees temporarily in June 2025, revised terms in August 2025, waived relevant fees from February 2026 and completed a remediation programme spanning December 2006 onwards, resulting in more than $26.4 million in payments to about 93,000 customers, including about 40,000 small businesses. CBA says it self-reported, cooperated, corrected the disclosure and implemented further prevention measures; apologises but disputes formulation of some allegations and expects to defend aspects. These are attributed accounts, not a court finding. The litigation period and remediation population differ: do not subtract or equate the amounts or counts.
INFERENCE: regulatory proceedings can coexist with substantial reported remediation already carried out; accountability assessment must preserve both. UNKNOWN: actual individual adequacy of payments, unpaid residual entitlements, complete pleadings, final judicial findings, sustained controls.

### A03 — ABS respiratory mortality revisions across consecutive releases
ACCEPTED — incremental cross-vintage evidence; not a claim of a new underlying mortality incident. September reference release dated 29 October 2026 (registration cut-off 30 September), October reference release dated 27 November 2026 (cut-off 31 October). Original ABS statistical publications, monthly underlying-cause counts.
FACT: September 2026 influenza underlying-cause deaths were recorded as 69 in the October 29 publication and 201 in the November 27 publication. August 2026 influenza counts changed from 86 to 92; September COVID-19 counts from 30 to 55. In the later publication, deaths involving influenza (including contributing cause) were 234 in September, distinct from the 201 underlying-cause deaths.
Counter-evidence: ABS explicitly labels data provisional, subject to revision and affected by receipt of registrations and cause-of-death updates. The earlier release was not a final September count. The revised 2026 figures remain provisional; differences between registration-based data and other surveillance systems are expected. No fabrication or failure by the ABS established.
INFERENCE: date of publication, observation period, data cut-off and definition must be preserved in a longitudinal archive. Comparing incomplete months against later-completed months without qualification may generate false conclusions.
UNKNOWN: final counts, contribution of individual registration/coding changes, local incidence and cause of mortality changes. No current month estimate or predictive model.

## 3. Findings
M01 — Completion-conditioned measurement: A01 extends SR-523 A05 by measuring actual recorded commencement rather than agreement alone. A separately reported cohort of people still awaiting a decision does not capture every person yet to receive services. Human outcomes among non-commencers require separate evidence. The distinct service pathways and their published methodological limitations are material positive evidence.
M02 — Remediation counter-evidence: A02 adds actual reported payments and respondent position to SR-520. A corrective system must not treat enforcement proceedings as proof that no remediation has happened. Conversely it must not treat reported remediation as a substitute for individual outcome verification or adjudication.
M03 — Temporal integrity: A03 gives a concrete example of the importance of retaining both statistical vintages. This extends SR-526's measurement-change track. A revision is not necessarily an error or misconduct. Preserve data cut-off and definition before interpreting trends.
C01 — Append-only clarification to SR-520 A01 (CommBiz only). SR-520 recorded actual remediation and the complete institutional response as unknown on the basis of the parliamentary opening statement. The more detailed ASIC release and CBA disclosure now establish reported completed remediation and the bank's response, as described in A02. Those previous unknowns are narrowed, not erased; independent verification of every affected customer's outcome remains unknown. No change to AIG/Macquarie classifications. Prior report remains unchanged. C01 is a separately identified correction record within this append-only report, not an extra candidate.

## 4. Conclusion
The most significant new local-relevant evidence is the Mid North Coast aged-care commencement measure: median 283 days, mean 335 days for covered service commencements in the July–September quarter. Not a Bellbrook-specific estimate or current waiting list. Material counter-evidence has been added to the CommBiz case. The ABS comparison reinforces vintage-aware evidence preservation. No coordinated misconduct, judicial finding of CommBiz liability, avoidable deaths caused by aged-care delays, or new Bellbrook-specific failure established.

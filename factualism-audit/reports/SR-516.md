## 2. Analysis — Accepted candidates

### A01 — South Australia Police withdraw approximately 8,383 mobile speed-camera notices

ACCEPTED — directly retrieved official police statement, published 27 November 2026 at 2:52 pm local time.

SAPOL announces withdrawal of approximately 8,383 notices and 13 further court matters identified to date, covering affected mobile-camera detections from 6 November 2025 to 29 October 2026. The stated reason is non-compliance with the timing of testing required by the applicable Australian Standard. Police say the separate regulatory testing requirement was met but the evidentiary certification requirement was not. Police changed deployment procedures on 29 October following identification of the issue; additional processing resources and notifications from next week are announced.

Counter-evidence: SAPOL maintains camera accuracy was not at issue. Fixed cameras, point-to-point cameras, handheld devices and officer-issued notices are outside this announced withdrawal. This is a police account, not an independent determination of every offence or camera reading.

UNKNOWN: completed withdrawals, refunds, consequential licence or demerit-point corrections, exact legal instruments and individual outcomes. A notice is not necessarily a unique person.

INFERENCE: technical accuracy and legal evidentiary validity are separate protective conditions. Compliance with one overlapping requirement cannot replace compliance with another. Correcting future procedures and remediating historical consequences are also separate tasks.

Source: https://www.police.sa.gov.au/sa-police-news-assets/traffic/south-australia-police-advise-of-withdrawal-of-expiation-notices

### A02 — NSW feral pig surveillance identifies previously under-recorded disease exposure

ACCEPTED — departmental research announcement, 26 November 2026.

NSW DPIRD reports testing 2,548 feral pigs across 81 locations between 2022 and 2026. Evidence of exposure to Brucella suis extends farther south and west than earlier surveys indicated. More than half of tested animals showed evidence of prior exposure to a leptospirosis-associated pathogen. Additional surveillance is planned.

Counter-evidence: structured surveillance and adaptation of future monitoring are positive measures; the announcement does not establish that every sampled animal was infectious.

UNKNOWN: underlying journal methods, regional sampling weights, current infections, transmission to people and Bellbrook-specific prevalence.

INFERENCE: earlier detection boundaries are not necessarily actual risk boundaries. Exposure evidence, active infection and human disease must not be conflated.

Source: https://www.dpird.nsw.gov.au/about-us/media-centre/releases/2026/general/ongoing-biosecurity-actions-key-in-managing-feral-pig-disease-risks

### A04 — NSW publishes state-finance report; ministerial comparison requires baseline discipline

ACCEPTED — Treasury report landing page and ministerial statement, 27 November 2026. The report PDF itself was not analysed.

The ministerial statement reports a 2025–26 deficit of $2.9 billion against $10.6 billion for 2022–23, and says the result was $575 million better than the 2025–26 Budget forecast. It reports net debt below earlier projections, while acknowledging emerging revenue pressure and borrowing-cost risks. The Treasury landing page independently confirms publication of the 2025–26 report, not the individual figures.

Counter-evidence: the same announcement acknowledges adverse economic pressures. Partisan credit and historical superlatives are not admitted as independently established.

UNKNOWN: audited accounting definitions, actual debt trend, reconciliation to forecast baselines, audit opinion and effects on services. A smaller deficit is not a surplus; debt below forecast does not establish an absolute fall in debt.

INFERENCE: actual-to-actual and actual-to-forecast comparisons must remain labelled separately. Fiscal results do not independently establish human outcomes.

### A06 — Insolvency review separates legal process from creditor recovery

ACCEPTED RETROSPECTIVELY — ASIC original announcement and report landing page, report released 27 October 2026; review period 1 July 2021–30 June 2025.

REP 843 examined 26,664 grouped appointments. ASIC reports around half of finalised liquidations realised gross assets of $10,000 or less, around one quarter realised none, and 5.3% of finalised liquidations paid an ordinary unsecured creditor dividend. Limited assets constrain investigations, recoveries and remuneration. Only eight simplified liquidations were adopted in 2024–25.

Counter-evidence: ASIC has published a data pack to enable granular examination; finalised cases may overrepresent shorter or simpler matters. Low assets and poor recoveries do not independently establish wrongdoing. Liability size alone does not establish simplified-liquidation eligibility.

UNKNOWN: full report methods, subgroup variation, recoveries in unfinished matters, employee-specific outcomes and prior admission within missing audits. No PDF or spreadsheet was analysed.

INFERENCE: formal recovery procedures cannot guarantee practical recovery where resources are exhausted. Process completion and beneficiary resolution must be distinguished.

## 3. Findings — longitudinal structural analysis

M01 — Overlapping compliance boundaries: SAPOL documents a divergence between two testing requirements and the validity of resulting evidentiary certification. This extends SR-514's control-layer separation. The scope of the law itself has not been independently interpreted.

M02 — Remediation continuity: withdrawal announcements and future procedural correction do not establish complete historical remediation. This extends SR-513's residual-burden investigation. Actual repayments or restoration of rights require verification.

M03 — Detection boundaries: NSW disease surveillance strengthens SR-515's adaptive surveillance track. New evidence can expand a known problem boundary without establishing the underlying change in prevalence.

M04 — Baseline discipline: the state-finance announcement contains both historical actuals and forecast comparisons. Preserve both labels, acknowledge stated adverse conditions and test claims against audited accounts before adopting political interpretations.

M05 — Resource constraints and human outcomes: ASIC's insolvency findings and the childcare audit scope illustrate distinct contexts where administrative mechanisms require resources to produce actual protective outcomes. No common motive or causal link established.

M06 — Case isolation: the separate dismissed migration appeal prevents extrapolation of SR-515 A01 into a general assertion about Tribunal decisions.

## 4. Conclusion

Strongest new evidence: SAPOL's announced mass withdrawal following discovery of testing-procedure non-compliance. The distinction between claimed factual accuracy, legal evidentiary validity and completed historical remediation warrants continuing scrutiny.

No coordinated wrongdoing, common improper intent, Bellbrook-specific disease prevalence or new Bellbrook service failure established. No inference is promoted to an established fact.

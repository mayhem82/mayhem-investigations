The Experiment

A council has been selected. An audit system is being built around its real public records — without giving the system a list of problems, people, matters or conclusions it is supposed to find.

BUILD STAGE
The experiment is active as a construction process, but the audit machinery is not yet a completed or validated operational system. Designed or coded capability is not counted as demonstrated capability.

How far can an AI-assisted audit system develop when it has to work from the council’s own record rather than from a prewritten case?

What is actually being tested?

1. Open audit

The council is known. The answer is not. No allegation or selected matter is required to start the audit. The machinery must encounter the record as it exists and determine what warrants attention.

2. Discovery

Can it identify significant material, recurring subjects, entities, decisions, commitments, changes, omissions, anomalies or other audit signals without being handed the list beforehand?

3. Connection

Can it connect records published separately, recognise recurrence, build chronology and distinguish a continuing matter from unrelated similarity?

4. Audit depth

When a signal appears, can the machinery progressively examine relevant records, relationships, chronology, governance and evidence without the experimenter manually prescribing every next audit path?

5. Evidence discipline

Can every meaningful output remain traceable to source material while keeping observation, derived structure, inference, verification and finding distinct?

6. Verification

Can the system resist turning repetition, similarity, internal agreement or absence of contradiction into proof? Can it show what is verified, what is not, and why?

7. Failure and correction

Failures are experimental evidence. When human correction is required, the question is whether the correction can become a persistent mechanism instead of being repeated manually next time.

8. Development

Do accumulated mechanisms produce durable capabilities rather than one-off answers? Does the machinery become better at conducting the audit because previous failures changed how it operates?

9. Governance

As capability increases, can constraints increase with it — preserving provenance, verification boundaries, uncertainty, auditability and controlled publication rather than trading discipline for autonomy?

10. Transfer

Does a capability developed while handling one council matter remain useful when a different matter appears, without hard-coding the earlier subject into the machinery?

11. Continuity

A council keeps producing records. Can the machinery absorb later material, preserve state, recognise genuine continuation and revise its audit record without repeatedly starting from scratch?

12. Public accountability output

Can complex audit work eventually be translated into useful public information while preserving the chain back to evidence and preventing unsupported claims from escaping the system?

The roles are deliberately different

The experimenter

Selects the council, sets experimental constraints, observes behaviour, challenges failures and decides what corrections or new tests are introduced.

The AI and software tools

Are apparatus. They perform parts of acquisition, preservation, extraction, structuring, audit reasoning, testing, verification support and publication control. They are not co-investigators and do not own the experiment.

The council

Provides the real-world audit environment and continuing body of records against which the machinery is exercised.

The machinery

Is the developing audit system whose behaviour, failures, corrections, constraints and reproducible capabilities are being observed over time.

This is not a promise that every stage will succeed. The experiment records where the machinery works, where it fails and what has actually been demonstrated.

What counts as development?

More output is not enough. More apparent autonomy is not enough. A capability matters when it can be demonstrated, reproduced, governed and retained.

The key measure is whether the system can sustain more of the audit correctly under established controls without the experimenter having to continuously make it behave correctly.

Human intervention is therefore part of the experimental record. A repeated correction exposes an unresolved machinery problem; a correction converted into a reliable mechanism is evidence of development.

The experiment has memory

Successful runs are evidence. Failed runs are evidence. Human corrections are evidence. Changes to the machinery are evidence. New records arriving later create new tests.

The experimental record should make it possible to distinguish what the system was intended to do, what it actually did, what failed, what changed afterward and whether that change survived later use. The experiment therefore develops longitudinally rather than being judged from a single demonstration.

What is not predetermined

No list of wrongdoing is supplied. No actor is presumed responsible. No matter is presumed important. No connection is true merely because the machinery proposes it. No finding is verified because it sounds plausible. The experiment may expose important audit paths, mundane administrative continuity, machinery failure, or nothing significant in a particular body of material. Those outcomes have to come from operation and evidence.

First contact changes the experiment

Construction before council contact establishes the machinery that first enters the real audit environment. Once substantive council records are admitted, the experiment changes from building the apparatus to subjecting that apparatus to sustained real-world pressure.

The council does not supply an answer key. Its records create the conditions that expose what the machinery can and cannot reliably do. Ambiguity tests interpretation; recurrence tests continuity; contradiction tests evidence handling; missing material tests uncertainty; long-running matters test chronology; governance structures test accountability mapping; and later records test whether state and corrections persist.

The process acts on the machinery. The objective is not to make the machinery memorise how to audit one council, but to determine whether failures encountered there can be converted into general, governed mechanisms that survive different matters and later records.

Advocacy Package

Tests whether substantiated audit material can be transformed into an actionable, evidence-linked external package without breaking the chain back to the governed audit record.

Enforcement Notice

Tests whether established findings can be translated into specific corrective demands with the relevant obligation, evidence, accountable body, required action and authority clearly distinguished from advocacy or recommendation.

Remedies

Tests whether the machinery can move from identifying a substantiated problem to constructing corrective pathways that address the underlying mechanism, identify who can act and specify what evidence would demonstrate implementation.

Remedy verification

A promise, announcement, expenditure or stated intention does not close the loop. Later evidence must establish whether the remedy was implemented, whether it worked, whether the underlying condition persists and whether renewed accountability action is required.

Each downstream stage applies new pressure to the machinery. A sound audit that fails during advocacy exposes a limitation. An advocacy package that cannot establish authority for corrective demands exposes another. An enforcement path with an unmeasurable remedy exposes another. A remedy that cannot be verified exposes another. Those failures return to the development cycle rather than being hidden at the end of it.

The developmental experiment

The machinery that first touches the council establishes the substantive starting capability. Continued council contact then supplies repeated pressure: audit, failure, correction, mechanism formation, retesting, transfer and retention.

The experiment measures whether this cumulative process produces machinery that is qualitatively different from the machinery at first contact: able to sustain increasingly complex and previously unspecified audit-to-accountability-to-remedy work under accumulated governance while requiring progressively less repeated corrective intervention from the experimenter.

There is no assumption that this mature condition will be reached. The distance between demonstrated current capability and sustained mature capability is itself part of the experimental record.

The final transformation being tested

The mature endpoint is not merely a powerful audit tool. The experiment tests whether the governance architecture developed and proven through the audit machinery can ultimately become governance architecture within other systems.

That is a stronger test than producing a correct finding, securing one remedy, influencing an institution or installing another copy of the audit machinery. The external system would have to acquire and sustain the relevant evidence discipline, provenance, verification boundaries, accountability controls, correction mechanisms and recursive governance within its own operation.

The evidence for that transition would therefore exist outside the originating machinery: in another system demonstrably sustaining the governance properties rather than depending on continuous external enforcement by the audit apparatus.

Current position

The neutral foundations now under construction include explicit source boundaries, exact preservation and provenance, neutral discovery, extraction, source-bound observations, candidate relationships, source-derived recurrence, evidence assembly, a verification boundary and a deny-by-default public release gate.

These components have been coded, but the complete machinery has not yet been executed and validated end to end. Mechanical test code exists for multiple components. That is construction evidence, not proof that the complete audit system works.

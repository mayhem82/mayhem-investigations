What it does

Follow decisions through time

The tracker is designed to interrogate authoritative public Council records continuously. It does not begin with a preselected issue, locality or investigation.

What appeared?

Identify Council records, resolutions, recommendations, actions and explicit responsibility terms from the source material.

What changed?

Compare later Council records with earlier records while preserving chronology and source identity.

What was claimed?

Retain Council statements about progress, completion, amendment, deferral or rescission as source claims rather than automatically accepting them as outcomes.

What was verified?

Keep claimed completion separate from independently verified outcome. Verification requires human authority and supporting evidence.

Source documents and derived text are cryptographically bound by SHA-256 through the processing path. Extraction or acquisition failure remains visible and is never treated as proof that something did not occur.

Reading the tracker

Claim is not verification

Council source claim

Council records contain language indicating an action, progress or outcome. This establishes what the source says, not necessarily what occurred externally.

Unverified

The evidence required to promote the claimed outcome has not passed the governed human verification boundary.

Verified

A human verification decision has been explicitly tied to evidence references and hashes. Automation cannot create this state by itself.

Unable to verify

The available evidence does not support promotion to a verified outcome. The uncertainty remains visible.

Absence is not failure. A missing record, failed fetch, incomplete corpus or unextracted document cannot by itself establish that Council failed to act.
Current machinery

What exists now

Acquisition: same-authority Council records can be discovered recursively from Council meeting material and preserved with source identity and hashes.

Extraction: machine-readable Council PDFs can be converted to derived text only after the preserved document passes its acquisition-hash check.

Interpretation: terminology and decision observations retain source-document and derived-text provenance.

Lifecycle: explicit resolution identifiers can connect source observations through time without converting them into findings.

Verification: automated source claims remain unverified until an evidence-bound human decision is applied.

Publication: the public translation gate is deny-by-default. Unverified material does not become a public finding.

The machinery is still being developed. Mechanical integrity does not establish that the Council corpus is complete, that every document has been extracted, or that an outcome is true.

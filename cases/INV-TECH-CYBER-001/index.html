DFAPTI investigation frame · INV-TECH-CYBER-001

Technology Disruption Surge, Cybercriminal Signals and Critical Infrastructure Dependencies

Public explanation: Read what the evidence shows so far, in plain language.

Status: DFAPTI evidence collection is in progress. The evidence register is open; no evidence freeze or causal determination has been authorised.

Investigation purpose

Establish a source-supported account of technology disruptions and cybercriminal signals within the operator-defined scope, examine possible critical infrastructure dependencies, and document consequences for people. Investigate both potential connections and evidence of separate causes.

Central question

What can the evidence establish about the incidents, cybercriminal activity, infrastructure dependencies and human consequences under investigation, and is there a demonstrated connection between any particular events?

Scope

Initial subjects are Microsoft 365, FAA aviation systems, Workday, JPMorgan Chase, Google Security Operations, United Airlines, Australian government services, Australian ports and terminals, and Australian financial infrastructure. These are investigation targets, not findings that incidents occurred or were connected. Establish the relevant dates, locations and incident boundaries from sources. Earlier material may be examined where relevant, without implying a connection.

Questions before evidence collection

  1. What happened in each incident, when and where, and which services and people were affected?
  2. What causes, technical changes and recovery measures have operators reported, and what independent evidence supports or challenges each account?
  3. What malicious campaigns, threat-actor claims, phishing, credential theft or published indicators are relevant?
  4. For each incident, does the evidence establish an outage, exploitation of an outage, a verified compromise, or a proven connection between a compromise and an outage?
  5. Which supplier, cloud, identity, telecommunications, disaster recovery, change-management and alternative-service dependencies can be established?
  6. What consequences for people are documented, including access to healthcare, money, government services, employment, travel, deadlines and recovery?
  7. What evidence supports a connection between particular incidents, and what evidence supports separate explanations?
  8. What remains unknown, contradictory, unavailable or dependent on an unverified allegation?

Source acquisition

Seek government and regulatory records, official incident reports, provider status publications, security advisories, public vulnerability databases, research, public threat intelligence, archived records and authorised commercial sources where available. Preserve original material separately from analysis; record its source, dates, acquisition details, limitations and SHA-256 where obtainable. Log unsuccessful acquisition attempts. Repeated reports based on the same source are not independent corroboration.

Public-source observation

Open MAYHEM Observer to explore saved public-source reports, their provenance and stated limitations. These observations are inputs to this investigation; source claims and automated connections require review and do not become verified case findings automatically.

Cyber intelligence boundaries

Collection is lawful and passive. Anonymous criminal claims remain unverified unless independently corroborated. No unauthorised intrusion, criminal-marketplace transactions, malware execution or direct engagement with threat actors.

Investigation threads

  1. Incident chronology and operator reports
  2. Cybercriminal activity and outage-related phishing
  3. Supplier, cloud, identity and telecommunications dependencies
  4. Change management, failure and recovery
  5. Australian critical infrastructure and public services
  6. Human consequences and access barriers
  7. Contradictions, missing records and alternative explanations
  8. Investigation continuity and record integrity

Method and authority

Distinguish fact, evidence, inference, assumption, opinion, speculation and unknown. Preserve evidence and acquisition boundaries; do not overwrite records or conceal corrections. Keep acquisition separate from causal analysis. Similar timing or technology alone does not prove a common cause. The operator controls the investigation scope and any evidence freeze.

Investigation stages

Stage 1 · DFAPTI evidence workspace — evidence collection is in progress.

Stages 2–6 are locked until an explicit operator-authorised evidence freeze and evidence-supported progression.

Public investigation update · record 03

What the evidence shows about remedies and dependencies

A public institution extended deadlines after an outage, and two artificial-intelligence providers reported problems involving Microsoft services. A later GitHub disruption also affected some Codex activity, according to OpenAI. These are separate, attributed records; they do not establish one cause for every incident.

This account adds to the previous public update. Earlier explanations remain available.

A remedy: two submission deadlines were extended

South Africa's Nelson Mandela Museum issued a notice on 16 November saying a Microsoft system outage or technical challenge on 13 November affected its quotation-closing process. It extended the closing date for two requests for quotations from 13 November to noon on 17 November 2026.

This establishes the remedy the museum announced. The notice does not say whether a person had already missed the original deadline, how many applicants were affected, or whether every submission was ultimately successful.

A documented connection between Microsoft 365 and Claude

Anthropic reported that, between 12 November 16:03 and 13 November 16:40 Coordinated Universal Time, mail, calendar and search tools in its Microsoft 365 connector, and Claude for Outlook, returned errors for many requests because of a Microsoft 365 service incident.

At 21:49 Coordinated Universal Time on 13 November, Anthropic marked its incident resolved but said Microsoft was still completing recovery and occasional errors or delays could remain. That records the provider's own description of the dependency and its recovery boundary; it does not establish the outcome of every failed request.

OpenAI reported a connector incident separately

OpenAI reported that new Microsoft 365 connector connections could fail and tools were not working as expected, followed by monitoring and an operator-reported resolution. Its notice does not itself identify the technical cause or prove that every affected person's work recovered. It should not be treated as independent confirmation of Anthropic's causal explanation.

Another connection: GitHub and Codex

On 25 November, GitHub reported degraded performance across several services. During its response it reported high database load, later said it had changed load distribution, and marked its incident resolved at 16:52 Coordinated Universal Time. Its resolution notice said a detailed root-cause analysis would follow.

OpenAI attributed some Codex automated review and merge failures to a GitHub service disruption. Its notice did not identify the particular GitHub incident above, so the matching times alone do not establish that they are the same event. At its 16:58 Coordinated Universal Time update, OpenAI said GitHub had mitigated the disruption and it was monitoring recovery. OpenAI's monitoring update does not establish that every affected review or merge completed.

What the phishing evidence does—and does not—show

Cofense reported that an outage-themed phishing campaign bypassed three named email-security controls. This is the researcher's account; its report does not specify the number of delivered messages or configurations. It does not by itself show that the outage disabled those protections, that a victim's credentials were captured, or that an account was compromised.

Microsoft separately reported that its protections blocked or quarantined the campaign it described. The reports may concern different samples and times; their statements alone do not prove a contradiction.

What is still not established

  • Whether every deadline, failed review, connector request or affected person's issue was resolved.
  • Whether phishing caused any of these service outages, or resulted in an actual account takeover.
  • A complete technical cause for the GitHub incident, or a common cause linking all the disruptions.
  • The content of sources that refused or disallowed automated retrieval; those access gaps are recorded rather than bypassed.

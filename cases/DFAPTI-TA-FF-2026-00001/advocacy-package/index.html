A referral or silence past 28 days is still worth recording - it's part of the record of how this was handled, not a dead end. Confirmation that the camp has been added to the National Flying-Fox Monitoring Program is the single most important response to watch for - it is the precondition for every grant-related remedy in this case.

Five-Step Primary Source Verification

Every finding in this case traces to a specific evidence register entry. Here's how to check any one of them yourself, using no more than a browser.

Step 1 — Find the reference
Every claim that matters carries an evidence reference like EV-0006. Note it down.
Step 2 — Look it up in the register
Open DFAPTI, go to Evidence Register, and find that ID (or open cases/DFAPTI-TA-FF-2026-00001/data/evidence_register.json directly). Read its source_title, source_location, and evidence_description.
Step 3 — Open the original
Council agenda and minutes items cite a filename but no direct URL - go to nambucca.infocouncil.biz and search by date. Government-policy items mostly cite real, live URLs directly.
Step 4 — Check the claim against the source
Confirm what the evidence entry says the document says is actually what the document says. If it doesn't match, that's a finding in its own right - document it.
Step 5 — Record your check
Note the date you checked, the ID, and whether it held. If you're relying on this case for your own submission, this independent check strengthens it.
Worked example. This repository already did one: EV-0038 and EV-0039 cite DCCEEW's Monitoring Flying-Fox Populations page. Step 3: a direct fetch of dcceew.gov.au timed out. Step 4: falling back to a WebSearch, multiple independent results confirmed the viewer's 25 June 2025 decommissioning and the CSIRO Data Access Portal migration. Step 5: recorded as a classification upgrade to Verified, with the method (search-synthesis, not direct fetch) noted explicitly in each item's investigation_relevance field - a real but weaker form of verification than a direct primary-source fetch, and labelled as such.

If a Document Has Moved, Never Existed Publicly, or Can't Be Found

  • Two sections of council's own reports - the Biosecurity (Noxious Plants) service review (2023/24 Annual Report, page 78) and the Environmental Protection section (State of Our Valley Report 2022-2024, page 27) - were not rendered in the original investigation's PDF fetch. A direct re-fetch or a GIPA request may succeed where the original attempt didn't.
  • The CSIRO Data Access Portal returned a 503 error at the time of the original investigation (EV-0058) - it may be available now; check data.csiro.au/collection/csiro:64370.
  • An inability to access a document is itself worth recording - note the date, the URL, and what you found or didn't.

If There's No Response, or an Inadequate One

This case's own Watchdog Bodies Register (preserved/watchdog-bodies-register-20260616.txt) sets out a recommended order. Use it in sequence, after the primary recipients have had a fair chance to respond.

1. NSW Ombudsman
The primary, most applicable watchdog for Nambucca Valley Council's conduct - requires documented prior Council contact, which the Enforcement Notice or Letter 1 creates once sent.
2. NSW Office of Local Government
Parallel lodgement alongside the Ombudsman.
3. Commonwealth Ombudsman
If the Commonwealth department's response, or non-response, is inadequate.
Do not refer this matter to ICAC on current evidence - the source material's own Watchdog Bodies Register lists it for completeness only and explicitly advises against it. Log every escalation the same way as the original recipients (see Logging Responses).

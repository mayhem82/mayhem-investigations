Part One — What the Record Shows

On 16 March 2023, Vicki Provost formally addressed Nambucca Valley Council at a meeting held in Taylors Arm Hall, describing a flying-fox colony that had more than doubled in size, roosting in Camphor Laurel trees at the rear of residential properties, and causing tank drinking-water contamination, soiled washing, foul odour and vehicle damage. She asked whether council could remove the trees. The minutes record no resolution, referral, or staff direction following her presentation (EV-0001, EV-0002, EV-0006, EV-0007).

What the public record does not show is any response across the following 37 months. Four consecutive Outstanding Actions registers and the most recently retrieved council agenda all contain no entry for this matter (EV-0008, EV-0009, EV-0026, EV-0027). A mayoral transition in October 2024 has left the current council with no formal institutional knowledge the complaint was ever made (EV-0018).

Separately, council's own policy documents confirm a mandatory Biosecurity Act obligation to control Camphor Laurel as a priority weed (EV-0010, EV-0011, EV-0012) - a pathway the adjacent council, Kempsey Shire, successfully navigated in April 2024 via a Review of Environmental Factors (EV-0014). That model has not been applied at Taylors Arm. Nor has the camp been notified to the National Flying-Fox Monitoring Program - a single administrative email that would establish the precondition for grant eligibility (EV-0021, EV-0030, EV-0059).

None of this is presented as concealment or wrongdoing. It is presented as a documented gap between a formally-raised community complaint, the legislative and administrative pathways already available to close it, and 37 months of institutional non-response - a gap only the recipients above can close.

Part Three — Basis for These Remedies

The Biosecurity Act 2015 (NSW) makes council the Local Control Authority for Camphor Laurel, a declared priority weed, with a positive obligation to control its spread (EV-0010, EV-0012, EV-0035). This notice does not ask for a new power to be created - Kempsey Shire Council's April 2024 Review of Environmental Factors process (EV-0014) demonstrates the existing mechanism working.

The National Flying-Fox Monitoring Program, coordinated by CSIRO and DCCEEW, exists specifically to register camps such as this one (EV-0021, EV-0038, EV-0059); registration is the documented precondition for the NSW Flying-Fox Camp Management Grant Program that this same council accessed in 2017 for Gordon Park, Nambucca Heads (EV-0013, EV-0030).

The Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009 (NSW) creates a presumption in favour of disclosure of government information - relevant to the open questions in Part Two regarding council's internal records (DR-001 through DR-004 in the Analysis Register).

Part Four — Remedies Required

Each item below is a specific action or binding commitment, not a request for information.

NVC-1 — Written Acknowledgement
  • Acknowledge the March 2023 complaint in writing and confirm what action, if any, was taken and what is now proposed.
NVC-2 — Camp Registration
  • Notify the Taylors Arm colony to the National Flying-Fox Monitoring Program via CSIRO (david.westcott@csiro.au or adam.mckeown@csiro.au) or speciespolicy@dcceew.gov.au.
NVC-3 — Review of Environmental Factors
  • Commence a Review of Environmental Factors for the Camphor Laurel trees, engaging a qualified ecologist, consistent with the Kempsey Shire Council model of April 2024.
NVC-4 — Grant Application
  • Apply for available flying-fox camp management grant funding once the camp is registered.
NVC-5 — Management Plan
  • Develop a flying-fox camp management plan for the Taylors Arm site in consultation with the community.
NSW-1, NSW-2, NSW-3 — NSW DCCEEW
  • Provide direct support to Council on registration and management planning; confirm current registration status; confirm current grant-program status.
CWLTH-1, CWLTH-2 — Commonwealth DCCEEW
  • Confirm whether the camp is known to the monitoring program and ensure assessment once the program review concludes; confirm the review's current status and how previously-unregistered camps can be included.
DNSW-1, DNSW-2, DNSW-3 — Destination NSW
  • Confirm awareness of the colony's impact on the Visit NSW-listed Pub With No Beer; notify NSW DCCEEW of the tourism interest in resolution; consider whether tourism or regional-development funding could support the Camphor Laurel pathway.

WHAT THIS PACKAGE IS
This package documents a governance failure affecting the village of Taylors Arm, New South Wales. A flying fox colony has been established in the village. A resident formally complained to Nambucca Valley Council in March 2023. The council took no action. No response of any kind has been documented in any council record across the 37 months that followed.
The package contains all documents produced through a full forensic investigation of that failure. It names what happened, what should have happened, what obligations were not met, and what remedy each responsible body is required to provide.
Every finding in this package is grounded in primary source documents — official council minutes, government policy documents, and legislation. No finding is based on inference or opinion. Every source can be independently verified. The five-step verification guide in this package shows you how.
The package is designed to be used by a person with no prior knowledge of the investigation methodology. Read the Plain Language Summary first. Then read the Recipient Guide. Then submit the Enforcement Notice to the bodies named in it.

WHAT HAPPENED — PLAIN STATEMENT
Taylors Arm is a village of 133 people in the Nambucca Valley. It has no reticulated water supply. Residents rely on rainwater tanks. Its main economic asset is the Pub With No Beer, a nationally known tourism destination.
A colony of grey-headed flying foxes established itself in the village. By March 2023 the colony had more than doubled in size. The colony roosted in Camphor Laurel trees at the back of residential properties. Residents experienced contaminated drinking water, soiled washing, vehicle paint damage, foul odour, and loss of outdoor space. The Pub With No Beer and the council-managed campground beside it were commercially at risk.
On 16 March 2023, a resident named Vicki Provost addressed Nambucca Valley Council at a public meeting held in Taylors Arm Hall. She described the infestation and its effects. She asked council whether it could remove the Camphor Laurel trees in which the colony was roosting. The council recorded the presentation in its official minutes. It took no action.
The outstanding actions register — the mechanism by which councils track unresolved matters between meetings — contains no entry for this matter at any meeting from March 2023 to April 2026. The matter does not appear in any annual report or community strategy document in that period. When the mayor changed in October 2024, there was no institutional record to transfer. The current council has no formal knowledge the complaint was ever made.
The Camphor Laurel trees Vicki Provost asked about are a legally mandated weed control target. Council is required by law to manage their spread. The adjacent council — Kempsey Shire — navigated the same regulatory question and removed Camphor Laurels in April 2024 after conducting a formal environmental assessment. Nambucca Valley Council has not initiated that process.
A state government grant program exists to help councils manage flying fox colonies. Access requires the colony to be registered with the Commonwealth monitoring program. The colony at Taylors Arm is not confirmed as registered. Council has not notified the colony to the monitoring program. Without registration, grant access cannot be established.
The same pattern of failure is occurring simultaneously at Bellbrook, in the adjacent Kempsey Shire — same species, same hinterland ecology, same result: no management plan, no grant access, no response. Both communities sit within the same state and federal electorates. Neither their state member nor their federal member has documented public advocacy on the issue for either community.

FRAMING NOTE
Every document in this package describes system behaviour. No document attributes intent, motivation, or personal blame to any individual. The findings describe what the governance architecture produced — not why anyone chose to act or not act. This framing is deliberate. Findings that describe systems rather than accuse individuals are harder to dismiss, harder to deflect, and survive scrutiny that allegation-based framing does not.
The institutions named as recipients in the Enforcement Notice are named because they hold documented obligations — not because any individual within them has done something wrong. The remedy demands are addressed to the obligations, not to the people.

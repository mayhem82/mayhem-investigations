HOW TO USE THIS LETTER

Notifying the Taylors Arm Flying Fox Colony to the National Flying-Fox Monitoring Program

This document contains a ready-to-send letter notifying the Commonwealth and NSW governments of an unregistered flying fox camp at Taylors Arm village, New South Wales. Anyone can send this. No special authority, council approval, or expert knowledge is required.

WHY THIS MATTERS
The grey-headed flying fox is a threatened species. Both the Commonwealth and NSW governments run programs to track where colonies live, so they can monitor population health and help councils manage impacts on communities. A colony at Taylors Arm has been causing serious problems for residents since at least 2023 — contaminated drinking water, soiled washing, vehicle damage, foul odour, and loss of usable outdoor space. Despite a formal complaint to council in March 2023, no action has been taken in over three years.
One of the reasons nothing has happened is that the colony does not appear to be registered with the national monitoring program. Without registration, the council cannot access state government grant funding that exists specifically to help manage situations like this. Registration is the single most important first step that does not depend on the council, the state government, or anyone else taking action first. Any member of the public can do it.
Once the colony is registered, the path opens for grant funding and a proper management plan. (For context: research into this issue found that the neighbouring Kempsey Shire Council successfully removed similar trees from a flying-fox roost in April 2024 by following a formal environmental assessment process — so a workable model already exists nearby, even though it has not yet been applied at Taylors Arm.)

HOW TO SEND IT
Step 1 — Fill in the blank fields
On the letter page that follows, fill in the bracketed fields: your name, your contact details (if you wish to be contactable for follow-up), and the date. Leave any field blank if you prefer not to provide it — the letter still works without your personal details, but including a contact email increases the chance of a useful reply.
Step 2 — Choose your recipient
You can send this letter to any or all of the following: David Westcott at CSIRO (david.westcott@csiro.au), Adam McKeown at CSIRO (adam.mckeown@csiro.au), the Commonwealth Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water (speciespolicy@dcceew.gov.au), or NSW Environment's flying-fox count team (ff.census@environment.nsw.gov.au). Sending to more than one increases the chance of a timely response.
Step 3 — Attach this letter or paste the text
You can either attach this Word document to your email, or copy and paste the letter text into the body of your email. Both are acceptable. If attaching, save the document with your name in the file title, for example "Taylors-Arm-Flying-Fox-Notification-[YourName].docx".
Step 4 — Send it
Use your own email account. Write a short subject line such as "Notification of unregistered flying-fox camp — Taylors Arm, NSW". Send the email. That is the entire action required.
Step 5 — Keep a copy
Save a copy of the email you sent and note the date. If you receive a reply, keep that too. This record may be useful later if you wish to follow up or if the matter is raised with council or government at a later date.

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT
CSIRO and the relevant government departments coordinate the National Flying-Fox Monitoring Program and maintain the database of known camp locations. Once notified, they may add the Taylors Arm camp to their records, which is the formal first step in establishing eligibility for council grant applications. A reply is not guaranteed and may take time given the program is currently under review, but the notification itself is recorded the moment you send it.

Subject: Notification of unregistered flying-fox camp — Taylors Arm, New South Wales

Dear Dr Westcott and Mr McKeown,
I am writing to notify you of a flying-fox camp that I believe may not currently be registered with the National Flying-Fox Monitoring Program, so that it can be assessed for inclusion in your records.
Location
The camp is located in and around the village of Taylors Arm, in the Nambucca Valley Local Government Area, New South Wales. Taylors Arm is approximately 27 kilometres west of Macksville, on Taylors Arm Road. The colony is roosting in Camphor Laurel trees (Cinnamomum camphora) at the rear of residential properties within the village.
Species
The colony is understood to be grey-headed flying foxes (Pteropus poliocephalus), a species listed as Vulnerable under the Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 (Commonwealth) and under New South Wales legislation.
Duration and size
The colony has been present in the village since at least early 2023, and was formally raised at the Nambucca Valley Council Ordinary Meeting on 16 March 2023, where it was described in the official council minutes as having "more than doubled" in size that year. I am not aware of any subsequent public record of the colony's current size, but the available evidence indicates it has been an established presence in the village for at least three years.
Community impact
The presence of the colony has been associated with significant impacts on the Taylors Arm community, as documented in the official council minutes of 16 March 2023. These include contamination of rainwater drinking tank supplies (the village has no reticulated water supply), soiling of washing left outdoors, damage to vehicle paintwork, foul odour, and loss of usable outdoor space for residents.
Why I am raising this
Despite the matter being formally raised with Nambucca Valley Council in March 2023, no record of any council action, referral, or response appears in any subsequent council document I have been able to locate, including outstanding actions registers from May 2023, November 2024, and June 2025, and the council's own annual reports. I understand that access to relevant state flying-fox camp management grant funding may require the camp to be formally registered with the National Flying-Fox Monitoring Program. I am raising this matter in the hope that registering the camp will help open a pathway toward a properly resourced management response for the affected community.
Request
I would be grateful if you could:
1. Confirm whether the Taylors Arm camp is currently included in your records or those of the National Flying-Fox Monitoring Program.
2. If it is not currently included, advise what steps are needed to have it assessed for inclusion.
3. Advise whether there is a local or regional contact who could assist with providing further detail, such as a more precise location, estimated colony size, or photographic record, if that would assist your assessment.
I am happy to provide any further information that would assist, and I am glad to be contacted at the email address above if that is useful. Thank you for your time and for the work the monitoring program does.
Yours sincerely,

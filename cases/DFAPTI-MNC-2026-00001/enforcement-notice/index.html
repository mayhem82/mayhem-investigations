Part One — What the Record Shows

NSW Police's PolAir-Remote is a genuine, operationally deployed remote-drone capability that commenced trial in Moree on 8 January 2026, and independently documented tactical successes exist (EV-0002, EV-0012). Kempsey's underlying community-safety concerns are also real and independently documented, predating this program by years (EV-0003, EV-0004, EV-0029). Neither of those facts is in dispute.

What the public record does not show is any of the specific claims used to justify expanding this program to the Mid North Coast. No complete NSW Police evaluation of the six-month Moree trial has been publicly located, despite the trial having continued operating for at least seven months past its own stated window (EV-0037, EV-0782). No primary source has been located for the petition's headline claims of a 50% crime reduction, 100+ arrests, 70+ stolen vehicles recovered, six missing people located, 30-60 second deployment, or cost "a fraction" of a police helicopter (EV-0002, EV-0012, EV-0090, EV-0342). No controlling operational policy governing launch authority, proactive patrol, or recording has been publicly located, despite NSW Police having told Parliament that RPAS use must comply with such policies (EV-0021). No public stop, failure, or decommissioning criteria exist for the program (EV-0042).

None of this is presented as concealment or wrongdoing. It is presented as a gap between what a public petition is asking the NSW Government to expand and what has actually been made available for the public, or this investigation, to independently verify — a gap only the recipients above can close.

Part Three — Basis for These Requests

The Law Enforcement Conduct Commission Act 2016 (NSW) s 35 gives any person standing to complain about NSW Police Force conduct that is or could be agency maladministration, and expressly directs the Commission to examine the effectiveness and appropriateness of police procedures concerning legality and propriety (EV-0026). A systemic governance gap — an operational capability expanding without public evaluation, operational policy, or exit criteria — falls within that jurisdiction; it is not confined to a complaint about a named officer's conduct.

The Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009 (NSW) creates a presumption in favour of disclosure of government information, subject to defined public-interest exemptions (EV-0034, EV-0782 onward). The six GIPA applications already drafted in this case's evidence register apply that Act directly to NSW Police InfoLink; this notice separately puts the underlying governance concern to the Commissioner and the Minister, since a GIPA application produces documents but does not itself ask the institution to account for why those documents are not already public.

The Minister for Police and Counter-terrorism holds ministerial responsibility for NSW Police Force policy and publicly committed, on 3 June 2026, to assessing the Moree trial's results before determining next steps (EV-0010) — a commitment this notice asks be honoured in writing before any Mid North Coast expansion decision is made public. The NSW Information and Privacy Commission's role concerns the GIPA process itself and any applicable AI-governance framework, which is why it is included as a recipient rather than NSW Police alone.

It does not ask these bodies to explain themselves; it tells each of them what must happen — evaluation published, operational policy published, exit criteria published, no expansion decision made in their absence — before this program goes any further. Part One — What the Record Shows

NSW Police's PolAir-Remote is a genuine, operationally deployed remote-drone capability that commenced trial in Moree on 8 January 2026, and independently documented tactical successes exist (EV-0002, EV-0012). Kempsey's underlying community-safety concerns are also real and independently documented, predating this program by years (EV-0003, EV-0004, EV-0029). Neither of those facts is in dispute.

What the public record does not show is any of the specific claims used to justify expanding this program to the Mid North Coast. No complete NSW Police evaluation of the six-month Moree trial has been publicly located, despite the trial having continued operating for at least seven months past its own stated window (EV-0037, EV-0782). No primary source has been located for the petition's headline claims of a 50% crime reduction, 100+ arrests, 70+ stolen vehicles recovered, six missing people located, 30-60 second deployment, or cost "a fraction" of a police helicopter (EV-0002, EV-0012, EV-0090, EV-0342). No controlling operational policy governing launch authority, proactive patrol, or recording has been publicly located, despite NSW Police having told Parliament that RPAS use must comply with such policies (EV-0021). No public stop, failure, or decommissioning criteria exist for the program (EV-0042).

None of this is presented as concealment or wrongdoing. It is presented as a gap between what a public petition is asking the NSW Government to expand and what has actually been made available for the public, or this investigation, to independently verify — a gap only the recipients above can close.

Part Three — Basis for These Remedies

The Law Enforcement Conduct Commission Act 2016 (NSW) s 35 gives any person standing to complain about NSW Police Force conduct that is or could be agency maladministration, and expressly directs the Commission to examine the effectiveness and appropriateness of police procedures concerning legality and propriety (EV-0026). A systemic governance gap — an operational capability expanding without public evaluation, operational policy, or exit criteria — falls within that jurisdiction and empowers LECC to require it be fixed, not merely explained.

The Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009 (NSW) creates a presumption in favour of disclosure of government information, subject to defined public-interest exemptions (EV-0034, EV-0782 onward). The six GIPA applications already drafted in this case's evidence register apply that Act directly to NSW Police InfoLink to obtain the underlying documents; this notice goes further and puts the governance question itself to the Commissioner and the Minister — obtaining the documents does not by itself stop a decision being made ahead of them, so the remedy has to be a precondition on the decision, not just disclosure of the paperwork.

The Minister for Police and Counter-terrorism holds ministerial responsibility for NSW Police Force policy and publicly committed, on 3 June 2026, to assessing the Moree trial's results before determining next steps (EV-0010). That commitment is currently a public statement with no binding force; the remedy sought converts it into a written precondition the Minister can be held to. The NSW Information and Privacy Commission has the statutory authority to determine whether an AI Assessment Framework gate applies here and has been skipped — a question no other recipient can settle, which is why it is asked for a ruling rather than copied for information.

Part Four — Remedies Required

Each item below is a specific action or binding commitment, not a request for information. Where a document is also needed to verify compliance, that document is named — but the remedy is the commitment itself, not the paperwork.

Required of the Commissioner of Police
  • Do not authorise, fund, or publicly recommend any Mid North Coast PolAir-Remote deployment until the complete Moree trial evaluation is published in full.
  • Do not deploy any RPAS capability procured under EOI YR-2026-0058 to a new location until a public operational policy governing launch authority, proactive patrol, recording, and retention is published.
  • Publicly commit that no facial-recognition or biometric-matching function will be applied to PolAir-Remote imagery without prior public disclosure and any legally required AI Assessment Framework determination.
  • Publish binding stop, failure, and decommissioning criteria for PolAir-Remote before further public money is committed to expansion.
Required of the Minister for Police and Counter-terrorism
  • Publicly confirm that no Mid North Coast expansion decision — site selection, funding commitment, or procurement award — will be made or announced until the Moree evaluation is published and open to independent scrutiny.
  • Direct NSW Police to make publication of the controlling operational policy a precondition of any further public funding for expansion.
Required of the Law Enforcement Conduct Commission
  • Open, or confirm it has opened, a systemic examination into whether deploying and preparing to expand a surveillance-capable capability without a public evaluation, operational policy, or exit criteria is a governance deficiency within LECC's Act 2016 examination function, and publish its findings.
Required of the NSW Information and Privacy Commission
  • Confirm publicly whether an AI Assessment Framework determination is legally required for PolAir-Remote given its imagery-analytics capability, and if so, confirm whether NSW Police has been directed to complete one before further deployment.

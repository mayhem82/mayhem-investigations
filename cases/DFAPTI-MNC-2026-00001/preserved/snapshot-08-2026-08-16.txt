
MAYHEM - MNC POLAIR-REMOTE DEEP RESEARCH
SNAPSHOT 8
Delta snapshot - 16 August 2026 - Findings F-141-F-160
Exact predecessor: MAYHEM-MNC-POLAIR-REMOTE-DEEP-RESEARCH-SNAPSHOT-7-2026-08-16.docx
Scope: evidence integrity, courtroom use, command-and-control, human factors, operational resilience, emergency-airspace interaction and civil-liability branches. Earlier findings are incorporated by exact predecessor reference and are not restated here.
Classification rule
FACT/EVIDENCE = directly supported by located primary or authoritative material. INFERENCE = analytical consequence drawn from established evidence. UNKNOWN = a material point not established by the public record located as at this snapshot.
F-141 - THE PLATFORM IS TECHNICALLY CAPABLE OF PRODUCING AN AUDITABLE FLIGHT RECORD
FACT/EVIDENCE. DJI FlightHub 2 states that manual Dock flights and remote-controlled missions can be fully logged, including drone status, key actions and user operations, and that flight summaries and detailed logs can be exported. This means an assertion that dock-based remote operations are inherently incapable of producing auditable mission records would be technically incorrect. Whether NSW Police enables, preserves and discloses the full available record remains UNKNOWN.
F-142 - CASA REQUIRES A SEVEN-YEAR AVIATION RECORD EVEN IF VIDEO IS NOT ORDINARILY RECORDED
FACT/EVIDENCE. CASA ReOC record-keeping requirements require operational releases, operational logs and remote-pilot logs to be kept for seven years. Required fields include the nature and purpose of the operation, aircraft/model, remote pilot station, date/time, operating locations, maximum authorised height, pilot/crew identities, VLOS/EVLOS/BVLOS status, CASA approvals, serviceability, launch/landing coordinates, relevant flight segments and heights, and unserviceability. Therefore the public statement that PolAir-Remote flights are not ordinarily recorded as video does not imply that no auditable aviation record exists.
F-143 - DRONE-DERIVED MATERIAL CAN FALL WITHIN NSW CRIMINAL DISCLOSURE DUTIES
FACT/EVIDENCE. Section 36B of the Criminal Procedure Act 1986 requires law-enforcement or investigating officers to disclose to prosecutors all relevant information, documents or other things obtained during an investigation that might reasonably assist either the prosecution or the accused, and to retain such material while that duty continues. Comparable duties apply where the DPP is exercising relevant functions. INFERENCE: where PolAir imagery, telemetry, operator notes, logs or associated records are relevant to a prosecution or defence, the fact that they originated in a drone system does not remove them from the disclosure architecture.
F-144 - NO PUBLIC POLAIR-REMOTE EVIDENCE-HANDLING / CHAIN-OF-CUSTODY PROTOCOL HAS BEEN LOCATED
UNKNOWN / NEGATIVE FINDING. Public sources establish general NSW criminal-disclosure duties and CASA operational records, but no public PolAir-Remote-specific rule has been located defining when imagery must be recorded, evidentiary export format, hash or integrity controls, metadata preservation, who may edit or annotate footage, continuity certification, or how raw source files are provided for criminal proceedings. This does not prove such an internal protocol does not exist.
F-145 - SOFTWARE AND FIRMWARE VERSION SHOULD BE TREATED AS A FORENSIC ARTIFACT
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. DJI continues to issue Dock 3 / Matrice 4D-series firmware and FlightHub 2 updates, and the platform supports detailed operational logging. Where system behaviour, automated tracking, alerts, geolocation, image processing or mission logic becomes disputed, the software/firmware/configuration version operating at the time may affect reproducibility. A defensible evidence chain should therefore preserve relevant version/configuration information, not merely exported video.
F-146 - AVIATION COMMAND IS THE CENTRAL RPAS CONTROL POINT, BUT LOCAL COMMANDS CAN REQUEST SUPPORT
FACT/EVIDENCE. NSW Police told Parliament in 2022 that RPAS are typically centrally held and managed by Aviation Command, that individual commands may request RPAS assistance, and that planned use must comply with law, the agency Operator's Certificate and relevant NSW Police Force policies and procedures. This establishes a central-management/local-request architecture.
F-147 - THE POLICING AUTHORISATION LAYER REMAINS PUBLICLY OPAQUE
UNKNOWN. The public record located does not identify the exact role or rank that may authorise a proactive PolAir-Remote patrol, extended tracking, sensor escalation, recording, following a subject across district boundaries, or terminating a mission for proportionality/privacy reasons. CASA aviation approval answers whether and how an aircraft may safely operate; it does not itself answer why police may target a particular person/place. The unpublished NSW Police policy/procedure layer remains the decisive missing control.
F-148 - RPAS HUMAN-FACTORS GOVERNANCE IS STILL EVOLVING AT REGULATOR LEVEL
FACT/EVIDENCE. CASA's RPAS sector planning identifies ongoing work around complex operations, BVLOS training and human-factors/safety-management standards. This establishes that automation and remote flight do not remove human-factors risk. It does not establish any deficiency in NSW Police training. Police pilots are confirmed to receive specialist training; the content and competency standards for remote identification/interpretation decisions remain UNKNOWN.
F-149 - REMOTE VISUAL IDENTIFICATION AND GROUND INTERVENTION REQUIRE A VERIFICATION RULE THAT IS NOT PUBLIC
INFERENCE + UNKNOWN. PolAir-Remote separates the observer/pilot from the local officers who act on the information. A remote operator may identify or track a person/vehicle and relay that intelligence to ground police. No public NSW Police protocol has been located specifying what corroboration is required before consequential action where identification depends on zoom, thermal imagery, partial visual cues or automated detection/tracking. This is a human-factors and procedural-fairness gap, not evidence that a misidentification has occurred.
F-150 - EQUALITY OF ARMS MAY REQUIRE ACCESS TO MORE THAN THE EXPORTED CLIP
INFERENCE FROM VERIFIED DISCLOSURE AND LOGGING CAPABILITY. If the reliability of PolAir-derived evidence is disputed, a defence may need more than a selected video segment: relevant flight path, timestamps, user actions, original media, mission logs, sensor state, software/configuration information and operator records may bear on reliability. NSW disclosure law can encompass relevant material that assists the accused. Whether NSW Police routinely preserves/provides these specific PolAir artefacts is UNKNOWN.
F-151 - DOCK 3 DEPENDS ON STABLE POWER AND INTERNET; ITS BACKUP BATTERY DOES NOT CHARGE THE AIRCRAFT
FACT/EVIDENCE. DJI specifies that Dock 3 should be installed in an open environment with stable power supply and internet. The built-in backup battery can support the dock, but does not charge the aircraft. This creates a distinction between keeping dock electronics alive temporarily and sustaining repeated flight operations through an extended power outage.
F-152 - REMOTE-AIRCRAFT AVAILABILITY HAS A MULTI-LAYER FAILURE CHAIN
INFERENCE. Operational availability depends on aircraft serviceability and battery, dock power/thermal systems, network connectivity, positioning/navigation, video/control link, remote operations infrastructure and the local airspace/weather environment. Failure of any one layer can reduce or terminate the remote capability even where other layers remain functional. Public Moree performance reporting has not located an aggregate measure of aborted missions, lost links, downtime, recharge delays or unavailable-tasking events.
F-153 - FLYAWAY, COMPASS, DATA-LINK AND RETURN-TO-HOME FAILURES ARE REAL RPAS HAZARDS, NOT PROOF DOCK 3 IS UNSAFE
FACT/EVIDENCE. ATSB investigated a 2021 DJI Inspire 2 flyaway at Darling Harbour in which compass failure following electromagnetic interference rendered the aircraft unresponsive, disabled the relevant return-to-home safeguard and resulted in collision with a hotel window and minor injury. ATSB stated reported RPA flyaways are not rare. This was a different aircraft and operating context. It establishes real failure modes in the RPAS sector; it does not establish a defect in the Matrice 4TD/Dock 3 used at Moree.
F-154 - EMERGENCY AIRSPACE REQUIRES ACTIVE DECONFLICTION
FACT/EVIDENCE. CASA warns that unauthorised drone operation near bushfire, flood, rescue, police or other emergency aviation can force crewed aircraft to stop operating because of collision risk. Police RPAS can operate under agency approvals/exemptions, but this does not create a general public rule that a police drone automatically has priority over firefighting, rescue or aeromedical aircraft. The specific NSW inter-agency deconfliction protocol applicable to PolAir-Remote has not been located.
F-155 - FIRST-RESPONDER BVLOS GOVERNANCE IS AN ACTIVE REGULATORY AREA
FACT/EVIDENCE. CASA continues to develop and maintain regulatory mechanisms supporting emergency-service and first-responder RPAS, including BVLOS operations subject to conditions. This confirms that remote regional policing is operating inside an actively evolving aviation-governance environment rather than a static rule set.
F-156 - CYBERSECURITY IS ALSO AN AVIATION-SAFETY ISSUE
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. Remote operations depend on digital control, communications, navigation, software and data links. CASA has issued RPAS-related airworthiness cybersecurity guidance, while Dock 3/FlightHub infrastructure relies on networked systems. A cyber or communications failure can therefore affect not only confidentiality of police information but availability, command integrity and safe aircraft behaviour. NSW Police's PolAir-specific cyber-resilience architecture remains UNKNOWN.
F-157 - A RESILIENCE PLAN MUST INCLUDE A NON-DRONE FALLBACK
INFERENCE. Because PolAir-Remote can become unavailable through weather, airspace conflict, power/network failure, aircraft serviceability or mission saturation, a regional policing model cannot safely treat it as the sole response capability. The relevant performance question is whether the system degrades gracefully to conventional PolAir, ground units or other search assets. No public Moree evaluation located publishes fallback activation frequency or service-availability metrics.
F-158 - POLICE TORT CLAIMS GENERALLY ROUTE THROUGH THE NSW CROWN
FACT/EVIDENCE. Under the Law Reform (Vicarious Liability) Act 1983, a police officer is treated as a person in the service of the Crown; the Crown is vicariously liable in defined circumstances, and police tort claims for conduct in the performance or purported performance of police functions are generally made against the Crown rather than directly against the officer. This provides the basic civil-liability route for alleged wrongful police conduct involving a drone.
F-159 - VENDOR OR OPERATOR LIABILITY CANNOT BE INFERRED FROM A FAILURE ALONE
FACT/EVIDENCE + LIMITATION. NSW law does not prevent claims against another non-police person who is jointly or severally liable, but liability would depend on the actual cause, fault, contract, product defect and other law. A crash, lost link or incorrect police decision would not by itself establish DJI, an integrator, a network provider, the remote operator or the Crown as legally liable without evidence of causation and applicable duty.
F-160 - SAFETY AND RELIABILITY MUST BE PART OF THE TRIAL OUTCOME DATA
INFERENCE. A credible PolAir-Remote evaluation should report total missions, successful tasking rate, aborted missions, weather cancellations, network/control-link interruptions, return-to-home activations, navigation/positioning anomalies, aircraft/dock unserviceability, emergency deconfliction events, crashes/near misses, maintenance downtime and median recovery time. Public Moree success reporting located to date focuses heavily on arrests/recoveries and does not provide this system-reliability denominator.
Block-level conclusion
The evidence/authority and failure/safety branches establish an important asymmetry. The technical and aviation systems are capable of producing detailed audit records and CASA mandates substantial seven-year flight record keeping. General criminal-disclosure law also creates a pathway for relevant drone-derived material to enter prosecution and defence disclosure. What remains least visible is the police-specific decision layer: tasking authority, verification standards, evidence-export protocol, sensor-use rules and mission termination criteria. Separately, RPAS failure modes are empirically real and civil-liability mechanisms exist, but no evidence currently establishes a Moree crash, near miss, wrongful identification or system failure.

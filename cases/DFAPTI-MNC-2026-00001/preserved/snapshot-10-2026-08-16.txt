
MAYHEM - MNC POLAIR-REMOTE DEEP RESEARCH
SNAPSHOT 10
Delta snapshot - 16 August 2026 - Findings F-181-F-200
Exact predecessor: MAYHEM-MNC-POLAIR-REMOTE-DEEP-RESEARCH-SNAPSHOT-9-2026-08-16.docx
Scope: infrastructure siting and physical vulnerability, lifecycle/vendor dependency, alternative capability, aviation incident history, international governance/failure comparators, political/vendor influence provenance, and counterfactual Kempsey/Mid North Coast deployment architecture.
Classification rule
FACT/EVIDENCE = directly supported by located primary or authoritative material. INFERENCE = analytical consequence drawn from established evidence. UNKNOWN = a material point not established by the public record located as at this snapshot.
F-181 - DOCK SITING IS AN OPERATIONAL-ENGINEERING DECISION, NOT A SIMPLE ROOFTOP COPY
FACT/EVIDENCE. DJI specifies that Dock 3 requires an open environment, stable power and internet, and operation within defined wind/rainfall limits. The platform also depends on positioning, safe take-off/landing geometry and communications. Therefore a Kempsey installation cannot responsibly be selected merely because a police-station roof is available. Site evaluation must include airspace, terrain/obstructions, communications, power resilience, weather exposure, maintenance access and the incident geography it is intended to serve.
F-182 - MACLEAY FLOOD AND SERVICE INTERRUPTION RISK BELONGS IN SITE SELECTION
FACT/EVIDENCE + LIMITATION. Kempsey Shire emergency information recognises that storms and floods can interrupt electricity, phones and internet in affected areas. Dock 3 requires stable power/internet, and its backup battery does not charge the aircraft. This makes flood/service resilience a legitimate site-design factor. No site-specific conclusion is made here that West Kempsey Police Station or any other candidate dock location is itself flood-prone; that requires property-level assessment.
F-183 - ONE FIXED DOCK CANNOT CONSTITUTE A MID NORTH COAST RAPID-RESPONSE NETWORK
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. The Matrice 4D-series/Dock 3 architecture has a finite operational radius and endurance; prior manufacturer material examined in this investigation gives a maximum test operating radius around 10 km and flight time up to 54 minutes under specified conditions. Mid North Coast Police District spans a very large geography from the Upper Macleay through the coast and south toward Port Macquarie. A single Kempsey node is therefore a local aerial-response asset, not district-wide rapid coverage.
F-184 - REGIONAL COVERAGE REQUIRES A MULTI-NODE, MOBILE OR MIXED ARCHITECTURE
FACT/EVIDENCE. Dock 3 supports vehicle-mounted deployment and multi-dock tasking. One dock supports one aircraft; it does not support multiple drones, while one drone can operate with multiple docks in supported configurations. Reference-condition recharge from 15% to 95% takes about 27 minutes and there is no automatic battery swap. INFERENCE: regional design must account for node density, concurrent demand, recharge downtime and mobile deployment, rather than assume uninterrupted one-aircraft coverage.
F-185 - KEMPSEY ALREADY HAS A SUBSTANTIAL FIXED-CAMERA BASELINE
FACT/EVIDENCE. Kempsey Shire Council has reported operating more than 100 CCTV cameras and providing footage to NSW Police several times per month, with permanent coverage in multiple public locations. Therefore a PolAir proposal must identify the incremental capability deficit left by fixed cameras - for example mobile tracking, thermal/night search, coverage beyond camera fields, rapid incident assessment or missing-person search - rather than compare drones against a zero-surveillance baseline.
F-186 - THE ALTERNATIVE SET IS COMPLEMENTARY, NOT ONE-FOR-ONE
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. NSW Police already operates conventional PolAir helicopters/fixed-wing aircraft with FLIR, high-definition video and live downlink; it also has a long-running Mobile Automatic Number Plate Recognition capability, including a current seven-year MANPR contract, while Kempsey Council supplies extensive fixed CCTV. Ground officers remain necessary for physical intervention. These assets solve different problems. A valid procurement counterfactual should compare combinations of fixed CCTV, MANPR/vehicle detection, mobile patrol capacity, conventional PolAir and remote drones rather than ask only 'drone or no drone'.
F-187 - DJI FLIGHTHUB INTEROPERABILITY CREATES A POTENTIAL SWITCHING-COST PATH
FACT/EVIDENCE. DJI FlightHub 2 supports DJI docks/aircraft and explicitly does not connect drones from other manufacturers, although it can integrate some third-party algorithms. Dock 3 itself only supports the Matrice 4D series. INFERENCE: if NSW Police adopts this stack deeply for remote operations, workflows, data, training and automation, future migration to another aircraft/vendor ecosystem can carry non-trivial switching costs. The actual NSW Police backend architecture remains UNKNOWN, so existing vendor lock-in is not asserted.
F-188 - LIFECYCLE GOVERNANCE SHOULD INCLUDE MIGRATION AND DECOMMISSIONING FROM THE START
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. FlightHub 2 is available as public-cloud or on-premises infrastructure; DJI continues firmware/software updates and ecosystem changes. Long-lived police capability therefore requires explicit policy for firmware support, battery/aircraft replacement, software support, data export, archival formats, credential revocation, replacement-vendor migration and secure decommissioning. No public PolAir-Remote lifecycle/migration plan has been located.
F-189 - AUSTRALIAN RPAS INCIDENT HISTORY ESTABLISHES NON-ZERO FAILURE RISK, NOT MOREE FAILURE
FACT/EVIDENCE. ATSB's 2021 Darling Harbour investigation documented a DJI Inspire 2 flyaway following compass failure/electromagnetic interference, loss of control and collision, and noted that reported RPA flyaways were not rare. ATSB is also investigating a May 2026 Sydney Harbour event in which 89 RPAS from a large swarm were lost following collisions with water/terrain. These systems and contexts differ from Moree PolAir-Remote. No Moree/NSW Police Dock 3 crash or equivalent incident has been located.
F-190 - A MATURE POLICE DFR PROGRAM CAN PUBLISH FLIGHT HISTORY AND EXPLICIT OPERATIONAL LIMITS
FACT/EVIDENCE. Chula Vista Police Department publicly provides drone-activity dashboards and flight-history maps, states that every flight is tracked for transparency, and its policy prohibits general surveillance/general patrol. It also publicly states that operators may not intentionally record/transmit places where a person has a reasonable expectation of privacy except under warrant or emergency conditions. This is a comparator, not NSW law.
F-191 - PUBLIC-RECORD ACCESS TO POLICE-DRONE FOOTAGE CAN CREATE REAL PRIVACY AND REDACTION BURDENS
FACT/EVIDENCE. Litigation over Chula Vista drone records challenged a categorical refusal to provide a large requested set of DFR footage. The California appellate decision rejected treating all footage as automatically exempt merely because it was collected by police, while recognising the substantial privacy/redaction issues posed by aerial footage, including private-property imagery. This is not binding in NSW; it demonstrates an operational governance burden that emerges once a high-volume drone program creates large records holdings.
F-192 - THE BALTIMORE PERSISTENT-AERIAL-SURVEILLANCE CASE SHOWS THAT RETENTION PRACTICE CAN DIVERGE FROM POLICY
FACT/EVIDENCE. In Leaders of a Beautiful Struggle v Baltimore Police Department, the US Fourth Circuit considered a persistent wide-area aerial-surveillance program and held the warrantless program unconstitutional under the US Fourth Amendment framework. The record also documented that retention implementation did not initially match the stated 45-day rule, with more data kept due to technical/operational issues before later deletion and case-linked preservation. It was not a drone program and US constitutional law does not govern NSW. The transferable lesson is that written retention promises require implementation audit.
F-193 - STRONGER EXPLICIT TECHNOLOGY-GOVERNANCE GATES ARE ADMINISTRATIVELY POSSIBLE
FACT/EVIDENCE. New Zealand Police has a two-stage governance/oversight policy that applies before new policing technology is trialled/adopted and also when significant extra functionality is added to existing technology. UK ICO law-enforcement guidance requires DPIAs before high-risk/new-surveillance processing and gives a police-drone example in which assessment occurs before purchase/use; unresolved high residual risk requires regulator consultation. US DOJ policy confines UAS to authorised activities, bars sole monitoring of First-Amendment-protected activity, requires training/supervision and annual privacy reviews. These are foreign comparators, not NSW legal requirements.
F-194 - DJI HAS A DOCUMENTED NSW LOBBYING FOOTPRINT
FACT/EVIDENCE. The NSW Electoral Commission lobbyist register contains 'DJI' among listed client records associated with an active registered lobbyist and separately 'DJI TECHNOLOGY AUSTRALIA PTY. LTD.' among another active lobbyist's listed client records. Electoral Commission guidance defines a client as a third party that has retained/provided lobbying services directly or indirectly. Because the register view aggregates current and historical client information, this establishes a NSW lobbying footprint but not the precise dates or subject matter of each engagement.
F-195 - NO EVIDENCE LINKS THE DJI LOBBYING FOOTPRINT TO MOREE POLAIR-REMOTE OR KEMP'S CAMPAIGN
UNKNOWN / NEGATIVE FINDING. No evidence located connects the registered DJI lobbying footprint to the Moree trial, Michael Kemp, Yasmin Catley, the RPAS EOI, a particular ministerial meeting, procurement outcome or policy decision. The correct classification is: lobbying presence VERIFIED; influence over PolAir-Remote UNKNOWN / NOT ESTABLISHED.
F-196 - AUSTRALIAN DRONE-INTEGRATION VENDORS DEMONSTRATE MARKET CAPABILITY, NOT A MOREE SUPPLIER RELATIONSHIP
FACT/EVIDENCE + LIMITATION. Buy NSW material identifies Australian suppliers capable of autonomous drone-in-a-box/BVLOS/remote-operations integration, including DJI-compatible systems and Australian-hosted data options. This demonstrates that alternatives/integration capability exist in the market. No evidence located establishes that Sphere Communications or another named example vendor supplied Moree, responded to the RPAS EOI or has been selected by NSW Police.
F-197 - A WEST-KEMPSEY FIXED NODE WOULD BE A LOCAL NODE, NOT A DISTRICT NODE
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. Mid North Coast Police District includes widely separated coastal and inland communities. Distances from Kempsey to other major district centres are many multiples of a roughly 10 km dock operating-radius benchmark. Therefore a West Kempsey installation could improve Kempsey-local observation but could not provide the petition's apparent 'Mid North Coast' rapid-response effect by itself.
F-198 - THE NUMBER AND LOCATION OF NODES CANNOT BE RESPONSIBLY SPECIFIED FROM POLITICAL GEOGRAPHY ALONE
INFERENCE. Correct network design requires geocoded calls-for-service/offence demand, time-critical incident categories, existing CCTV/MANPR coverage, ground-response times, terrain/obstruction and radio/network modelling, CASA operating approvals, emergency-airspace interaction, flood/power resilience, maintenance/recharge constraints and acceptable response-time thresholds. Without those inputs, naming an exact number of docks would be false precision.
F-199 - VEHICLE-MOUNTED AND MIXED DEPLOYMENT MEANS 'PUT A DOCK ON EACH POLICE STATION' IS NOT THE ONLY ARCHITECTURE
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. Dock 3 supports vehicle-mounted deployment, while conventional PolAir and local ground assets already exist. A Mid North Coast model could therefore use fixed high-demand nodes, mobile docks/aircraft for temporary coverage, conventional aviation for long-range/high-end tasks and ground/CCTV/MANPR systems for other incidents. The optimal architecture remains UNKNOWN until comparative demand and cost data are available.
F-200 - ALL TWENTY FIRST-ORDER UNTOUCHED PATHS HAVE NOW BEEN OPENED; THE NEXT PHASE SHOULD BE SYNTHESIS, NOT PATH PROLIFERATION
ANALYTICAL GATE. Evidence integrity/courtroom use, human factors, command authority, communications/resilience, emergency airspace, liability, defence equality, records/GIPA, inter-agency use, children's rights, Indigenous data governance, victim outcomes, siting, lifecycle dependency, alternatives, chilling effects, incident history, international comparators, political/vendor influence and counterfactual Kempsey architecture have now each been investigated at first order. Remaining work should consolidate these into a pre-rollout decision architecture: required evidence, mandatory safeguards, unresolved unknowns and explicit go/hold/no-go gates.
Block-level conclusion
The lifecycle/alternatives block shows that a Mid North Coast rollout is an architecture decision, not simply the replication of Moree. A fixed dock has finite local coverage, depends on resilient site infrastructure and sits inside a broader mix of CCTV, MANPR, ground policing and conventional PolAir. A DJI-centred software/hardware stack can create switching costs if adopted deeply, but the actual NSW Police backend has not been established. International comparators show that strong transparency, privacy and feature-change gates are administratively possible. DJI has a documented NSW lobbying footprint, but no evidence links that footprint to Moree, Kemp or the NSW Police procurement.


MAYHEM - MNC POLAIR-REMOTE DEEP RESEARCH
SNAPSHOT 9
Delta snapshot - 16 August 2026 - Findings F-161-F-180
Exact predecessor: MAYHEM-MNC-POLAIR-REMOTE-DEEP-RESEARCH-SNAPSHOT-8-2026-08-16.docx
Scope: records retention/deletion and GIPA, secondary/inter-agency use, child-rights architecture, Indigenous data governance, victim outcomes and lawful-activity chilling effects. Earlier findings are incorporated by exact predecessor reference.
Classification rule
FACT/EVIDENCE = directly supported by located primary or authoritative material. INFERENCE = analytical consequence drawn from established evidence. UNKNOWN = a material point not established by the public record located as at this snapshot.
F-161 - VIDEO RETENTION AND AVIATION-RECORD RETENTION ARE DIFFERENT QUESTIONS
FACT/EVIDENCE. NSW Police has publicly said PolAir-Remote flights are not ordinarily recorded unless responding to crime, while CASA requires substantial operational releases/logs and remote-pilot records to be retained for seven years. State Records law separately restricts unauthorised abandonment, disposal, transfer, damage or alteration of State records. Therefore 'no recorded video' does not equal 'no government record of the flight'.
F-162 - GIPA CAN TARGET THE AUDIT TRAIL EVEN WHEN IMAGERY DOES NOT EXIST
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. NSW Police accepts formal GIPA applications, normally with a 20-working-day decision period. Because CASA-mandated operational records can include purpose, date/time, operating location, launch/landing coordinates, pilot/crew, flight mode and relevant flight segments, accountability requests need not depend solely on video. A carefully framed request could target operational releases, flight logs, tasking records, policies, evaluation material and aggregated reliability data, subject to applicable exemptions.
F-163 - GIPA ACCESS IS NOT AUTOMATIC
FACT/EVIDENCE. The GIPA Act creates a presumption and public interest in favour of disclosure, but permits withholding where listed public-interest harms outweigh disclosure, including prejudice to law enforcement, emergency preparedness, safety/security, facilitation of crime, personal information, fair-trial interests and a child's best interests. Therefore the existence of a record does not mean the public is entitled to an unredacted copy.
F-164 - NO POLAIR/RPAS ITEM APPEARS IN THE CURRENT 2026 NSW POLICE DISCLOSURE LOG LOCATED
FACT/EVIDENCE / NEGATIVE FINDING. The NSW Police 2026 disclosure log currently lists a small number of unrelated releases, including strip-search statistics, monetary-reward applications, operation expenditure and barrister fees. No PolAir-Remote, RPAS or Moree-drone release appears in that public log as inspected. This does not prove no GIPA application has ever sought such material; not every release necessarily becomes a disclosure-log item.
F-165 - GOVERNMENT DATA-SHARING LAW PROVIDES PATHWAYS, BUT NOT A BLANK CHEQUE
FACT/EVIDENCE. The Data Sharing (Government Sector) Act 2015 authorises defined inter-agency data sharing for government policy, program management and service planning/delivery, subject to safeguards. It does not authorise sharing of GIPA Schedule 2 excluded information or Schedule 1 information through that Act, and requires privacy, custody/control and State Records safeguards. Other Acts or legal pathways can separately permit or require sharing. Therefore the mere existence of a cross-government data-sharing statute does not prove PolAir imagery can be freely circulated.
F-166 - NSW PRIVACY LAW CONTAINS SPECIFIC LAW-ENFORCEMENT SECONDARY-USE / DISCLOSURE EXEMPTIONS
FACT/EVIDENCE. Section 23 of the Privacy and Personal Information Protection Act contains exemptions where secondary use is reasonably necessary for law-enforcement purposes and where disclosure is made for law-enforcement purposes or in specified circumstances. This substantially weakens any assumption that information collected for one police incident can never be used for a related law-enforcement purpose. The exact limits depend on the circumstances and other law.
F-167 - NO EVIDENCE HAS BEEN LOCATED THAT MOREE POLAIR LIVE FEEDS OR HISTORICAL IMAGERY ARE ROUTINELY SHARED WITH OTHER AGENCIES
UNKNOWN / NEGATIVE FINDING. Legal pathways for inter-agency information exchange exist, but no located public evidence establishes routine Moree PolAir imagery sharing with Youth Justice, councils, Transport for NSW, Corrective Services, EPA, Commonwealth bodies or other agencies. The investigation must distinguish 'legally possible in some circumstances' from 'actually occurring'.
F-168 - NSW CHILD-JUSTICE LAW EXPRESSLY REQUIRES CHILD-SPECIFIC PRINCIPLES
FACT/EVIDENCE. Section 6 of the Children (Criminal Proceedings) Act 1987 requires functions under the Act to be exercised having regard to children's equal rights and freedoms, their right to be heard and participate, dependency and immaturity, continuity of education/employment and home, reintegration and family/community ties, responsibility/reparation and victim impact. Current law also expressly addresses criminal responsibility for children aged 10-13. This confirms that children are not simply smaller adult subjects within the justice architecture.
F-169 - ANOTHER NSW JUSTICE AGENCY ALREADY HAS A CHILD-SPECIFIC SURVEILLANCE POLICY
FACT/EVIDENCE. Youth Justice NSW identifies an 'Audio Visual Surveillance and Footage Policy' governing specific surveillance hardware/software in Youth Justice Centres, although the policy itself is withheld from public access on GIPA public-interest grounds. The significance is institutional: NSW already treats youth surveillance as a subject capable of dedicated policy. No equivalent publicly located PolAir-Remote child-specific safeguard has been identified.
F-170 - INTERNATIONAL CHILD-JUSTICE STANDARDS REQUIRE HEIGHTENED PRIVACY OF CHILD RECORDS
FACT/EVIDENCE. UN Committee on the Rights of the Child General Comment No. 24 states that a child's privacy should be fully respected throughout child-justice proceedings and that child case files/records should be kept strictly confidential and closed to third parties except those directly involved in investigation, adjudication and disposition. This is an international interpretive standard, not a freestanding NSW cause of action, but it reinforces the need for child-specific controls over reusable surveillance records.
F-171 - CLOSING THE GAP PRIORITY REFORM FOUR CREATES A DATA-GOVERNANCE EXPECTATION RELEVANT TO POLAIR EVALUATION
FACT/EVIDENCE. Priority Reform Four commits governments to locally relevant, disaggregated data access; partnerships guiding collection, access, management and use; giving Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities access to the same data used for decisions, subject to privacy/security; and telling communities what data governments hold and how it can be accessed. If PolAir data are used to evaluate policing or justify expansion in Aboriginal communities, the policy architecture is directly relevant.
F-172 - INDIGENOUS DATA SOVEREIGNTY IS A GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLE HERE, NOT PROOF OF LEGAL OWNERSHIP OF POLICE RECORDS
LIMITATION. Closing the Gap supports shared access, governance and local decision-making; it does not by itself transfer legal ownership or operational control of NSW Police records to Aboriginal organisations. The unresolved question is whether Moree's Aboriginal-led governance structures receive meaningful, suitably deidentified PolAir deployment/outcome data for shared decision-making. No evidence located establishes that they do.
F-173 - CURRENT PUBLIC POLAIR PERFORMANCE METRICS ARE POLICE-OUTPUT HEAVY AND VICTIM-OUTCOME LIGHT
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. Public claims emphasise arrests, recovered vehicles, missing people, launch speed and cost. A victim-centred evaluation would additionally measure time to safety, repeat victimisation, injury severity/outcome, property loss avoided/recovered, time to return of property, search-and-rescue health outcome, victim confidence and whether rapid aerial information changes frontline protection decisions. No aggregate Moree victim-outcome evaluation has been located.
F-174 - THE MOREE DV CASE SHOWS A REAL VICTIM-PROTECTION USE CASE WITHOUT PROVING PREVENTION
FACT/EVIDENCE. In July 2026, police reported that a 34-year-old woman suffered serious facial injuries in an alleged domestic-violence assault. Days later, officers using PolAir-Remote assistance stopped a vehicle and arrested the wanted man allegedly hiding in it. This demonstrates tactical yield in locating/apprehending an alleged high-risk offender. It does not establish that the drone prevented the original assault or quantify subsequent victim-safety benefit.
F-175 - MORE LEGAL ACTION CAN REFLECT BETTER DETECTION / ENFORCEMENT RATHER THAN MORE UNDERLYING HARM
FACT/EVIDENCE. BOCSAR reported that 2025 domestic-violence court actions increased partly because police were more likely to charge matters that came to attention, even where some incident rates were stable. BOCSAR also notes that much violence is not reported to police and victimisation surveys are needed to estimate prevalence. This reinforces the investigation's earlier ascertainment-bias finding: police-output counts and underlying victimisation are not interchangeable.
F-176 - A CHILLING EFFECT FROM GOVERNMENT SURVEILLANCE IS A RECOGNISED HUMAN-RIGHTS MECHANISM
FACT/EVIDENCE. The Australian Human Rights Commission has described how the possibility of government surveillance and recording can cause people to adjust otherwise lawful behaviour, with potential effects on privacy, expression and association. This establishes the mechanism as credible. It does not establish that PolAir-Remote has caused such an effect in Moree.
F-177 - NO MOREE CHILLING-EFFECT MEASUREMENT HAS BEEN LOCATED
UNKNOWN / NEGATIVE FINDING. No public evaluation located measures whether residents avoid public spaces, gatherings, political activity, cultural activity, youth socialising or other lawful behaviour because of the drone program. Nor has evidence been located demonstrating that such behaviour change has occurred. The effect therefore remains plausible but unmeasured.
F-178 - PUBLIC ACCESS, DEFENCE DISCLOSURE, INTER-AGENCY SHARING AND COMMUNITY DATA ACCESS ARE DISTINCT CHANNELS
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. GIPA governs public access; criminal-procedure disclosure protects prosecution/defence fairness; government data-sharing statutes and law-enforcement provisions govern defined agency-to-agency transfers; Closing the Gap establishes a policy expectation for locally relevant Aboriginal community data access. A restriction in one channel does not necessarily extinguish duties or possibilities in another. Accountability analysis must therefore identify which channel is being invoked.
F-179 - DELETION GOVERNANCE MUST BE TESTED AGAINST BOTH RECORDS LAW AND LITIGATION / DISCLOSURE DUTIES
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. State Records law restricts unauthorised disposal/alteration of State records, while criminal-procedure law requires relevant investigative documents/things to be retained while disclosure duties continue. Therefore a generic operational retention rule cannot safely be treated as authority to delete material that has become an evidentiary record or is otherwise subject to a preservation obligation. The PolAir-specific retention schedule remains UNKNOWN.
F-180 - ACCOUNTABILITY DOES NOT REQUIRE PUBLIC RELEASE OF IDENTIFIABLE RAW SURVEILLANCE
INFERENCE. The competing interests can be separated. Public accountability can use deidentified/aggregated flight geography, mission purpose, proactive/reactive classification, reliability, demographic impact and outcome statistics, while raw identifiable footage and sensitive tactical details can remain restricted where justified. This weakens any false choice between 'publish everything' and 'publish nothing'.
Block-level conclusion
The data/rights branch shows that PolAir accountability is not a single privacy question. The system can generate State records, criminal evidence, operational aviation records and potentially reusable government information, each governed by different channels. Children and Aboriginal communities introduce additional policy and rights considerations, while victim outcomes require a separate measurement stream from police activity. No evidence currently establishes routine inter-agency PolAir imagery sharing or a measured chilling effect in Moree.

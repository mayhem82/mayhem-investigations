
MAYHEM – MNC POLAIR-REMOTE DEEP RESEARCH
SNAPSHOT 7
Delta snapshot • 16 August 2026 • Findings F-131–F-140
Exact predecessor: MAYHEM-MNC-POLAIR-REMOTE-DEEP-RESEARCH-SNAPSHOT-6-2026-08-16.docx
Scope: policy-feedback / technology-ratchet branch only. This snapshot does not restate earlier findings; it freezes new evidence and analysis developed after Snapshot 6.
Evidence classification
FACT/EVIDENCE = directly supported by located material. INFERENCE = analytical consequence drawn from established facts. UNKNOWN = material point not established by the public record located as at this snapshot.
F-131 — NOMINAL SIX-MONTH WINDOW AND AUGUST CONTINUATION
FACT/EVIDENCE. On 8 January 2026, Moree Online News reported from an NSW Police/community briefing that the trial would run for the next six months and that the trial 'came into effect today'. NSW Police-originated material then records PolAir-Remote assisting an arrest arising from an incident on 2 August 2026. If 8 January is treated as the operative commencement date, operational use continued beyond six calendar months. The program was also officially launched on 19 February, creating ambiguity over which date NSW Police treats as the formal trial clock. No public notice located explains an extension, transition period, or revised end date. Classification: continuation beyond the January six-month window VERIFIED; legal or procedural impropriety NOT ESTABLISHED.
F-132 — ENTERPRISE EXPANSION PLANNING PRECEDED CONSULTATION CLOSE
FACT/EVIDENCE. NSW Police published EOI YR-2026-0058 on 2 June 2026 for an enterprise-grade RPAS panel covering all regions. Its stated goal was to understand capability for future expansion and evolution, including autonomous/dock-based systems, VLOS/BVLOS, Remote Operations Centre models and specialist payloads. The Moree community consultation remained open until 12 June 2026. The EOI was non-binding market research, not an award. Classification: expansion capability planning before consultation closed VERIFIED; predetermined rollout decision NOT ESTABLISHED.
F-133 — RPAS EXPANSION SITS INSIDE A BROADER AVIATION-COMMAND REDESIGN
FACT/EVIDENCE. On 7 April 2026 NSW Police published EOI YR-2026-0012 seeking specialist aviation providers to inform a possible Aviation Command Operating Model Review, covering governance, regulatory compliance, workforce sustainability, training, fleet, engineering, operational efficiency and capacity. It expressly stated that it did not itself procure services. Classification: broader Aviation Command transformation planning VERIFIED.
F-134 — PUBLIC CONSULTATION WAS FRAMED TOWARD CAPABILITY DEVELOPMENT
FACT/EVIDENCE. The official Have Your Say page states that feedback was sought to inform 'future training, policy and capability development' in line with community expectations and police/CASA requirements. The public landing page does not state a binary continuation/termination test. The underlying survey questions were not available in the public page text inspected, so it cannot be claimed that respondents were denied a stop/no-rollout option. Classification: development-oriented public framing VERIFIED; exact survey decision architecture UNKNOWN.
F-135 — SCALING WAS CONTEMPLATED FROM THE START
FACT/EVIDENCE. At the 8 January briefing, Assistant Commissioner Andrew Holland said that if the trial succeeded it would be spread to other NSW locations and described the project as the next step in policing. At the 19 February official launch, Commissioner Mal Lanyon said the trial would determine whether the program should be scaled up. Classification: statewide scaling contemplated from inception VERIFIED.
F-136 — 'ASSESS FIRST' AND 'PREPARE TO SCALE' OCCURRED IN PARALLEL
FACT/EVIDENCE + INFERENCE. In early June, Police Minister Yasmin Catley publicly said the six-month Moree trial should run its course and that police would assess results before determining next steps. By then, the enterprise RPAS EOI had already been published on 2 June. These facts are not contradictory because the EOI was expressly capability research rather than a purchase decision. They do show institutional preparation for possible expansion occurring before the trial evaluation and public consultation were complete.
F-137 — NO RPAS PANEL AWARD IS ESTABLISHED
FACT/EVIDENCE. The RPAS EOI closed on 30 June and listed an estimated decision date of 20 July. As of this snapshot, the Buy NSW opportunity page shows no posted notices, and the located NSW Police contract search does not establish an RPAS panel contract award. An estimated decision date is not evidence of award. Classification: procurement award UNKNOWN / NOT LOCATED.
F-138 — FORMAL STOP, FAILURE AND DECOMMISSIONING CRITERIA ARE NOT PUBLICLY LOCATED
UNKNOWN / NEGATIVE FINDING. No public material located specifies a threshold at which Moree PolAir-Remote would be judged unsuccessful and stopped, nor public criteria for removing the docks, ceasing Remote Operations Centre tasking, decommissioning data integrations, or ending operational use. This does not prove such criteria do not exist internally. It establishes that they are not publicly auditable in the material located.
F-139 — INFRASTRUCTURE CREATES PATH DEPENDENCE
INFERENCE. PolAir-Remote is not merely two drones. It already uses a Bankstown Remote Operations Centre, specially trained Aviation Command personnel, Technology and Communication Services Command collaboration, dock infrastructure, live video workflows and integration into frontline and Soteria operations. Once those fixed capabilities exist, expanding to another dock location can be institutionally easier than recreating the system from zero. This creates path dependence, but does not itself prove expansion will occur.
F-140 — TECHNOLOGY-RATCHET RISK IS NOW EVIDENT
INFERENCE FROM VERIFIED ARCHITECTURE. The sequence is: trial capability installed → operational successes accumulated and publicised → community/political demand emerges in other towns → consultation is used to shape future policy/capability → enterprise market capability is scanned → broader Aviation Command operating model is reviewed → infrastructure remains operational beyond the January six-month window. No single step proves an irreversible rollout. Together they create a technology-ratchet risk: continuation and expansion can become progressively easier while explicit public exit criteria remain absent.
Critical evidentiary distinction
This branch does NOT establish that NSW Police predetermined the Moree trial outcome, unlawfully extended the trial, awarded a statewide RPAS contract, or committed to a Kempsey rollout. It establishes parallel preparation for scalable capability, continued operational use, and the absence of publicly located exit criteria.

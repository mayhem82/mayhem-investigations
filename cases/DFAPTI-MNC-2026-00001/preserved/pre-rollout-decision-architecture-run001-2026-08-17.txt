
MAYHEM — POLAIR-REMOTE
PRE-ROLLOUT DECISION ARCHITECTURE
RUN 001 • Moree → Kempsey → Mid North Coast • 17 August 2026
Status: OPEN DECISION GATE. This is not a final approval, rejection or enforcement instrument. It converts the completed Q1–Q763 Answer Register into explicit evidence gates for any proposed Kempsey or Mid North Coast expansion.
Control basis: MAYHEM-MNC-POLAIR-REMOTE-ANSWER-REGISTER-A-to-K-RUN-001-2026-08-16.docx; MAYHEM-MNC-POLAIR-REMOTE-ANSWER-REGISTER-L-to-T-RUN-001-2026-08-16.docx; MAYHEM-MNC-POLAIR-REMOTE-ANSWER-REGISTER-U-to-AF-RUN-001-2026-08-16.docx.
1. CUMULATIVE CONTROL STATE
The complete Question Bank is classified through Q763. RUN 001 records 38 RESOLVED questions, 125 PARTIAL questions, 469 OPEN - PRIMARY RECORD questions and 131 OPEN - ANALYTICAL questions.
The dominant constraint is not absence of investigation questions. It is absence of the PolAir-specific primary control records and linked mission/person/outcome/cost data needed to convert operational success stories into a defensible expansion decision.
Current high-value unresolved controls include the formal Moree evaluation and KPI framework, complete trial cost, controlling RPAS policy/SOPs, mission and outcome datasets, privacy/AI/cyber/vendor assessments, procurement outcome, formal stop/go instrument, and termination/decommissioning architecture.
2. DECISION RULE
No evidentiary transition may be compressed. The decision chain must be proved in order:
Moree tactical utility → Moree population-level effectiveness.
Moree effectiveness → Kempsey necessity and transferability.
Kempsey suitability → Mid North Coast network suitability.
Technical capability → lawful, auditable and proportionate operating architecture.
Lower aircraft cost → positive net system yield after ground-response, justice-system, privacy, safety and distributional effects.
A failure to prove a later transition does not erase an earlier verified benefit. Conversely, proof of tactical utility does not supply the missing causal, economic, governance or transferability evidence.
3. GATE STATES
PASS — Evidence is sufficient for the defined gate at the present investigation state.
PASS WITH CONDITIONS — Evidence supports proceeding only under explicit safeguards, monitoring and re-evaluation conditions.
HOLD — A material but remediable evidence, governance or analytical dependency remains unresolved.
NO-GO FOR APPROVAL NOW — The proposal is too undefined or too evidentially incomplete to approve at this stage. This is not a permanent rejection.
TERMINATE — Reserved for a later evidence state showing persistent negative/insufficient net yield, unmitigable safety/governance failure or unacceptable rights/distributional effects.
4. GATE 1 — MOREE EVALUATION INTEGRITY
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
Required before Moree results can justify expansion: the complete evaluation; original trial objectives; pre-deployment KPIs; success, failure, continuation, expansion and termination criteria; evaluator identity/independence; baseline and comparison design; included/excluded offence definitions; separate treatment of reactive and proactive operations; and explicit modelling of Soteria, bail changes, surge policing, Youth Action Meetings, diversion and place-based interventions.
Current evidence verifies that an evaluation was contemplated and that possible scaling was to be informed by the trial, but the complete evaluation and formal decision instrument remain unacquired.
5. GATE 2 — CLAIM REPRODUCIBILITY
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
The public expansion case must reproduce from primary records the meaning and source of the petition claims: approximately 50% crime reduction, 100+ arrests, 70+ stolen vehicles, six missing people, 30–60 second deployment and the 'fraction of helicopter cost' comparison.
Pass requires definitions, denominators and attributable contribution rules. 'Assisted', 'crime reduction', 'deployment time' and 'cost' cannot remain elastic promotional terms.
6. GATE 3 — TACTICAL UTILITY VERSUS POPULATION EFFECT
CURRENT STATE: PASS FOR TACTICAL UTILITY / HOLD FOR POPULATION EFFECT
Tactical utility is established through individual operations involving suspect location, vehicle interception, missing-person/search functions and victim-protection uses. Population-level crime reduction attributable to PolAir-Remote is not established.
Pass for population effect requires mission-level exposure linked to offence-level trends, matched comparison locations or another credible counterfactual, pre-existing trend controls, offence-category interaction tests, and explicit separation from other simultaneous interventions.
7. GATE 4 — KEMPSEY NECESSITY
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
Before a Kempsey deployment can be justified, NSW Police should define the exact capability deficit to be solved: incident types, annual frequency, location/time distribution, current ground response, current suspect-location delay, existing CCTV/MANPR/conventional PolAir contribution, and the proportion of incidents that a docked aircraft could materially improve.
A general statement that Kempsey experiences crime is insufficient. The test is marginal capability gain over existing policing and technology.
8. GATE 5 — MID NORTH COAST TRANSFERABILITY AND ENGINEERING
CURRENT STATE: NO-GO FOR NETWORK APPROVAL NOW
The phrase 'Mid North Coast' does not yet define an implementable system. Pass requires an engineering model specifying proposed geographic scope, dock count and sites, aircraft count, coverage radius, terrain/vegetation/coastal-weather effects, telecommunications and redundancy, BVLOS/airspace permissions, concurrent incident handling, recharge/maintenance/spares, fallback arrangements, operating hours and full capital/recurrent cost.
Moree metrics cannot be transferred unchanged to a materially different coastal, forested, mountainous and dispersed environment without local validation.
9. GATE 6 — OPERATIONAL AUTHORITY, PRIVACY AND PURPOSE CONTROL
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
Pass requires publication or independent verification of the controlling RPAS policy and the parts of SOPs necessary for meaningful audit: launch/tasking authority; reactive/proactive classification; covert-use rules; secondary detection and purpose-change controls; private-yard/window/thermal/zoom boundaries; tracking duration; mission termination authority; complaint handling; and breach logging.
CASA aviation compliance is necessary but does not answer why a person/place may be observed, what may be recorded, or how police purpose changes are authorised.
10. GATE 7 — RECORDING, EVIDENCE, RETENTION AND DISCLOSURE
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
The platform and aviation framework can generate detailed audit records. Pass requires a PolAir-specific evidence architecture defining video-recording triggers, source-file integrity, metadata, flight logs, operator identity, sensor state, software/firmware configuration, evidence export, chain of custody, defence disclosure, retention, deletion verification, litigation preservation and access logging.
A public statement that video is not ordinarily recorded cannot be treated as absence of an auditable flight record.
11. GATE 8 — AI, BIOMETRICS, DATA FLOW AND CYBERSECURITY
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
Pass requires an enabled/disabled capability register; identification of any onboard or central object/person detection and tracking; confirmation of whether custom/third-party analytics are present; a separate rule for biometric/facial matching; applicable NSW AI assurance; PIA; data-flow diagram; hosting location; manufacturer/vendor connectivity; encryption/authentication/MFA/privileged access; penetration/cyber assessment; supply-chain assessment; update approval and reassessment controls.
No evidence presently establishes active facial recognition in the Moree workflow. That negative finding must not be converted into a permanent technical prohibition unless the policy says so.
12. GATE 9 — SAFETY, RESILIENCE AND PHYSICAL RESPONSE
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
Pass requires reliability denominators: total missions, successful tasking, aborted/no-outcome missions, weather cancellations, network/control-link interruptions, return-to-home events, positioning anomalies, dock/aircraft unserviceability, emergency-airspace deconfliction, maintenance downtime and recovery time.
The operating model must also specify fallback to ground units, conventional PolAir or other assets when the remote aircraft is unavailable. Remote observation cannot substitute for physical intervention capacity.
13. GATE 10 — ABORIGINAL, CHILD AND DISTRIBUTIONAL IMPACT
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
Pass requires de-identified measurement of who is exposed to flights, tracking, stops, arrest, bail refusal, remand, detention and repeated drone-associated contact by geography, age and Aboriginality; comparison with local population and offending patterns; separate analysis of proactive versus reactive operations; and a documented response threshold for disproportionate effects.
The governance process should also identify the role of Moree and Kempsey Aboriginal community-controlled organisations, local governance/reference groups, Closing the Gap data-sharing expectations, and child-specific surveillance safeguards.
14. GATE 11 — ECONOMIC NET YIELD AND ALTERNATIVES
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
Pass requires full capital and recurrent cost, including aircraft/docks, ROC, operators, maintenance, communications, software/data, compliance, storage, training and ground response; plus downstream custody, court, Legal Aid, bail, supervision and detention effects where attributable.
Benefits must include displaced conventional PolAir/search time, officer-hours saved, dangerous pursuits avoided, property recovery, victim harm avoided, missing-person/search-and-rescue benefit and subsequent offences prevented.
The decision metric is net cost per prevented harm and marginal yield of the next dollar versus realistic alternatives: frontline staffing, prevention/diversion, bail support, community supervision, fixed/mobile CCTV, MANPR and conventional aviation.
15. GATE 12 — PROCUREMENT, RATCHET AND EXIT CONTROL
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
Pass requires the RPAS procurement outcome, contract values/durations, exit rights, data return/deletion clauses, capability-addition rules, supply-chain/cyber requirements, and the Aviation Command Operating Model Review findings.
It must also identify the person/body with authority to continue, expand, modify or terminate the program; whether evaluation is a mandatory precondition to expansion; and a real decommissioning path for docks, aircraft, software permissions, data and ROC capability.
The central control question remains whether the formal architecture retains a genuine ability to say no after infrastructure, training, workflows and political expectations have accumulated.
16. GATE 13 — CONSULTATION, OVERSIGHT AND PUBLIC AUDITABILITY
CURRENT STATE: HOLD
For a new Kempsey/Mid North Coast deployment, consultation should occur before material site/configuration choices are irreversible and should identify what the community can actually influence, including whether deployment occurs, proactive use, retention and analytics.
Pass also requires continuous de-identified reporting and an oversight path able to review systemic practice, not merely individual misconduct.
17. MINIMUM CONTINUOUS PUBLIC REPORTING
Mission totals and purpose: reactive, proactive, training/testing and other defined categories.
Launch time, arrival time and time to useful intelligence; ground-police arrival and intervention where measurable.
No-outcome, aborted, unavailable, failure and safety/reliability rates.
Direct tactical outcomes with explicit attribution definitions.
Crime/victim outcomes, displacement/diffusion and deterrence/desistance measures where analytically valid.
Geographic and demographic exposure, including Aboriginal and child justice-system effects.
Privacy complaints, substantiated incidents, secondary-purpose changes and access/audit events in de-identified form.
Complete capital/recurrent cost, conventional-PolAir displacement and net system-yield measures.
Major procurement, software, sensor, AI/analytics or operating-policy changes.
18. PROPOSED MAYHEM LIMITED-PILOT SAFEGUARD ARCHITECTURE
This section is a MAYHEM proposal, not a statement of current NSW Police policy. If decision-makers wish to test Kempsey before the above expansion evidence is complete, the least contaminating design would be a tightly bounded local pilot rather than a district-wide rollout.
Pre-specify the exact policing deficit and target incident classes before launch.
Fix the pilot duration and sunset date before deployment.
Publish the evaluation framework, baseline, comparison design and GO/HOLD/NO-GO thresholds before launch.
Use reactive incident-response as the default; any proactive patrol must have a separately published justification, authority and audit category.
No facial recognition or new biometric linkage unless separately approved, assessed and consulted on.
Publish recording, retention, private-property, secondary-use and purpose-change rules before launch.
Create a unique mission ID and preserve the aviation/tasking audit trail for every flight.
Publish monthly de-identified mission, outcome, failure, complaint, demographic and cost metrics.
Provide an independent evaluation route and local Aboriginal/shared-governance review.
Prevent automatic continuation: the pilot expires unless an identified decision-maker signs a recorded post-evaluation continuation decision.
19. MODIFICATION AND TERMINATION TRIGGERS
MAYHEM modification triggers should include:
Material KPI miss or absence of measurable marginal operational benefit.
Repeated reliability/safety failures or unacceptable fallback gaps.
Privacy, purpose-expansion or access-control failure.
Disproportionate geographic/demographic exposure not explained by operational need or not effectively mitigated.
Complaint patterns showing systemic policy or supervision failure.
Cost growth or net-yield deterioration relative to alternatives.
Material software, sensor, AI or data-flow change without reassessment.
MAYHEM termination should remain available where:
Net benefit is persistently negative or materially insufficient after full system costs and harms are counted.
A safety, legality, privacy, cyber or distributional failure cannot be adequately mitigated.
The capability does not solve a defined local problem better than realistic alternatives.
The formal decision architecture can no longer demonstrate a genuine, usable stop/exit mechanism.
20. CURRENT PROVISIONAL DISPOSITION
MOREE TACTICAL CAPABILITY: PASS as an established individual-incident capability. This does not resolve population-level effectiveness, full net yield or governance sufficiency.
MOREE AS EVIDENCE FOR KEMPSEY EXPANSION: HOLD. Current public Moree evidence is insufficient on its own to justify Kempsey deployment.
KEMPSEY LOCAL DEPLOYMENT: HOLD pending a local necessity/engineering assessment, full Moree evaluation, governance controls, cost/alternatives analysis and pre-specified decision thresholds.
MID NORTH COAST NETWORK APPROVAL: NO-GO FOR APPROVAL NOW. The network proposition is operationally undefined and lacks site, coverage, cost, transferability and governance architecture. This is not a permanent rejection.
STATEWIDE EXPANSION CLAIM: HOLD pending the formal Moree evaluation, procurement outcome and a demonstrably genuine stop/go architecture.
21. REQUIRED RECORDS THAT CAN CHANGE THE GATE
Complete Moree evaluation, business case, evaluation framework and original KPI/success/failure/expansion criteria.
Trial risk register; PIA; AI assessment or documented non-applicability decision; cyber and DJI/vendor risk assessments; data-flow and retention records.
Controlling RPAS policy, releasable SOPs, launch authority, proactive-patrol and mission-classification rules.
De-identified mission-level flight, outcome, failure, complaint/privacy and demographic data.
YR-2026-0058 assessment outcome, panel/contract records and Aviation Command Operating Model Review findings.
Complete trial expenditure, recurrent-cost projection and conventional-PolAir comparison methodology.
Consultation submissions, analysis, response record and Moree/Kempsey Aboriginal/shared-governance records.
Kempsey capability-needs and site/coverage engineering assessment; any Mid North Coast feasibility study.
Formal expansion criteria, stop/go decision instrument and termination/decommissioning plan.
22. CONTROL NOTE
This architecture does not silently reclassify any Q1–Q763 Answer Register item. Any future change to a gate must be tied to newly acquired evidence and recorded as an append-only reclassification of the affected question(s). The purpose is to preserve a real ability to distinguish operational usefulness from sufficient evidence for expansion.

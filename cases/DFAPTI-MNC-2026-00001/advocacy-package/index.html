All requests addressed to the Commissioner in Enforcement Notice Part Four: publishing the trial evaluation, the controlling operational policy, facial-recognition/biometric confirmation, and stop/decommissioning criteria. NSW Police operates PolAir-Remote and holds these records.
Minister for Police and Counter-terrorism
Ministerial policy responsibility for NSW Police, and the specific 3 June 2026 public commitment to assess the Moree trial before determining next steps - this notice asks that commitment be honoured in writing before any Mid North Coast decision.

How to Submit

To the Commissioner of Police
Submit via the NSW Police Community Portal (formal complaint) or by post to NSW Police Force Headquarters, 1 Charles Street, Parramatta NSW 2150 - verify this is still current before sending. State that you're submitting a formal complaint and information request, and ask for a written response within 28 days. Keep a copy and the date sent.
To the Minister for Police and Counter-terrorism
Write to GPO Box 5341, Sydney NSW 2001 - verify this is still current before sending, as ministerial portfolios and correspondence addresses can change. Reference the Minister's own 3 June 2026 public statement and ask for a written response within 28 days.
To the Law Enforcement Conduct Commission
Check the LECC's current website for its complaint/information-report form and postal address before sending - not independently confirmed while building this page. Frame this as a systemic-practice concern, not an allegation against a named officer.
To NSW Information and Privacy Commission
Send as a copy/FYI rather than a formal complaint at this stage - check the IPC NSW's current website for contact details before sending.
The six GIPA applications
Lodge via the NSW Police Community Portal or by post/hand to InfoLink (EV-0040 in DFAPTI's Evidence Register). Standard application fee is A$30; statutory decision period is ordinarily 20 working days. Each application is already narrowed by record class and date range to reduce the risk of an "unreasonable diversion of resources" refusal - don't broaden them when lodging.

A redaction citing a specific statutory exemption is more informative than no response at all, since it tells you where to focus a narrower follow-up request.

Five-Step Primary Source Verification

Every finding in this case traces to a specific evidence register entry. Here's how to check any one of them yourself, using no more than a browser. This matters more here than in most cases: the entire evidence base was produced by an external AI research process (see DFAPTI's Decision Register, DEC-0001), so none of it should be treated as confirmed until checked.

Step 1 — Find the reference
Every claim that matters carries an evidence reference like EV-0037. Note it down.
Step 2 — Look it up in the register
Open DFAPTI, go to Evidence Register, and find that ID (or open cases/DFAPTI-MNC-2026-00001/data/evidence_register.json directly). Read its source_title, source_location, and evidence_description.
Step 3 — Open the original
source_location is the URL where one was captured, or a reference to the preserved research corpus (cases/DFAPTI-MNC-2026-00001/preserved/) where it wasn't. Many entries in this case cite the preserved snapshot text rather than a live URL, because the external research pass this case was built from did not capture a pinpoint citation for every finding - that's a real limitation, not an oversight to paper over.
Step 4 — Check the claim against the source
Confirm what the evidence entry says the document says is actually what the document says. If it doesn't match, that's a finding in its own right - document it.
Step 5 — Record your check
Note the date you checked, the ID, and whether it held. If you're relying on this case for your own submission, this independent check strengthens it - and if you can locate a live URL for an entry that only cites the preserved corpus, that's a genuine upgrade worth recording back into the register.
Worked example. Enforcement Notice Part One states the Moree trial's total cost had not been finalised as of 30 July 2026, citing EV-0012. Step 2: EV-0012's source is a NSW Parliament Q&A (tracking id 108763). Step 3: search the NSW Parliament Q&A tracking system for that id. Step 4: confirm the answer states the trial total cost had not yet been finalised. Step 5: record what you found and the date - and if you can locate the direct URL, note it for a future correction to the register.

If a Document Has Moved, Never Existed Publicly, or Can't Be Found

  • Search the NSW Police, NSW Parliament, or Buy NSW site directly for the document title or procurement ID (e.g. YR-2026-0058).
  • Try the Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) for the original URL.
  • For a NSW Police record not covered by one of the six drafted GIPA applications, a fresh Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009 (NSW) request can be lodged directly - see the GIPA pack's own filing-strategy notes for how to keep a new request narrow.
  • For the complete 11 August 2026 Macleay Argus article specifically: this is a media/publisher record, not a government one - GIPA does not apply. Try the publisher directly, a library newspaper database, or an archive service.
  • An inability to access a document is itself worth recording - note the date, the URL or request reference, and what you found or didn't.

If There's No Response, or an Inadequate One

None of this is regulatory enforcement - it's real, legitimate pressure that has moved things before. Use it in order; skipping straight to the top doesn't usually work better.

1. Law Enforcement Conduct Commission — formal complaint
If the Commissioner or Minister haven't responded within 28 days, or the response doesn't actually answer the questions, submit or escalate a formal complaint to LECC, referencing this Enforcement Notice and the non-response. LECC has real statutory investigation powers over systemic NSW Police Force issues.
2. Your state MP
A state MP can raise the matter directly with the Minister for Police, or ask a question in Parliament, as a constituent-service channel distinct from a regulator. One honest complication specific to this case: if you live in the Oxley electorate (which includes Kempsey), your state MP is Michael Kemp - the same person who promoted the petition this case audits. That is not a reason to avoid contacting his office (he may still take up a constituent's request), but it means he is not an independent party to this specific matter the way a different electorate's MP would be. If you live outside Oxley, your own state MP has no such conflict and can raise it independently.
3. NSW Ombudsman — last resort
The Ombudsman expects other avenues (the Commissioner, then LECC) to be exhausted first, and has jurisdiction to examine NSW Police Force complaint-handling and administrative conduct. If LECC's response is itself inadequate, the Ombudsman can investigate and report publicly or to Parliament.
Log every one of these the same way as the original recipients (see Logging Responses). A constituency letter that produces a ministerial enquiry, or a LECC complaint that produces a systemic review, is a real outcome - worth recording as carefully as a direct reply would be.

Who Has Standing on What

General Manager, Kempsey Shire Council
The remedies required in Enforcement Notice Part Four: tenure confirmation within 30 days; a transfer request or documented refusal within 60 days of that confirmation; a published Complaints Handling Policy; a formal management instrument if transfer isn't immediately executable. Council holds the maintenance capability, the budget, and the capacity to request transfer - the Analysis Register's Self-Remediation Assessment names Council as the entity best placed to close the gap.
Michael Kemp MP, Member for Oxley
Ordinary constituent-service standing, distinct from this case's other investigation (DFAPTI-MNC-2026-00001), where Mr Kemp is himself the subject of a definitional-accuracy finding about his own petition. Here he is asked to act on a constituent's behalf, not examined - a formal briefing request to Council and Crown Lands, which carries more weight than a resident request alone.
NSW Crown Lands
Holds formal tenure authority and the power to initiate transfer without waiting on a Council request. The Enforcement Notice documents that the transfer-eligibility criterion in Crown Lands' own policy has been continuously met by Council's maintenance activity.
NSW Office of Local Government / NSW Ombudsman
Secondary recipients - see If There's No Response below. Both require documented prior Council contact as a precondition; the formal notice, once sent, creates that record.

How to Submit

To Kempsey Shire Council
Send the formal notice (preserved/advocacy-a-formal-notice-ksc-20260718.docx) to council@kempsey.nsw.gov.au or PO Box 3078, West Kempsey NSW 2440. State plainly that this notice sets out remedies required, not a request for information, and ask for a written response within 30 days. Keep a copy and the date sent.
To Michael Kemp MP
Send the MP letter (preserved/advocacy-b-mp-letter-20260718.docx) to oxley@parliament.nsw.gov.au. Reference the formal notice sent to Council on the same date.
The GIPA application
Send preserved/advocacy-c-gipa-application-20260718.docx to Council's GIPA Officer via council@kempsey.nsw.gov.au. Statutory decision period is 20 working days under s.57 of the Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009 (NSW). Consider lodging at the same time as the formal notice.
Crown Lands Current Road Status Search
Fee-based, at crownland.nsw.gov.au/find-services/conveyancing-status-and-other-searches/current-road-status. Demand 1 of the Enforcement Notice requires Council to obtain this - but if Council does not comply, this can be conducted independently.

Logging Responses

Log every response you get, in a separate document you keep yourself:

FieldWhat to record
Date received
FromBody and, if given, the person's name
FormEmail / letter / phone call / GIPA decision notice / no response by the requested date
ContentVerbatim quote or a faithful summary
ClassificationAcknowledged without action / referral elsewhere / partial release with redactions / full release / action committed / no response

A referral, a partial GIPA refusal citing an exemption, or silence past 30 days (20 working days for GIPA) is still worth recording - it's part of the record of how this was handled, not a dead end. If Council responds with a verbal deflection rather than a written statement, a follow-up email asking for the response in writing, referencing the original notice, is itself worth logging as a step.

Five-Step Primary Source Verification

Every finding in this case traces to a specific evidence register entry. Here's how to check any one of them yourself, using no more than a browser.

Step 1 — Find the reference
Every claim that matters carries an evidence reference like EV-0039. Note it down.
Step 2 — Look it up in the register
Open DFAPTI, go to Evidence Register, and find that ID (or open cases/DFAPTI-BB-LW-2026-00001/data/evidence_register.json directly). Read its source_title, source_location, and evidence_description.
Step 3 — Open the original
source_location is the URL where the extract was drawn from. Most entries in this case cite real, live government and legislation URLs - this case's tenure and policy findings are unusually well pinpoint-sourced compared to some others on this site.
Step 4 — Check the claim against the source
Confirm what the evidence entry says the document says is actually what the document says. If it doesn't match, that's a finding in its own right - document it.
Step 5 — Record your check
Note the date you checked, the ID, and whether it held. If you're relying on this case for your own submission, this independent check strengthens it.
Worked example. This repository already did one: EV-0039 through EV-0041 cite Crown Lands' Administration of Crown Roads Policy IND-O-250. Step 2: source_location is the actual policy PDF URL. Step 3: the PDF was fetched directly. Step 4: the exact quoted transfer-eligibility criterion and the exclusion of road-condition/financial-implications objections both appear verbatim in the real document. Step 5: recorded as EV-0039/0040/0041's classification upgrade to Verified, with the confirmation date (2026-08-23) and method noted in each entry's investigation_relevance field.

If a Document Has Moved, Never Existed Publicly, or Can't Be Found

  • Both the actual signed council minutes for the 20 July 2026 meeting and the Roads Act 1993 s.7/s.108 provisions were blocked to automated fetch during this case's ingestion (austlii.edu.au and legislation.nsw.gov.au both returned HTTP 403) - a manual browser visit may succeed where automated fetch didn't.
  • For the 1892 NSW Government Gazette notice: trove.nla.gov.au, search Newspapers & Gazettes, select the NSW Government Gazette title, filter to 1892, keyword Bellbrook. Free, but requires manual refinement - an initial broad search returned over 3,000 results.
  • For the NSW LRS Historical Parish Map, Parish of Uralgurra: hlrv.nswlrs.com.au, free public access, requires manual browser navigation.
  • An inability to access a document is itself worth recording - note the date, the URL or request reference, and what you found or didn't.

If There's No Response, or an Inadequate One

None of this is regulatory enforcement - it's real, legitimate pressure grounded in an existing, applicable transfer mechanism. Use it in order.

1. Follow up in writing
If Council responds verbally rather than in writing, ask explicitly for the response in writing, referencing the original notice and the specific demand it addresses.
2. NSW Office of Local Government
If Council does not provide a documented written response within 30 days, submit a complaint to OLG referencing the formal notice and the non-response date.
3. NSW Ombudsman
If Council's response is inadequate - acknowledges without addressing the specific demands - the Ombudsman can investigate maladministration by local councils, provided the documented prior Council contact (the formal notice) exists.
Log every one of these the same way as the original recipients (see Logging Responses). A response that acknowledges without acting is itself a structural finding worth recording, not a dead end.

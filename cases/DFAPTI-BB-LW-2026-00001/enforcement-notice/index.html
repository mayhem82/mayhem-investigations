Part One — What the Record Shows

Kempsey Shire Council maintains the Bellbrook laneway - documented in its own weekly works updates, bin collection schedule for adjacent Oreen Street, and Operational Plan capital works (EV-0002, EV-0042, EV-0048, EV-0049, EV-0050). Council also tells residents the laneway is Crown land and therefore not its responsibility. Both statements trace to Council's own material. Neither is in dispute.

What the public record does not show is any confirmation of the tenure classification the disclaimer rests on. No gazette notice, transfer instrument, or cadastral record confirming Crown road status has been located (EV-0023, EV-0038). The laneway is not selectable in the NSW Planning Portal (EV-0001, EV-0010) and does not appear in Council's own 2021 Crown Land Plan of Management (EV-0009).

Separately, Crown Lands' own Administration of Crown Roads Policy IND-O-250 makes a Crown road that a council is already maintaining eligible for transfer to that council - a criterion the laneway meets on Council's own maintenance record - and neither Council nor Crown Lands has ever requested or initiated that transfer (EV-0039, EV-0040, EV-0041, independently re-confirmed against the policy text 2026-08-23).

None of this is presented as concealment or wrongdoing. It is presented as a gap between what Council's own records document and what has been formally confirmed, resolved, or acted on for a parcel residents depend on for their only access - a gap only the recipients above can close.

Part Three — Basis for These Remedies

The Roads Act 1993 (NSW) s.152I and Crown Lands' Administration of Crown Roads Policy IND-O-250 v2.0 together provide a clear, existing mechanism for transferring a Crown road to a council already maintaining it - independently re-confirmed against the actual policy text (2026-08-23): a council or the department may request or initiate transfer, and road condition or financial implications are pre-emptively excluded as valid grounds for a council to decline (EV-0039, EV-0040, EV-0041). This notice does not ask for a new power to be created - it asks for an existing, applicable mechanism to be triggered.

The Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009 (NSW) creates a presumption in favour of disclosure of government information. A GIPA application already drafted in this case's Advocacy Package applies that Act directly to Council for the internal works-order and asset-classification records that would confirm or contradict the maintenance-without-obligation finding (AR-002) at the individual-record level.

The NSW Ombudsman's published complaint-handling standard requires councils to operate a three-tier escalation system (EV-0024); Council's public complaint infrastructure does not currently meet that standard on the evidence located (EV-0016, EV-0022). Demand 3 asks Council to close that specific, checkable gap.

Part Four — Remedies Required

Each item below is a specific action or binding commitment, not a request for information. Where a document is also needed to verify compliance, that document is named - but the remedy is the commitment itself, not the paperwork.

Demand 1 — Formal Tenure Confirmation
  • Obtain and disclose in writing a Current Road Status Search from NSW Crown Lands confirming the laneway's tenure classification, within 30 days. A verbal assertion of Crown land status is not an adequate response.
Demand 2 — Transfer Request or Documented Refusal
  • If tenure confirmation confirms Crown road status: within 60 days, either lodge a formal transfer request under Roads Act 1993 s.152I and IND-O-250, or produce a documented written statement of the specific legal basis for declining to do so - given that road condition and financial implications are pre-emptively excluded as valid grounds.
Demand 3 — Published Complaints Handling Policy
  • Publish a Complaints Handling Policy that discloses the Crown road exclusion, specifies an internal escalation process for jurisdictional deflections, and commits to written reasons for any deflection within 14 days.
Demand 4 — Formal Management Instrument or Maintenance Agreement
  • If transfer is not immediately executable, Council and Crown Lands must, within 90 days, execute a formal written maintenance agreement identifying the laneway, assigning documented maintenance responsibility to a named entity, and made publicly accessible.

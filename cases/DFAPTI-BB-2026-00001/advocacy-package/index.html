Five-step primary source verification

Every finding in this case traces to a specific evidence register entry. Here's how to check any one of them yourself, using no more than a browser.

Step 1 — Find the reference

Every claim that matters carries an evidence reference like EV-0006. Note it down.

Step 2 — Look it up in the register

Open DFAPTI, go to Evidence Register, and find that ID (or open data/evidence_register.json directly). Read its source_title, source_location, and evidence_description.

Step 3 — Open the original

source_location is the URL (or, for a locally preserved copy, preserved_file_reference points to the file in preserved/). Open it.

Step 4 — Check the claim against the source

Confirm what the evidence entry says the document says is actually what the document says. If it doesn't match, that's a finding in its own right — document it.

Step 5 — Record your check

Note the date you checked, the ID, and whether it held. If you're relying on this case for your own submission, this independent check strengthens it.

Worked example. Enforcement Notice Part One states Willawarrin's plant was targeted for "early 2026" completion, citing EV-0006. Step 2: EV-0006's source is an official NSW Government release, "Sod turns on new $8 million Willawarrin water treatment plant" (water.dcceew.nsw.gov.au). Step 3: open that URL. Step 4: confirm the release states an early-2026 completion target and that reservoir refurbishment follows commissioning. Step 5: record what you found and the date.

If a document has moved or disappeared

  • Search the NSW Government or Council site directly for the document title.
  • Try the Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) for the original URL.
  • For a Council record, a Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009 (NSW) request can be made directly to Kempsey Shire Council; councils must respond within 20 working days.
  • An inability to access a document is itself worth recording — note the date, the URL, and what you found or didn't.

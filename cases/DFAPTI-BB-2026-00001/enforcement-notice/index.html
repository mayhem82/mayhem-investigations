Part One — What the Record Shows

Willawarrin and Bellbrook are two small communities in Kempsey Shire supplied by standalone riverbank-bore water systems, treated locally rather than connected to the main Kempsey-Lower Macleay network. Both have a documented history of water being trucked in when local supply became unsuitable, most substantially across 2019-2020 (EV-0002) — an episode still being cited by Council's own communications as recently as December 2023 (EV-0024) as the reference point for community water-restriction questions.

Kempsey Shire Council is delivering an $8 million replacement treatment plant for Willawarrin (EV-0004, EV-0006), under construction since March 2025, with completion originally targeted for early 2026 (EV-0006, EV-0068) — a date that has now passed without a commissioning record appearing in the public evidence trail this investigation has been able to locate. Council's own 2020-21 annual report separately records that planning of a dedicated Bellbrook treatment plant augmentation was under way (EV-0071); no evidence of that project's current status, funding, or delivery timeline has been located since.

None of this is presented as concealment or wrongdoing. It is presented as a gap between what has been publicly documented (funding, construction milestones, planning intent) and what has not (commissioning, operational confirmation, Bellbrook's current delivery status) — a gap only the recipients above can close.

Part Two — What Remains Unconfirmed

The following specific questions are open in this investigation's evidence register (DFAPTI-BB-2026-00001) as of this draft.

Q-0001 — Has the Willawarrin plant been commissioned and is it currently operational, given the announced "early 2026" target has passed?
Q-0002 — What is Bellbrook's water treatment status today, independent of Willawarrin — has it received any dedicated upgrade since the 2020-21 augmentation planning noted at EV-0071?
Q-0003 — Why did Willawarrin's projected cost rise from approximately $4 million (August 2024) to $8 million (March 2025), and what scope changes accompanied the increase?
Q-0005 — What is the Steuart McIntyre Dam / Sherwood Borefield plant's current construction status and expected completion date?
Q-0006 — A minor but unresolved discrepancy: was the 2024 Kempsey Shire Council election held on 9 or 14 September 2024? Multiple secondary sources indicate 14 September; Council's own swearing-in report states 9 September. Included for completeness, not as a matter of public importance in itself.

Part Three — Basis for These Requests

Under the Public Health Act 2010 (NSW) and the Public Health Regulation, a drinking water supplier — which includes a local water utility such as Kempsey Shire Council — must have and comply with a drinking water management system, addressing the elements of the Framework for Management of Drinking Water Quality (the Australian Drinking Water Guidelines). Council's own Drinking Water Management System Annual Reports (EV-0031, EV-0062, EV-0071) are themselves evidence this obligation is taken seriously in principle; this request simply asks that the same reporting standard extend to the two open questions above.

Water treatment works of this kind are also subject to approval requirements under the Local Government Act 1993 (NSW). The NSW Safe and Secure Water Program (administered by NSW DCCEEW) is a co-funder of both projects named above, which is why it is included as a recipient rather than Council alone.

The Steuart McIntyre Dam project is co-funded three ways: $12.5 million from the Commonwealth's National Water Grid Fund, $13.2 million from the NSW Safe and Secure Water Program, and $27.1 million from Council (EV-0005/EV-0029). The National Water Grid Authority, not NSW DCCEEW, holds the Commonwealth funding relationship for that contribution, which is why it is included as a separate recipient.

The Office of Local Government NSW oversees council governance and service delivery under the same Local Government Act 1993, including a formal complaints and investigation function for serious breakdowns in council operations. It is copied here for the public record rather than asked to act in the first instance — see Advocacy Package for when and how to escalate to OLG directly if Council's response is inadequate.

Part Four — What This Asks For

Requested of Kempsey Shire Council
  • A current, dated operational status for the Willawarrin plant: construction status, testing outcomes, commissioning date if completed, and whether the community is currently receiving treated water from it or from the prior system.
  • A current, dated status for the Bellbrook treatment plant augmentation referenced at EV-0071: scope, funding, approval status, and expected timeline, or confirmation that the project has not progressed since 2020-21 and why.
  • A brief reconciliation of the $4 million to $8 million Willawarrin cost movement: what changed, and whether any further increase is currently expected.
  • Confirmation of the correct 2024 Kempsey Shire Council election date, for the public record.
Requested of NSW DCCEEW (Water Division)
  • The Steuart McIntyre Dam / Sherwood Borefield plant's current construction status and expected completion date.
  • Confirmation of the Safe and Secure Water Program's current funding commitment to both projects, and whether either has required a funding variation since the original announcement.

  • Confirmation of where the 2019-2020 crisis-year Drinking Water Management System annual report (the one covering the primary trucked-water episode this investigation concerns) can be accessed — it could not be located in this investigation's search of Council's public archive.
  • Confirmation of current iron and manganese monitoring results for the Willawarrin and Bellbrook supply systems, for public reference alongside the 2020-21 figures already published.

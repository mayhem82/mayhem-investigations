Part One — What the Record Shows

On 20 May 2025, Paul Hodgson, representing the Bellbrook Hall and Progress Association, formally presented a Flying-fox Camp Management Plan support request to Kempsey Shire Council. What was actually resolved remains disputed between this case's own sources - independently-fetched press coverage describes a discretionary decline on budget grounds with an informal hope to revisit in October; a later operator- supplied advocacy document describes a specific numbered resolution passed 9/0. Whichever account is accurate, no item on Bellbrook flying-fox management appears in the October, November or December 2025, or February 2026 council agendas (EV-0001, EV-0003, EV-0005, EV-0010, EV-0014).

Council's own most recent written account to residents (18 March 2026) describes ongoing correspondence, a 2025 community meeting, door-to-door visits, camp monitoring, and a stated intention to seek external grants - but discloses no commitment to producing a Camp Management Plan, no confirmed grant application, and no outcome (EV-0004). Council has completed exactly one such plan in its whole shire - Rudder Park, East Kempsey, in 2022 (EV-0002) - and two councils outside the shire, Townsville and Mount Isa, currently operate or are developing the same instrument, one of them funded specifically for the development step itself (EV-0020, EV-0021).

Separately, the operator's own direct registration attempt with the Commonwealth (EV-0022) drew a written reply confirming the National Flying Fox Monitoring Program has had no operating capacity since 2022 due to a cessation of federal funding, and that the Department cannot currently send resources to survey this or any newly-notified camp (EV-0023).

None of this is presented as concealment or wrongdoing. It is presented as a documented gap between a formally-raised community request, a governance instrument this same council already operates successfully elsewhere in its own shire, and a Commonwealth pathway confirmed non-functional since 2022 - a gap only the recipients above can close.

Part Three — Basis for These Remedies

The NSW Flying-Fox Camp Management Policy 2015 and Code of Practice 2018 give councils a documented framework for managing camps, including Level 1 and Level 2 actions available without individual licensing (EV-0012). This notice does not ask for a new power to be created - Kempsey Shire Council's own 2022 Rudder Park plan (EV-0002), and Townsville's and Mount Isa's current plans (EV-0020, EV-0021), demonstrate the existing mechanism working, including with dedicated development funding.

The Commonwealth's EPBC Act 1999 lists the grey-headed flying-fox as Vulnerable and retains a referral obligation for camps meeting the "nationally important camp" threshold, independent of the National Flying Fox Monitoring Program's own operating capacity (EV-0023).

The Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009 (NSW) creates a presumption in favour of disclosure of government information - relevant to Part Two's open questions regarding the 20 May 2025 minutes and Council's current webpage text (DR-001, DR-002 in the DFAPTA Analysis Register).

Five-Step Primary Source Verification

Every finding in this case traces to a specific evidence register entry. Here's how to check any one of them yourself, using no more than a browser.

Step 1 — Find the reference
Every claim that matters carries an evidence reference like EV-0011. Note it down.
Step 2 — Look it up in the register
Open DFAPTI, go to Evidence Register, and find that ID (or open cases/DFAPTI-BB-FF-2026-00001/data/evidence_register.json directly). Read its source_title, source_location, and evidence_description.
Step 3 — Open the original
Council website pages return HTTP 403 to automated fetch as of this case's runs - try a direct browser visit rather than a script or search-preview. Press and comparator-council items mostly cite real, live URLs directly.
Step 4 — Check the claim against the source
Confirm what the evidence entry says the document says is actually what the document says. If it doesn't match, that's a finding in its own right - document it.
Step 5 — Record your check
Note the date you checked, the ID, and whether it held. If you're relying on this case for your own submission, this independent check strengthens it.
Worked example. This repository already did one: while trying to verify the operator-relayed "historic" classification claim (EV-0016), a search surfaced Council's own 27 October 2021 page reporting the colony had left Bellbrook that year (EV-0013) - not something any operator-supplied document mentioned. Step 4: this didn't confirm or refute the classification claim - it surfaced a real, independent alternative explanation for it. Step 5: recorded as new evidence (EV-0013) and a new open question (Q-0008) rather than quietly resolving CTR-0002 in either direction. A verification check that complicates a claim instead of confirming or denying it is still a successful check - log it as one.

If a Document Has Moved, Never Existed Publicly, or Can't Be Found

  • Kempsey Shire Council's own website returned HTTP 403 to every automated fetch attempt across this case's runs. A manual browser visit, rather than a script, is untested and may succeed where the automated attempts didn't (TPA-007).
  • The June 2026 report describing severe Bellbrook camp impacts (EV-0015) has not been located despite an extensive search across regional media, ABC Mid North Coast, Mirage News, and NSW Parliament Hansard - if you have the actual citation, adding it would resolve one of this case's two open contradictions (Q-0009).
  • The signed confirmed minutes of the 20 May 2025 meeting are the single highest-value missing document in this case - a GIPA/formal records request to Council is the most reliable path if a direct site visit doesn't surface them (DR-001).
  • An inability to access a document is itself worth recording - note the date, the URL, and what you found or didn't.
